SITUATION OVERVIEW 
Cumulative figures continue to be reported on the earlier period of hostilities (28 February – 10 April). In 
May, authorities reported at least 383 children and 496 women among those killed (at least 3,375 
people), and over 33,000 injuries.17 Critical infrastructure has been heavily impacted, including 245 
health facilities18, more than 1,500 schools19,  about 400 water-related sites.20The Iranian Red Crescent 
Society (IRCS) reported damage to 149,528 civilian infrastructures including 123,647 residential and 
24,450 commercial units, 32 universities, 56 Red Crescent centres, 43 ambulances, 49 rescue vehicles, 
three operational vehicles, and three Red Crescent and emergency helicopters. The number of 15 
critical infrastructures, five fuel depots, 15 airports21, and 211 stadiums and sports facilities22 were 
targeted. Several power plants serving industrial units were damaged, requiring the compensation of the 
shortfall using other available capacities.23  Reports indicate that over 500 communication sites were 
targeted causing damage estimated at nearly US $335 million in the Information Technology (IT) 
sector.24 Further, Iran’s scientific and research infrastructure including knowledge-based companies 
sustained an estimated $330 millions in damage.25 A recent study found that airstrikes on 7 March 
released 29,800 tons of toxic sulfur dioxide over Tehran, comparable to several days of emissions from 
Hawaii’s volcano.26

Although a ceasefire is in place, humanitarian conditions continue to deteriorate due to inflation and 
economic disruption, with food inflation reaching up to 115 per cent, severely limiting household 
purchasing power and increasing vulnerability. 
With public order remaining largely stable since ceasefire, increasing numbers of people are likely 
returning to their places of habitual residence following the large movement in March. However, the 
humanitarian situation is driven by a combination of repercussions of temporary relocation, infrastructure 
damage, economic decline, high inflation and reduced household purchasing power across affected and 
vulnerable groups including refugees, women, children, and persons with disabilities. The most critical 
needs relate to access to health services, mental support, safe water, food, protection, shelter, cash 
assistance, and education.  
 
HUMANITARIAN IMPACT AND NEEDS 
Health 
The health system has sustained significant damage, with approximately 245 health facilities and 90 
pharmaceutical facilities damaged, alongside reported shortages of nearly 1,000 types of medicines and 
rising prices of some medicines. The most affected provinces in terms of healthcare facilities were 
Kermanshah, Isfahan, Ilam, Kordestan, Hormozgan, and Tehran. More than 6,000 locations and 2,000 
operational zones within Iran’s electricity industry were indirectly affected by airstrikes causing 
widespread damage across the country’s power infrastructure.
27 This has worsened electricity imbalance 
and caused more frequent power cuts affecting the health sector.28 
The health system remains under strain, even while emergency care capacity has been mobilized, 
including treatment for over 30,000 injured individuals, the inauguration of 160 health centres during the 
conflict, the provision of more than 700,000 vaccination services, and care and treatment for 20,000 
patients with HIV and 6,000 patients with tuberculosis.  
Additionally, mental health conditions, including anxiety, trauma, and post-traumatic stress disorder, 
have increased significantly among affected and vulnerable population. Urgent needs include the 
restoration of health service delivery through provision of medicines, vaccines, and cold chain 
equipment, as well as temporary facilities and mobile health services. Expanding access to health 
services particularly in underserved areas and scaling up mental health and psychosocial support 
(MHPSS) remain critical priorities. 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene 
Water infrastructure has been significantly impacted, with damage reported in 388 locations. Immediate 
needs include emergency repair of water systems, provision of safe water through trucking and storage, 
and distribution of water treatment supplies. Hygiene promotion, access to sanitation facilities, and

Food Security 
Food markets remain operational; however, affordability has sharply declined as the point-to-point 
inflation reached 73 per cent. Food and beverage inflation surpassed 112.5% nationwide with essential 
staples like cooking oils skyrocketing up to 375 per cent and meat costs jumping 191 per cent.  This has 
significantly reduced household purchasing power particularly among vulnerable groups and Afghan 
refugees. Priority needs include the provision of emergency food assistance and expansion of cash-
based transfers to enable households to meet basic needs. In parallel, support to restore livelihoods and 
stabilize household incomes is essential to mitigate further deterioration in food security. 
Protection 
Protection risks have increased across affected populations, with rising levels of psychosocial distress. 
Refugees, particularly Afghan nationals, face additional challenges related to documentation 
requirements required for basic services such as health insurance that is dependent on financial capacity 
and access to reliable information. Economic hardship combined with conflict-related stress is also 
contributing to increased levels of trauma and the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. Key 
priorities include scaling up targeted cash assistance to vulnerable groups, strengthening legal 
assistance and documentation support, and expanding community-based protection mechanisms. There 
is also a critical need to increase access to MHPSS services to address widespread trauma and mental 
health needs. 
Education 
The education sector has experienced extensive loss and disruption. It has been reported that a total of 
346 individuals from the education community, including 67 teachers and 279 students, lost their lives. In 
addition, more than 200 teachers and students were injured, highlighting the direct human toll of the 
conflict on the education sector. National authorities reported that about 1,500 educational facilities 
including 1,288 schools sustained damage between 20% and 100%, with 16 schools completely 
destroyed primarily located in Tehran, Markazi, and Hormozgan provinces.29 Immediate needs include 
rehabilitation and reconstruction of damaged schools, provision of learning materials and connectivity 
support, and establishment of alternative learning arrangements. Additionally, psychosocial support for 
students and teachers is essential to address the significant emotional impacts of the crisis. 
Shelter and Non-Food Items  
The destruction of homes and loss of essential household items further exacerbate vulnerabilities. Key 
needs include the provision of emergency shelter solutions, distribution of non-food items, and cash 
assistance to support rental costs and housing recovery. Tehran Municipality reported that nearly 40,000 
units required minor repairs in the capital, and have been almost completed.30 Authorities deployed

Emergency Livelihoods 
The conflict has had a severe impact on livelihoods, particularly due to disruptions in businesses, 
industries, and income-generating activities. Authorities reported that around 3,000 industrial facilities of 
various sizes suffered damage.32 An estimated total of 512 industrial units were directly or indirectly 
damaged only in Tehran Province.33 There is a need to strengthening liquidity and resilience to help 
businesses resume functioning and contribution to local economic stability. Livelihood recovery efforts 
are essential to restore economic activity and reduce long-term dependency on humanitarian aid namely 
among refugee and most vulnerable populations.  
 
RESPONSE 
Health 
Despite water and wastewater infrastructure damage and the emergence of epidemics in some parts of 
the country, the outbreaks were successfully controlled through daily monitoring by the Ministry of Health 
and Medical Education. While population movements were high in some provinces during the conflict, 
access to health services was maintained, and even in densely populated areas, access to 
communicable disease control services was ensured.34 National Health partners have provided 
emergency care to more than 40,000 patients, including over 1,300 surgical procedures, while continuing 
to support health service delivery nationwide. Efforts include the provision of vaccines, medical supplies, 
and backup power systems to sustain operations in affected facilities. Mental health services have also 
been expanded by national partners, including mobilizing a network of 700 psychologists during the 
conflict for provision of support to affected people, and mental health screening for 340,000 individuals 
nationwide. The World Health Organisation (WHO) provided training for approximately 250 healthcare 
workers in 29 provinces to deliver MHPSS services. WHO is procuring power generators and UPS 
systems for target hospitals and strengthening outbreak control and sustains essential medical and 
diagnostic supplies and laboratory equipment. The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) is 
procuring emergency backup generators to provincial cold chain warehouses, hospitals, and primary 
health facilities to safeguard vaccine and medicine cold chain functionality and continuity of essential 
services. 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene 
Repair and reconstruction operations of the damaged water infrastructure have been implemented by 
local organizations. The United Nations Agency for Children (UNICEF) supported water quality 
monitoring and safe water storage and purification from its prepositioned Water, Sanitation and Hygiene 
(WASH) emergency stocks. The distribution of 4,300 hygiene kits of different kinds through a national 
NGO was completed in three affected provinces. In parallel, additional support in safe water provision,

 Food Security 
National organizations provided 188,000 malnourished children with free monthly food basket credits

The World Food Programme (WFP) is procuring food packages in collaboration with the Iranian Red 
Crescent Society and has provided assistance to 34,818 refugees through food and cash support. 
Current interventions include daily bread through WFP-supported bakeries and distribution of wheat 
flour, emergency food packages, school feeding items, and cash-based transfers. 
Protection 
National partners provided support to more than 40,000 individuals living directly in conflict-affected 
areas through cash and in-kind assistance such as livelihood packages, medical expenses, housing 
repair, and relief packages.36 About 1,200 vulnerable and high-risk elderly individuals living alone were 
identified and received necessary services.37 The International Organisation for Migration (IOM) provided 
cash assistance to 410 households, reaching 1,987 individuals (including multipurpose cash, cash for 
protection, and cash for health), targeting vulnerable migrants and host community members. UNICEF in 
collaboration with local partners conducted a one-day technical booster session on the Adolescent 
Wellbeing Kit for 14 facilitators following a three-day training workshop. UNICEF further supported 
MHPSS for general population in Minab city (Hormozgan Province), where 50 mental health 
professionals were trained along with technical sessions for a group of supervisors. The Norwegian 
Refugee Council (NRC) is providing a comprehensive package of support addressing protection needs 
and associated risks. NRC supported cash and non-cash assistance (such as wheelchairs) for 
individuals at risk of deprivation. 
Education 
Education partners are supporting continuity of learning through online local platforms, offline learning 
and educational packages, and television-based education, and have initiated rehabilitation activities, 
with above 1,000 schools already repaired and prepared for use. NRC secured funding to support light to 
medium rehabilitation of classrooms damaged by blast waves with preparatory activities ongoing 
including the identification of target schools. NRC is also supporting the provision of offline learning 
materials, lesson plans, and study guides. The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural 
Organisation (UNESCO) secured fund from its headquarters to support the provision of psychosocial 
assistance to children affected by the recent escalation, in collaboration with national partners.

Early Recovery 
National partners reported that 1,230 urban service teams, supported by over 2,030 machines, were 
deployed for debris removal and road clearance following the destructions caused by strikes.38 UNDP 
procured wheel loaders, excavators and the low-bed trailers and delivered to local partners. 
Procurement of the medical evacuation kits and more equipment is ongoing. Early recovery interventions 
are underway, focusing on debris clearance, restoration of access to markets and services, and 
livelihood recovery. Efforts also include support to small businesses and assessments to inform recovery 
programming. However, progress remains constrained by economic instability, infrastructure damage, 
and ongoing connectivity disruptions. UNESCO is working on a pipeline proposal to support the recovery 
and restoration of conflict-affected cultural heritage sites and is mobilizing heritage emergency funding 
mechanisms to strengthen preparedness and response capacities across the broader museums and 
cultural heritage sector, with the objective of enhancing systemic resilience and improving readiness for 
future crises.
