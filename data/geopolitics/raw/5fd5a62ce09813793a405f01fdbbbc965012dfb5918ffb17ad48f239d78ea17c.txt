Key Findings


• Nearly all assessed items were reported as having either no availability or low availability across all governorates in the Gaza Strip. The only exceptions were regular adhesive duct tape (non-construction quality) and re-used metal rebar, which were reported as widely available in Khan Yunis only.


• Construction materials were largely reported as unavailable, reflecting the continued constraints on recovery and rehabilitation activities. Key construction materials, including cement, metal rebars, and corrugated galvanized iron (CGI) sheets, were widely reported as having limited or no availability across assessed markets.


• The overall availability of shelter items declined compared with the February 2026 assessment3. Since the previous round, the availability of several key shelter items has deteriorated, reflecting continued instability in shelter-related markets. This decline is driven by ongoing restrictions on the import of essential materials, resulting in a growing number of shelter items being reported as completely unavailable.


• Market activity in June remained low across the Gaza Strip. Most vendors reported low to very low levels of activity, with a higher proportion indicating reduced market activity compared with the February 2026 round. Limited market functionality was attributed to supply shortages, high transportation costs, customs fees, reliance on a single crossing for commercial and humanitarian inflows, and elevated supplier prices.


• Commercial suppliers remained the primary source of shelter, household, and tools/site-level items, underscoring the continued role of formal market actors in supplying goods across the Gaza Strip. Individuals selling their own goods were reported as the second most common source of commodities, highlighting the role of secondary markets in complementing limited commercial supply. This pattern may reflect increased circulation of used goods and could indicate broader economic pressures affecting households, including potential reliance on asset sales as a coping strategy.
