Highlights 
The Gaza Strip  
• The latest WASH Cluster monitoring, conducted in June, indicate a continued deterioration in wa-
ter security, with 84 per cent of assessed households experiencing moderate-to-high water inse-
curity. Access to water for domestic use declined between May and June, and drinking water was 
identified as the most pressing humanitarian concern, reported by 62 per cent of households. 
• Sanitation and hygiene conditions also remain concerning. Eighty-six percent of households re-
ported barriers to accessing toilets and latrines, due to lack of water, privacy concerns, and poor 
cleanliness, among others. Environmental health risks persist, with 52 per cent of households re-
porting sewage or sludge near shelters and 43 per cent reporting nearby solid waste accumula-
tion.  More than half of households reported lacking hygiene items, while health concerns remain 
widespread, including skin diseases or scabies (64 per cent), bedbugs (31 per cent), and lice (22 
per cent).

1. Key points 
The Gaza Strip 
Fatalities and Injuries  
• Between 7 October 2023 and 29 June 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by 
the Health Cluster, 73,066 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 173,514 injured. 
This includes 1,053 fatalities and 3,406 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agree-
ment on 10 October 2025 and 30 June 2026.  
• UNRWA has recorded 392 colleagues killed in Gaza since the start of the war (310 UNRWA per-
sonnel, and 82 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2]), up to 7 July.  
 
Health crisis 
• With the support of WHO, insulin syringes entered the Gaza Strip over the reporting period, ad-
dressing a critical shortage affecting all major health partners there. These supplies are critical to 
avoid treatment disruptions and to safeguard thousands of patients from serious complications 
and life-threatening risks. 
 
Displacement, site management 
• UNRWA continues to monitor the movement of displaced persons and displacement sites. A total 
of 116 UNRWA collective emergency shelters host 76,000 internally displaced persons and are 
currently considered active. 
 
Operational implications and humanitarian response 
• Around 11,000 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to peo-
ple in Gaza. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with over 4,000 of the 
Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, health, and other services to Palestine Refu-
gees. 
• All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the 
Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws 
passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024[3] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s opera-
tions in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and 
seek to bar any contact by Israeli officials with UNRWA. The Israeli authorities have not granted 
the Agency’s international staff visas or permits to enter the Occupied Palestinian Territory, in-
cluding Gaza, since the end of January 2025.   
• Since March 2025, the Israeli authorities have been blocking UNRWA from directly bringing hu-
manitarian personnel and aid into the Gaza Strip. Despite the huge needs on the ground, UNRWA 
was therefore unable to distribute the aid it had pre-positioned outside Gaza, which included 
enough food parcels, flour, and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people.   
 
The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem 
• According to OCHA, between 7 October 2023 and 29 June 2026, 1,109 Palestinians – at least 243 
of them children – were killed in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Sixty-three of 
them have been killed since the beginning of 2026.

• A Palestinian baby died after reportedly being delayed passage by Israeli forces through the

UNRWA response  
Health and nutrition    
• UNRWA has provided over 18.9 million health visits, including 12.9 million medical consultations 
since the start of the war in the Gaza Strip in October 2023.   
• From 16 to 30 June, UNRWA screened 4,958 children aged between six and 59 months at its 
health centres and medical points, identifying 203 malnourished children.  
• By 5 July, six health centres and four additional UNRWA-rented facilities used as temporary health 
centres were operational in the Gaza Strip. In addition, by that date, UNRWA was providing pri-
mary health services through 61 mobile medical teams working in 28 medical points inside and 
outside collective emergency shelters in the Middle Area, Khan Younis, Al Mawasi and Gaza 
City[5].  
• From 29 June to 5 July, an average of 988 UNRWA health personnel per day worked in the 
Agency’s health facilities across the Gaza Strip, providing 70,914 medical consultations in total.  
• Between 29 June and 5 July, UNRWA health teams provided 7,326 consultations for post-natal 
and pregnant women at high risk, 3,393 dental and oral health consultations in fixed and mobile 
clinics, and 2,463 physiotherapy rehabilitation services sessions in health centres and medical 
points. A total of 7,841 laboratory tests were conducted in UNRWA health centres and medical 
points.  
• From 29 June to 5 July, 2,404 vaccination sessions were provided at health centres and medical 
points. In addition, a total of 1,941 students received routine immunisations through school 
health services.  
• UNRWA’s telemedicine initiative began in March 2024 and has gradually expanded to include 
other services such as teledentistry, telephysiotherapy, and telepsychosocial counselling. Cur-
rently, following the establishment of the Centralised Telemedicine Centre, the team provides tele-
consultations for curative outpatient care, non-communicable diseases, as well as maternal and 
cardiological consultations. 
 
 
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)  
• UNRWA remains one of the largest providers of emergency learning and psychosocial support 
(PSS) services across the Gaza Strip.   
• Between 7 October 2023 and 6 July 2026, UNRWA’s social work teams provided services to 
279,130 displaced persons, including psychological first aid, PSS services, family and individual 
activities, and case management. During the same reporting period, services were provided to 
4,830 survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and 7,403 children, including 3,400 unaccompa-
nied children. The teams supported 37,652 persons with disabilities with PSS. Awareness ses-
sions on GBV , child protection, disability inclusion, as well as managing social and psychological 
stressors, were conducted for 160,140 displaced persons.

• UNRWA health teams, including psychiatrists, psychosocial counsellors and supervisors, contin-
ued to provide MHPSS services in Gaza City, the Middle and Khan Younis areas, assisting special 
cases referred from UNRWA health centres and collective emergency shelters. From 29 June to 5 
July, UNRWA teams conducted 2,609 visits in health centres and at health points providing indi-
vidual consultations, awareness sessions, and support for protection cases. 
 
Learning  
• UNRWA provides learning services in the Gaza Strip in Temporary Learning Spaces and through 
its distance learning initiative. The final exams for all students (Grades 1-9) are taking place from 
27 June to 12 July.  
• Between 29 June to 5 July, 42,088 children (17,997 boys and 24,091 girls), including 928 children 
with disabilities, took part in learning activities conducted across 676 classrooms in 78 UNRWA 
collective emergency shelters.   
• UNRWA leverages digital tools to provide basic literacy and numeracy activities for over 280,000 
children in the Gaza Strip, facilitated by approximately 7,500 teachers using self-learning material 
developed by UNRWA that is hosted on the Agency’s digital learning platform.   
 
Food security   
• From 7 October 2023 until the start of the previous ceasefire on 19 January 2025, UNRWA 
reached over 388,000 families, or nearly 1.9 million people, with two rounds of flour deliveries; at 
least 374,000 of those families received three rounds. During that ceasefire, which lasted until 
March 2025, UNRWA reached over two million people with critical food assistance. 
• UNRWA ran out of food at the end of April 2025. The Agency has been blocked by the Israeli au-
thorities from directly bringing in any humanitarian assistance including food since 2 March 
2025. 
 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) 
• Since October 2023, UNRWA has carried out emergency WASH activities across the Gaza Strip, at 
UNRWA collective emergency shelters and informal displacement areas. Activities include operat-
ing and maintaining water wells and desalination systems and supplying water by truck. In addi-
tion, UNRWA continues to maintain hygiene in its shelters and sites by distributing cleaning sup-
plies, running community-based solid waste management, water tank disinfection campaigns, 
and pest control.   
• In 2025, UNRWA distributed 970 million litres of drinking and domestic use water and collected 
and disposed of 70,000 tonnes of solid waste. UNRWA estimates that around 1.7 million people 
benefitted from the Agency’s water provision and solid waste collection services in 2025.   
• Between 16 and 30 June, UNRWA distributed around 79 million litres of domestic and drinking 
water in various areas and from different sources, reaching over 860,000 displaced people daily.   
• Between 16 and 30 June, UNRWA teams collected around 6,000 tonnes of solid waste inside the 
Agency’s emergency shelters and surrounding areas. More than 1,250 cleaning campaigns were 
conducted, benefitting over 1.4 million displaced persons. Activities included cleaning emergency 
shelters, cleaning and clearing 500 manholes, as well as water desludging. UNRWA teams also 
conducted over 2,500 hygiene promotion sessions over the same period.
