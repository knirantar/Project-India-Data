Highlights 
The Gaza Strip  
• UNRWA continues to participate in the pest -control campaign led by the WASH Cluster, aiming to 
prevent the spread of diseases and improve living conditions for displaced communities amid se-
vere overcrowding and deteriorating public health conditions. 
• UNRWA is conducting cleaning campaigns , community awareness activities, and targeted pesti-
cide use by trained UNRWA personnel, alongside solid waste disposal, and other activities to im-
prove environmental conditions. In total, 264 campaigns to control the spread of disease-carrying 
animals and pests have already been completed across 371 displacement sites since 7 June, 
reaching an area where almost 684,000 people reside. UNRWA is a main contributor to the cam-
paign, taking part (directly or together with partners) in 335 out of the 352 planned campaigns.

1. Key points 
The Gaza Strip 
Fatalities and injuries  
• Between 7 October 2023 and 21 June 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by 
Health Cluster, 73,035 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 173,368 injured. This 
includes 1,005 fatalities and 3,157 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement 
on 10 October 2025 and 17 June 2026.  
• UNRWA has recorded 392 colleagues killed in Gaza since the start of the war (310 UNRWA person-
nel, and 82 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2]), up to 23 June.  
 
Health crisis 
 
• The reported cases of Acute Watery Diarrhoea continued to decrease during the reporting week. 
Trends will continue to be closely monitored over the coming weeks. 
• Reported cases of ectoparasitic skin diseases, such as scabies, and other communicable skin dis-
eases also declined compared with the previous week, likely reflecting the impact of the large-scale 
pesticide spraying campaigns conducted by UNRWA and partners across camps and displacement 
sites.  
 
Displacement, site management 
• UNRWA continues to monitor the movement of displaced persons and displacement sites. A total 
of 116 UNRWA collective emergency shelters host 76,000 internally displaced persons and are cur-
rently considered active [3]. 
• According to the Gaza Displacement Site Verification Assessment,  forty-two per cent of sites re-
ported the presence of unaccompanied and separated children , with an estimated total of 7,300 
children. UNRWA collective shelters reported the highest proportion, accounting for more than 3 
per cent of the people living in them , compared to 0.3 per cent at both makeshift sites and non -
UNRWA collective shelters. This difference likely reflects both the demographic composition of 
UNRWA facilities and the stronger protection, registration, and reporting systems operating within 
them.  
 
Operational implications and humanitarian response 
• Around 11,000 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to peo-
ple in Gaza. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with over 4,000 of the 
Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, health, and other services to Palestine Refu-
gees. 
• All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the 
Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws 
passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024 [4] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s operations 
in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and seek 
to bar any contact by Israeli officials with UNRWA. The Israeli authorities have not granted the

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem 
• According to OCHA, between 7 October 2023 and 21 June 2026, 1,105 Palestinians – at least 242 
of them children – were killed in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. 59 of them 
have been killed since the beginning of 2026. 
• Early on 17 June, two mosques in villages north of Ramallah were subject to arson attacks and 
graffiti, purportedly by Israeli settlers. Settler violence continues unabated, with attacks and har-
assment occurring across the West Bank during this period.  
• Late on 21 June, two Palestinians including a child were shot and killed by Israeli forces in Beit 
Ummar, in the vicinity of the Karmi Tzur settlement. Two additional Palestinians were injured dur-
ing a subsequent search operation.

Humanitarian access and armed conflict-related incidents [5] 
• From 16 to 22 June, no significant armed incident impacted UNRWA facilities, personnel and per-
sons supporting UNRWA’s activities. 
• Since the beginning of the war and up to 18 May, 975* incidents impacting UNRWA premises and 
the people inside them have been reported. 312* (or nearly all) UNRWA installations have been 
impacted by armed conflict-related incidents, some of them multiple times. UNRWA continues to 
verify and update the number of casualties caused by these incidents.  
   
*Since the war began in October 2023, the latest casualty figures are continuously under review as UNRWA 
gains access to locations that were previously inaccessible and as further verifications occur. The summary 
figures will be published/updated as information becomes available, noting that these numbers are subject to 
change once verifications are concluded. 
   
UNRWA response  
Health and nutrition    
• UNRWA has provided over 18. 7 million health visits, including 12.7 million medical consultations 
since the start of the war in the Gaza Strip in October 2023.  
• From 1 to 15 June, UNRWA screened 5,302 children aged between six and 59 months at its health 
centres and medical points, identifying 261 malnourished children. 
• By 14 June, six health centres and four additional UNRWA -rented facilities used as temporary 
health centres were operational in the Gaza Strip. In addition, by that date, UNRWA was providing 
primary health services through 69 mobile medical teams working in 28 medical points inside and 
outside collective emergency shelters in the Middle Area, Khan Younis, Al Mawasi and Gaza City [6]. 
• From 15 to 21 June, an average of 1,043 UNRWA health personnel per day worked in the Agency’s 
health facilities across the Gaza Strip, providing 65,129 medical consultations in total. 
• Between 15 and 21 June , UNRWA health teams provided 7,554 consultations for post -natal and 
pregnant women at high risk, 3,103 dental and oral health consultations in fixed and mobile clinics, 
and 2,398 physiotherapy rehabilitation services sessions in health centres and medical  points. A 
total of 9,822 laboratory tests were conducted in UNRWA health centres and medical points. 
• From 15 to 21 June, 2,371 vaccination sessions were provided at health centres and medical 
points. In addition, a total of 1,176 students received routine immunisations through school health 
services. 
• UNRWA’s telemedicine initiative began in March 2024 and has gradually expanded to include other 
services such as teledentistry, tele-physiotherapy, and telepsychosocial counselling. Currently, fol-
lowing the establishment of the Centralised Telemedicine Centre, the team provides teleconsulta-
tions for curative outpatient care, non-communicable diseases, as well as maternal and cardiolog-
ical consultations.

(PSS) services across the Gaza Strip.

Learning  
• UNRWA provides learning services in the Gaza Strip in Temporary Learning Spaces and through its 
distance learning initiative.  
• Between 15 to 21 June, 52,150 children (22,058 boys and 30,092 girls), including 971 children with 
disabilities, took part in learning activities conducted across 687 classrooms in 78 UNRWA collec-
tive emergency shelters[7].  
• UNRWA also leverages digital tools to provide basic literacy and numeracy activities for over 
280,000 children in the Gaza Strip, facilitated by approximately 7,500 teachers using self -learning 
material developed by UNRWA that is hosted on the Agency’s digital learning platform.  
 
Food security   
• From 7 October 2023 until the start of the previous ceasefire on 19 January 2025, UNRWA reached 
over 388,000 families, or nearly 1.9 million people, with two rounds of flour deliveries; at least 
374,000 of those families received three rounds. During that ceasefire, which lasted until March 
2025, UNRWA reached over two million people with critical food assistance.  
• UNRWA ran out of food at the end of April 2025. The Agency has been blocked by the Israeli 
authorities from directly bringing in any humanitarian assistance including food since 2 March 
2025.    
 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) 
• Since October 2023, UNRWA has carried out emergency WASH activities across the Gaza Strip, at 
UNRWA collective emergency shelters and informal displacement areas. Activities include operat-
ing and maintaining water wells and desalination systems and supplying water by truck. In addition, 
UNRWA continues to maintain hygiene in its shelters and sites by distributing  cleaning supplies, 
running community-based solid waste management, water tank disinfection campaigns, and pest 
control.  
• In 2025, UNRWA distributed 970,000 cubic metres of drinking and domestic use water and collected 
and disposed of 70,000 tonnes of solid waste. UNRWA estimates that around 1.7 million people 
benefitted from the Agency’s water provision and solid waste collection services in 2025.  
• Between 1 and 15 June, UNRWA distributed around 70,000 cubic metres of domestic and potable 
water in various areas and from different sources, reaching over 860,000 displaced people daily.  
• Between 1 and 15 June, UNRWA teams collected around 4,5 00 tonnes of solid waste inside the 
Agency’s emergency shelters and surrounding areas. More than 850 cleaning campaigns were con-
ducted, benefitting over 1,700,000 displaced persons. Activities included cleaning emergency shel-
ters, cleaning and clearing 600 manholes, as well as water desludging. UNRWA teams also con-
ducted over 2,500 hygiene promotion sessions over the same period.
