2025 was one of the most severe years on record for humanitarian WASH needs. Protracted conflict, climate shocks and surging displacement combined to push over 300 million people into humanitarian need globally. The Sudan crisis, the world's largest displacement emergency, peaked at 11.5 million internally displaced persons in January 2025, while Gaza saw near-total collapse of water and sanitation infrastructure for more than 2.1 million Palestinians. In the Democratic Republic of Congo, cholera deaths rose by 361 per cent compared to 2024 (declared the worst outbreak in 25 years) driven by critically low WASH access and compounded by conflict and displacement. South Sudan, Yemen, Afghanistan, Haiti and Myanmar each recorded deepening WASH crises, with humanitarian response plans collectively and severely underfunded against documented needs.


The humanitarian financing landscape worsened with an overall decline in overseas development assistance of 9– 17% in 2025, with cuts of 16–28% for sub-Saharan Africa, the region most dependent on WASH investment. Even before the United States Government withdrawal, humanitarian WASH was only 30 per cent funded against global documented needs. The structural dependence of the WASH sector on a small number of bilateral donors has accelerated calls for diversification into pooled funds, green finance mechanisms, and private sector partnerships.
