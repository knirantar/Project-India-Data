Executive Summary


Syria’s crisis, now in its fifteenth year, continues to represent one of the most severe displacement and protection emergencies globally. In 2025, an estimated 16.5 million people inside the country required humanitarian assistance, including more than 6.4 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), while a further 3.9 million Syrians remain in neighboring countries. Protracted conflict, economic collapse, weakened rule of law, and repeated shocks have significantly increased exposure to protection risks, particularly for women and children. The political transition following renewed conflict in late 2024 has further exacerbated instability, disrupted services, and strained already limited protection systems.


Through the Protection Analytical Framework (PAF) process conducted by Bahar in partnership with Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) and in close coordination with the Protection Cluster from the site selection phase through to the completion of the assessment, in Afrin district (Aleppo Governorate) and Sarmada sub-district (Idleb Governorate), five protection risks were identified as the most prevalent and severe, requiring immediate and prioritized attention.


Child, early or forced marriage:
 Emerged as the most critical protection concern. Findings indicate that economic hardship, displacement, and entrenched social norms are driving increased reliance on child and early marriage as a negative coping mechanism, particularly for girls. The perception of early marriage as a form of protection, combined with limited access to education and livelihood opportunities, continues to undermine girls’ safety, rights, and long-term well-being. Children without parental care, especially unaccompanied children and orphans, face the highest risk.


Psychological and emotional abuse:
 Psychological distress and emotional harm emerged as widespread concerns across assessed communities, reflecting the cumulative impact of prolonged displacement, economic deterioration, loss, insecurity, and repeated exposure to crisis. Respondents reported increased levels of stress, anxiety, emotional exhaustion, social withdrawal, and hopelessness among both adults and children, particularly within households experiencing chronic economic and social pressure. Women, children, persons with disabilities, and caregivers were identified as particularly affected due to compounded responsibilities, disrupted support systems, and prolonged exposure to instability.
