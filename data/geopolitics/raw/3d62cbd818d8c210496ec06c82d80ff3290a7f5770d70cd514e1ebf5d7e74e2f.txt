In May 2026, OCHA conducted a humanitarian access severity monitoring exercise to 
assess humanitarian actors’ perceptions of access constraints and their ability to 
conduct operations across Ukraine. Given the different access dynamics across the 
country, the exercise used two levels of analysis: hromada-level analysis in conflict-af-
fected front-line and border oblasts, and raion-level analysis in central and western 
Ukraine.
In the 521 conflict-affected hromadas assessed in front-line and border oblasts, 251 
hromadas were classified as having high or extremely high access difficulties, up 
from 242 hromadas in October 2025. The deterioration was most significant at the 
most severe end of the scale, with hromadas classified as having extremely high 
access constraints or no access (level 4) increasing from 196 to 211. At the same time, 
the number of hromadas classified as accessible or having low access constraints 
(level 1) declined from 243 to 225.
Access deterioration was driven primarily by the proliferation of drones, recurrent 
attacks affecting humanitarian personnel, assets and facilities, and increasingly 
constrained operating conditions in border areas. These factors reduced operational 
predictability and limited humanitarian presence, including in areas where the front line 
did not shift substantially. Overall, partners reported deterioration in access conditions 
in 42 hromadas. Of these, 15 hromadas worsened from high access difficulties (level 
3) to extremely high access constraints or no access (level 4), mostly in Zaporizka and 
Khersonska oblasts, where five and four hromadas respectively moved into the most 
severe category.
The highest concentrations of hromadas with high access difficulties were recorded 
in areas directly affected by the armed conflict, including areas along the front line or 
exposed to active hostilities, cross-border attacks, drone activity, mines and unexplod-
ed ordnance, and movement restrictions. In these areas, humanitarian operations may 
still take place, but are often intermittent, highly constrained or dependent on specific 
windows of access.
Most hromadas assessed as having extremely high access constraints or no access 
(level 4) were in territories of Ukraine occupied by the Russian Federation. During the 
exercise, these areas remained inaccessible to humanitarian actors operating from 
areas under the control of the Government of Ukraine. The access situation for these 
hromadas was assessed in the most severe category (level 4), with no breakdown by 
specific type of access impediment.
In central and western Ukraine, 74 raions were assessed. Of these, 71 raions were 
classified as accessible or having low access constraints (level 1), while three raions, 
all in Odeska Oblast, were classified as having moderate access constraints (level 2).
Access constraints varied by type, location, operating modality and organization 
type, but the overall pattern was consistent across UN agencies, international NGOs 
and national NGOs. In conflict-affected front-line and border hromadas, the main 
constraints identified were military operations and ongoing hostilities, violence 
affecting humanitarian personnel, assets and facilities, and the presence of mines 
and unexploded ordnance (UXO). Together, these three categories accounted for 
94 per cent of all constraints identified in front-line and border hromadas.
The similarity of constraint patterns across organization types suggests that 
access conditions in front-line and border hromadas were shaped primarily by the 
conflict environment. Access severity was assessed separately, taking into account 
how the identified constraints affected the ability of humanitarian partners to 
conduct operations in each location.
Partners’ concerns regarding violence affecting humanitarian personnel, assets and 
facilities, particularly drone-related incidents, are corroborated by incident reports 
to the Access Monitoring and Reporting Framework. Reported drone-related 
incidents affecting humanitarian personnel, assets and facilities increased from 25 
in the previous reporting period to 36 during the current reporting period, with their 
share of total incidents increasing from 35 per cent to 42 per cent. The impact was 
particularly acute in Khersonska Oblast, where partners identified drone activity as 
a critical constraint on humanitarian operations.
Administrative restrictions on the movement of agencies, personnel or goods 
accounted for a smaller share of constraints identified in front-line and border 
hromadas. These restrictions were nevertheless significant in specific locations 
where checkpoint procedures, enhanced screening, local movement requirements 
or military mobilization-related concerns affected humanitarian missions and staff 
movement. At the same time, partners noted that engagement and advocacy with 
authorities and security services had significantly reduced movement restrictions in 
Sumska Oblast in 2026, after these had been reported as a significant constraint 
since late 2024.
In central and western Ukraine, access constraints were less severe and more 
localized. The main constraints identified were movement constraints linked to 
mobilization-related documentation requirements, border-area access procedures 
and staff movement, as well as mines and unexploded ordnance. While these 
constraints were noted in several locations, the consolidated access severity 
classification remained low in most raions: only three raions, all in Odeska Oblast, 
were classified as having moderate access constraints. Similar concerns were 
reported by national NGOs in six raions in Zakarpatska and Ivano-Frankivska 
oblasts but did not affect the consolidated access severity classification.

METHODOLOGY
The access severity monitoring exercise was conducted through focus group 
discussions with humanitarian partners, including UN agencies, international 
NGOs and national NGOs. It captured partners’ perceptions of access 
constraints and their impact on humanitarian operations. Analysis was 
conducted at hromada level in conflict-affected front-line and border oblasts, 
covering all assessed hromadas, and at raion level in central and western 
Ukraine, covering all raions. Areas in territories of Ukraine occupied by the 
Russian Federation were included in the analysis and assessed as level 4, as 
they remained inaccessible to humanitarian actors operating from areas under 
the control of the Government of Ukraine. Findings reflect partners’ reported 
access perceptions and operational constraints at the time of data collection.
Between January and May 2026, humanitarian partners reached 1.46 million 
people in front-line and border oblasts, based on direct reach reporting. Reach 
was highest in hromadas classified as accessible or having low access 
constraints (level 1), with 527,000 people reached, or 36 per cent of reported 
reach. This was followed by hromadas with high access difficulties (level 3), 
where 482,000 people were directly reached and assisted, or 33 per cent; 
hromadas with moderate access constraints (level 2), where 368,000 people 
were reached, or 25 per cent; and hromadas with extremely high access 
constraints or no access (level 4), where around 80,000 people were reached, or 
6 per cent. Reported reach in the most severe access category should be 
interpreted with caution, given displacement, limited data availability, and the fact 
that most hromadas in this category are in territories of Ukraine occupied by the 
Russian Federation.
The scale of direct reach in hromadas with high access difficulties (level 3), 
nearly as high as in accessible areas and representing one third of all reported 
reach in assessed front-line and border hromadas, indicates that partners 
continued to deliver assistance at scale in prioritized areas despite significant 
constraints. Direct reach in this category was geographically concentrated, with 
Khersonska and Dnipropetrovska oblasts accounting for 178,000 and 117,000 
people reached respectively, or about 61 per cent of all reach in hromadas with 
high access difficulties. Sumska and Kharkivska oblasts also accounted for 
significant reach, with 62,000 and 59,000 people reached respectively. These 
figures should be interpreted alongside differences in population size, partner 
presence and established programming, which influence absolute reach
across areas.
Most hromadas classified as having extremely high access constraints or no 
access (level 4) are in territories of Ukraine occupied by the Russian Federation 
and remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors operating from Govern-
ment-controlled areas. At the same time, reported reach in this category was 
concentrated in areas of Khersonska, Zaporizka, Dnipropetrovska and Sumska 
oblasts that remain under the control of the Government of Ukraine but were 
assessed as having extremely high access constraints or no access due to 
intensified hostilities and severe operational constraints.
In the 15 hromadas that deteriorated to extremely high access constraints or no 
access (level 4) between the October 2025 and May 2026 exercises, reported 
direct reach fell from approximately 92,000 people in January–May 2025 to 8,500 
people in January–May 2026, a decline of more than 90 per cent. This reflects 
the operational impact of intensified hostilities and the narrowing or loss of 
access in areas where humanitarian response had previously
remained possible.
In central and western Ukraine, which are largely accessible and outside the 
front-line and border oblast total above, partners reported 372,000 people 
reached.
