SITUATION OVERVIEW 
 
• Burkina Faso faces a complex crisis driven by 
insecurity, climate shocks, and structural 
vulnerabilities. Reliance on rainfed agriculture 
heightens exposure to climate variability. Insecurity 
remains the primary driver of humanitarian needs, 
with 2 million internally displaced persons (latest 
official figure, March 2023), limiting access to land, 
markets, and essential services. 
• The preliminary results from the April 2026 national 
food security and nutrition analysis projects 1.66 
million people will face crisis-level food insecurity (IPC 
Phase 3+), including 223,000 people in emergency 
situations, during the June–August 2026 lean season, 
compared to 2.3 million in 2025. While the situation 
shows an overall improvement relative to last year, 
access constraints remain significant in several 
regions, despite localized progress observed in 2025 
such as the restoration of access to Titao and 
Barsalogho. 
• Already in 2025, funding shortfalls forced WFP to 
reduce its coverage by around 30 percent compared 
to 2024, contributing to deteriorating food 
consumption and increased negative coping 
strategies among vulnerable households. WFP 
continues to deliver an integrated response including 
emergency food and nutrition assistance, school 
feeding, resilience activities, and common services 
through UNHAS, supporting national efforts toward 
stabilization and food sovereignty.

May 2026 
OPERATIONAL UPDATES 
General Food Assistance  
• In April, WFP distributed over 2,907 mt of food and 
nutrition assistance to 197,404 people in localities 
such as Silmague, Seguenega, and Diapaga, 
including 180 mt airlifted to localities such as 
Kantchari or Gorgadji.  
• WFP took part in two government-mandated 
convoys: one to Gayeri and another to Sebba and 
Solhan. WFP had last joined convoys to these 
locations in April 2025 and December 2024, 
respectively. 
• The main challenge remains funding constraints, 
which limit WFP’s ability to meet needs. In addition, 
two WFP airlift helicopters were unavailable due to 
operational and administrative constraints, 
significantly reducing airlift capacity and increasing 
reliance on road transport. 
Nutrition 
• WFP supported malnutrition prevention by 
assisting 855 young children and 553 women, while 
screening over 2,600 children and identifying very 
few cases of moderate malnutrition; nutrition 
awareness reached more than 4,300 people. 
• To treat moderate acute malnutrition, WFP 
provided specialized nutritious foods to over 
31,700 children and 15,600 women. 
• WFP also strengthened local production of fortified 
foods through quality audits and training for 
national producers, improving standards and 
capacity. 
School-Based Assistance 
• In April, WFP reached 236,644 students (56 percent 
girls) with daily hot meals in 755 schools, including 
7,259 students who received take-home rations 
(THR), to support attendance and retention. 
• Under the integrated programme, 171,950 
students (56 percent girls) in 629 schools received 
hot meals, with THR provided to upper primary 
students meeting attendance criteria. Under 
emergency school feeding, 64,694 students (54 
percent girls) in 126 schools were assisted, 
including 34,484 IDPs, with support limited to on-
site meals due to funding constraints. 
• Efforts focused on programme quality and capacity 
strengthening (field monitoring, partnerships, 
kitchen construction), alongside coordination with 
partners and government, including Education 
Cluster engagement and planning for the phase-
out of emergency school feeding by end-2026. 
Resilience and Livelihood Support 
• In April 2026, WFP strengthened community 
resilience through the Food Assistance for Assets 
(FFA) programme, supporting the production of 
185 compost heaps (9 m³ each), treatment of 17 
gullies, and restoration of 86 hectares of degraded 
land. Activities also included the construction of 
2,101 improved cookstoves, rehabilitation of 14 
water ponds, seedling production, and assistance 
to 2,686 beneficiaries with 266 mt of food. 
• Through the Smallholder Agricultural Market 
Support (SAMS) programme, WFP reinforced 
livelihoods by supporting farmer organization 
targeting and delivering SBCC messaging, while 
strengthening capacities through trainings of 40 
participants (13 women) on GBV/PSEA and data 
collection, alongside project staff induction and 
technical workshops. 
United Nations Humanitarian Air Service 
(UNHAS) 
• In April 2026, UNHAS supported humanitarian 
operations through 246 flights to 27 destinations, 
serving 71 user organizations, and transporting 
1,351 passengers as well as 22.9 mt of light cargo. 
Despite limited flight cancellations, UNHAS 
maintained access to isolated and hard-to-reach 
locations, including Bourzanga, Gorgadji, Arbinda, 
Djibo, and other remote areas.
