Attacks on Health Care  
News Brief    
10-23 June 
2026 
 
Since the adoption of United Nations Security Council Resolution 2286 in 2016 and 09 June 2026, Insecurity Insight has 
documented over 19,000 attacks on health care worldwide, highlighting the persistent and widespread failure to protect 
health care in conflict. 
To mark a decade since the adoption, Insecurity Insight released a landmark report analysing ten years of violence against 
health care. Drawing on a decade of systematically collected data, Still Under Attack documents the scale, patterns, and 
consequences of attacks on health care in conflict-affected settings around the world. Among the report's key findings: 
● More than 3,860 health workers were killed and over 2,500 were arrested or detained between January 2016 
and December 2025. 
● Local health workers remain particularly vulnerable, accounting for more than 65% of documented health worker 
killings. 
● The growing use of armed drones and explosive weapons is contributing to increasing damage to health facilities 
and to health worker deaths and injuries. 
 
Based exclusively on Insecurity Insight's data collection, the report shows how violence against health care has become 
increasingly normalised, with severe consequences for civilians, disease response, and the resilience of health systems. 
The report also  provides detailed analysis of perpetrators, methods of attack, and emerging risks, including the growing 
role of new technologies in conflict. It concludes with recommendations for governments, international organisations, 
humanitarian actors, and the diplomatic community to strengthen the protection of health care and improve accountability 
for violations.

Insecurity Insight’s latest social media monitoring brief analyses public narratives surrounding the 17th Ebola epidemic 
in the Democratic Republic of the Congo between 15 June to 22 June 2026. Key findings: 
● Politics, not the outbreak, drove the conversation. Comment volume rose 33% on the previous period, but 
almost entirely on the back of a single statement by an opposition figure linking the DRC's World Cup 
performance to governance failures (1,160 comments, 26.5% of the total). Harmful content stayed minimal 
in posts and scattered in comments. 
● A government attempt to deflect criticism onto an NGO backfired. After a health organisation warned of 
"dangerous gaps" in the response, the government rejected the criticism as "alarmist" and urged NGOs to 
show restraint. The backlash was overwhelmingly h ostile to the government rather than the NGO, and 
dominated by the "Ebola business" corruption frame. 
● A cluster of attacks on Ebola facilities and health workers was amplified and reframed online. The incidents 
are real and corroborated, but their online life was driven by a small network of accounts - and the single 
highest-reach post had over 400,000 views.

Occupied Palestinian territory  
 
Data collected by Insecurity Insight shows that between 07 October 2023 and 09 June 2026, at least 3,327 incidents of 
violence against or obstruction of access to health care took place across the occupied Palestinian territory.1 95% of all 
incidents were attributed to Israeli forces. 
● Gaza: 2,328 attacks on health care were recorded during which health facilities were damaged 448 times, 765 
health workers were killed and 398 arrested.2 Patient access to medicine was obstructed on 289 occasions.   
● West Bank and East Jerusalem: 938 attacks on health care were recorded during which health facilities were 
damaged 30 times, 12 health workers were killed and 188 arrested. Patient access to medicine was obstructed 
on 579 occasions.   
Of the 938 attacks on health care in the West Bank, 47 were attributed to Israeli settlers, who were typically armed with 
guns or rocks. The proportion of such attacks more than doubled in 2025 compared to 2024, rising from around 2% to 
9% of total reported attacks. Between 01 January and 09 June 2026, Israeli settler-attributed attacks increased further, 
accounting for 22% of all reported attacks during that period. Most Israeli settler attacks  on health care targeted 
ambulances and first responders, including blocking their access to those needing care. On at least seven occasions, 
Israeli settlers attacked health care under the protection of the IDF.

Other resources 
Attacks on health care were relentless in 2025. Using data collected by Insecurity Insight, the Safeguarding Health in 
Conflict Coalition (SHCC) recorded 2,546 incidents in 33 countries in which damage to or the destruction of health 
care facilities was reported on at least 790 occasions, 455 health workers were killed, 218 kidnapped and 263 arrested. 
Key findings in 2025 
● Violence rose in 13 countries and recorded incidents remained far above the ten-
year annual average. 
● Continued high levels of violence by state forces alongside rising levels of violence 
by non-state armed actors. 
● The expansion of remotely piloted aerial systems (drones) increasingly affected 
health care in conflict settings. 
● Kidnapping of health workers increased, particularly in the Central African 
Republic, Colombia, the DRC, Mali, Pakistan and Syria. 
The report includes detailed profiles of 21 countries and territories where many acts of 
violence against health care by state parties and non -state armed groups, including 
organized criminal groups, took place. These include: 
Africa: Burkina Faso, Cameroon, the CAR, the DRC, Ethiopia, Mali, Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan and Sudan 
Asia: Myanmar and Pakistan 
The Middle East and Europe: Lebanon, Iran, the occupied Palestinian territory; Syria, Ukraine and Yemen 
The Americas: Colombia, Haiti and Mexico

Global Health Conference: The Right to Health in Times of War  
Bringing together health professionals, researchers, and activists, the conference will examine why medical neutrality 
is being eroded, what this means for affected populations in and beyond the immediate line of fire, and how health can 
be protected under conditions of violence. 
The program includes a keynote by Christina Wille (Insecurity Insight/Safeguarding Health in Conflict Coalition) as 
well as workshops with experts from Sudan, Lebanon, and Ukraine, including Hind Altaif (Emergency Response 
Rooms Sudan) and Dr. Zeina Mohanna (Amel Association International, Lebanon). The conference will address health 
systems under strain beyond active conflict zones, focusing on the militarisation and gradual erosion of health care as 
well as the ethical implications of these developments. Contributors include Prof. Dr. Yudit Namer (University of 
Twente) and the Gesundheitskollektiv Berlin.
