SITUATION OVERVIEW 
Despite the extension of the ceasefire announced last week, Lebanon continues to experience new waves of displacement 
driven by insecurity – airstrikes, shelling and displacement orders across broad swaths of southern Lebanon. The Israeli 
Defence Forces (IDF) issued three renewed displacement orders to residents of six localities  during the reporting period . 
Notably, a displacement order on 9 June covered all neighbourhoods of Tyre as well as the Palestinian Refugee Camps for 
the first time.

(601,384 m³). The report notes these figures are preliminary, derived from remote sensing supported by systematic ground 
inspections.

HUMANITARIAN RESPONSE 
Since 2 March, humanitarian partners in Lebanon have significantly expanded multi -sectoral assistance for vulnerable, 
conflict-affected civilians, including displaced people, host communities and those remaining in hard -to-reach areas.  
Education support continues through formal and non-formal learning, despite disruptions caused by schools being used as 
shelters, prompting the establishment of temporary learning hubs. Food assistance has scaled up through meal provision, 
ready-to-eat ra tions, and bread. Health services have been sustained despite funding gaps and facility closures, while 
nutrition interventions continue to support vulnerable children and pregnant or breastfeeding women through screening, 
targeted feeding, and dedicated safe spaces. 
Protection, shelter, and essential services responses have also expanded, though gaps persist. Protection partners are 
delivering awareness activities, psychosocial support, and assistance to vulnerable groups, while shelter actors are 
improving conditions and providing cash support. Site management is being scaled up, and WASH services continue to 
reach affected populations. Despite strengthened coordination, security constraints and access challenges continue to 
disrupt humanitarian operations, particularly in southern Lebanon. 
 Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) 
The Cash Working Group (CWG) maintains close collaboration with implementing partners and Government counterparts 
to ensure a well -coordinated and inclusive response. While most markets across areas of operation continue to function, 
persistent affordabili ty challenges and eroding household purchasing power are increasingly undermining the ability of 
vulnerable populations to meet their basic needs. One of the most pressing MPCA priorities remains the mobilisation of 
additional financial resources. Data cleaning processes are ongoing, and the targeting approach is scheduled to commence 
as of 9 June to disburse the second and third cash payments for the most vulnerable conflict -affected populations.

 Education 
 
As of 11 June, Education Sector partners have reached 257,493 displaced children through formal education, 94,073 
in person and 163,420 online, alongside 4,938 children through non -formal education (4,832 in person, 106 online). 
Partners distributed 1,728 individual learning materials, supported 106 children with disabilities through assistive devices 
or rehabilitation services, and delivered 2,003 education and supply kits at the shelter level.  
Of Lebanon's 3,341 schools, 446 (13.3  per cent ), 331 public, 66 private, and 49 technical institutes, remain in use as 
collective shelters, hosting approximately, 17,231 children. This continues to constrain access to safe learning spaces, 
particularly for adolescent girls, children with disabilities, and afternoon-shift students. 
A key development during this reporting period is the launch of Ministry of Education and Higher Education ( MEHE) 
Teaching and Learning Hubs (TLHs), bridging emergency response with longer -term learning recovery. As a first phase, 
MEHE rolled out intensive in -person academic support for Grade 12 students in public secondary schools preparing for 
official exam ination, implemented through Centre for Educational Research and Development ( CERD) in four priority 
locations with high concentrations of displaced learners: Aley , Chouf ( Mount Lebanon Governorate ), Saida (South 
Governorate), and Beirut. Approximately 600 Grade 12 students across all academic specialities have enrolled, with hubs 
operating in  morning and afternoon shifts and students organised into small groups for targeted, in -person teaching. 
Outreach has been conducted to identify and reach students most in need of this support.  
Looking ahead, under the revised appeal, the sector is planning to support MEHE in scaling up TLHs to additional locations, 
strengthening targeting criteria to prioritise the most vulnerable children, particularly those in shelters and hard -to-reach 
areas, and expanding summer catch -up programming to address accumulated learning loss. Sustained donor support 
remains critical to maximise reach and ensure continuity of these efforts ahead of the new school year.  
 Food Security and Agriculture 
Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector partners have distributed more than 13.3 million hot and cold meals 
and 145,436 ready -to-eat (RTE) rations  since 2 March. These distributions reached displaced families in collective 
shelters through in -kind assistance, as well as vulnerable households outside formal collective shelters through cash 
modalities. Such interventions remain critical in supporting households that have lost access to markets and livelihoods due 
to ongoing hostilities and displacement. Starting mid -April, shelter distributions were complemented by bread distributions 
coordinated with the Ministry of Economy and Trade and the Disaster Risk Management Unit. To date, 170,286 bread 
bundles have been distributed, through FSA Sector partners. 
In North and Akkar: Due to the recent increase in displacement towards Akkar, two additional shelters were opened over 
the past week. Across both regions, all shelters are receiving daily lunch meals. A second meal is being provided in a limited 
number of locations, covering  23 shelters in the North and 1 shelter in Akkar. Despite the steady progress, a gap in RTE 
assistance is anticipated starting mid -June based on the current pipeline, with an expected shortfall of 700 RTEs in the 
North and 384 RTEs in Akkar. 
In Bekaa and Baalbek-El Hermel: Shelters across Bekaa and Baalbek-El Hermel Governorates are currently receiving full 
coverage of daily hot meal distributions. In addition, a second daily meal is being provided, with 14 shelters benefiting from 
breakfast distributions, while the remaining sites are supported through ready -to-eat (RTE) food assistance. 
In Beirut and Mount Lebanon:  The distribution of food parcels to populations outside collective shelters is ongoing, 
targeting 25,000  displaced families in Beirut and 30,000 displaced families in Beirut’s southern suburbs , in close 
coordination with relevant authorities. Lunch meal provision has been successfully established across the majority of 
shelters. With rising temperatures, feedback was received from IDPs regarding difficulties in consuming the meals. The 
sector is actively exploring mitigation measures to adapt food assistance modalities to the current climatic conditions.  
In South Lebanon and Nabatieh: To support access to hard-to-reach areas in South Lebanon, convoys consisting of food 
and life-saving assistance were coordinated with the Logistics Cluster.  
 
Health 
Since the ceasefire announcement (17 April -11 June), 4,844 additional casualties were recorded, including 1,207 deaths, 
with Nabatiyeh and the South again most affected. Attacks on health care have continued despite the ceasefire, with 17 
hospitals damaged, three remaining fully non -operational, and 44 primary health centres closed; the WHO Surveillance 
System has recorded 39 4 injuries and 135 deaths among health workers and patients since 2 March. Displacement also 
surged on 7 June, with roughly 50,000 fam ilies displaced from Beirut's southern suburbs, placing additional strain on 
shelters, 52 per cent of whose occupants are women and girls.

Health Sector partners delivered over 134 m³ and 3.5 tons of emergency and trauma medical supplies to frontline facilities,

Nutrition 
Nutrition Sector partners continue to deliver life -saving nutrition services for the most vulnerable population groups, 
pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW), infants, young children, and adolescent girls, through primary health care 
centres, education actors, mobile teams, outreach activities, and collective shelters. The sector targets all children and PBW 
in shelters with essential nutrition packages. Outside shelters, identification and prioritisation of populations in need is 
conducted through communi ty outreach, nutrition screening, Rapid Nutrition Assessments, referrals through the national 
IYCF hotline and primary stabilisation units, and needs identified by DRM structures, particularly in hard -to-reach areas. 
Under the revised appeal, partners plan to scale up activities in areas affected by displacement and increasing vulnerability, 
while the sector is maximising coverage through collaboration with Food Security Sector partners to reach displaced 
households outside shelters and strengthen coordination with DRM. 
Since 2 March, 46,320 children under five, adolescent girls, and pregnant and breastfeeding women  (PBW) have 
been reached with emergency nutrition rations and micronutrient supplements, amounting to 682,433 rations and 
supplements covering at least one month. This includes 857 children aged 6–12 months in collective shelters receiving 
age-appropriate complementary feeding kits. In addition, 21,279 children under five and 4,312 PBW were screened for 
wasting, enabling early identification and referral of individuals requiring further nutrition support and treatment. 
The national Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) hotline continues to serve as a critical support channel, receiving 697 
calls from caregivers between 1 March and 4 June, with 544 callers referred for specialised IYCF support services . In 
addition, 1,107 IYCF Simple Rapid Assessments were conducted to identify feeding challenges and inform appropriate 
support and referrals. 
In parallel, under the MoPH -led Safe Provision of Breastmilk Substitutes (BMS) initiative, 335 infants under one year of 
age received infant formula  support following individual assessments conducted by Nutrition Sector partners' IYCF 
specialists, resulting in the distribution of 3,357 cans of infant formula . Complementing this, 16,486 caregivers of 
children under five engaged in counselling and awareness sessions covering IYCF, nutrition, and Early Childhood 
Development (ECD), delivered across both in-person and digital platforms. 
To support nurturing care and create safe spaces for caregivers and young children, 10 ECD and Mother -Baby Corners 
were established, providing integrated opportunities for caregiver support, responsive caregiving, early learning, and IYCF 
counselling. 
 Protection 
In hard-to-reach areas of West Bekaa, previously identified families remain in informal collective settings, and some have 
expressed willingness to relocate; however, financial constraints continue to hinder their movement. Cases have been 
referred to partners for assessment and possible cash assistance. 
As of 11 June, 262,946 internally displaced people (IDPs) and other affected people received protection services, of 
whom around 62 per cent are women and girls. Services provided included outreach, consultations, and information and 
awareness group sessions for 137,069 individuals on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Gender-Based 
Violence (GBV) core concepts, and digital violence;   child protection messages for children, adolescents and caregivers 
and legal issues ; psychosocial support activities for over 82,000 individuals, primarily children and caregivers; and case 
management services for 1,735 individuals. In addition, 3,135 protection cash grants have supported approximately 10,473 
vulnerable individuals, while  more than 30,375 women and girls have received dignity kits, including GBV awareness 
materials. Specialised support, including assistive devices and dedicated care, has also been provided to 1,744 persons 
with disabilities. 
Work on enhancing cross-sectoral collaboration on GBV risk mitigation and joint targeting  continued. During the National 
Protection Working Group, UNHCR presented a geospatial analysis highlighting critical coverage gaps in Mount Lebanon, 
particularly in Jbeil district, where approximately 70 out of 270 collective shelters remain unsupported by any prot ection 
partner. This underscores significant disparities in service coverage and the urgent need for reallocation or scaling up of 
protection presence in underserved areas. 
Protection partners in Beirut and Mount Lebanon reported ongoing delivery of psychosocial support sessions across 
collective shelters. These activities have active participation of women, children, and adolescents.  Partners noted that

referred to partners for assessment and possible cash assistance. 
Key operational challenges persist, particularly the overstretched capacity of partners to adequately cover collective shelters

Shelter 
 
Inside collective shelters, the Shelter Sector partners are prioritising repairs at 420 sites, with work completed in 341  (81 
per cent) of the  sites. Interventions focus on improving safety, privacy, and capacity. Since 2 March, all households in 
collective shelters have received initial Core Relief Items (CRIs).  Distributions include 111,370 mattresses, 138,569 
blankets, and 74,350 pillows, alongside other essential Non-Food Items (NFI)s. 
Outside collective shelters, partners have provided case -by-case support to vulnerable households, including those in 
hosted and rented accommodation. Since 2 March, distributions have reached 27,712 mattresses, 40,229 blankets, and 
16,786 pillows. In addi tion, 1,034 vulnerable households received a one -off USD 250 cash -for-shelter grant to support 
urgent shelter-related expenses, including minor repairs, rent, and utility costs. 
Under the revised Flash Appeal , Shetler partners  will continue response inside collective shelters, by providing in -kind 
assistance, shelter repairs, accessibility adaptations enhancements, and CRIs. Outside collective shelters, the focus will be 
on cash-for-rent and cash -for-shelter assistance . In-kind support will remain prioritised in hard -to-reach areas or where 
markets are constrained. Additionally, the sector will prioritise vulnerable families living in substandard residential buildings 
in need of repair. 
 
Site Management and Coordination (SMC) 
Amid continued escalation and displacement, which are placing increasing pressure on collective sites, the SMC sub-sector 
is scaling up its response, aiming to support approximately 80,000 individuals across 400 sites, subject to the availability of 
funding. This will be achieved through the deployment of mobile site management support teams to strengthen government-
led on-site coordination mechanisms and improve site management structures.  
With the launch of the Flash Appeal, and in an effort to strengthen partner reporting the sub-sector is reviewing its activity 
indicators to ensure alignment with SMART criteria. In parallel, a prioritisation methodology is being developed to enable a 
more targeted response. This approach will be based on key thematic parameters, including operational risks, population 
density, vulnerability and needs, as well as existing site management capacity. The exercise will draw on data from the 
Emergency Response and Needs Assessment (ERNA), GBV safety audits, and the MoSA site management activity tracker. 
To further strengthen the capacity of SMC partners —particularly newly engaged organi sations—23 staff members have 
registered to participate in the first Basic SMC training, scheduled for the week of 22 June.

Water, Sanitation and Hygiene 
As of 11 June, the WASH Sector has reached 1,074,702 people across collective shelters, host communities, and out-
of-shelter settings through emergency water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Despite this progress, significant gaps 
remain. Assessment data from the Rapid Technical Assessment (Round 2) identified additional needs to meet minimum 
sector standards. 
Since 2 March, WASH Sector partners have provided 851,000 litres of fuel to water establishments , sustaining public 
water supply services for an estimated 841,128 people . WASH partners further supported 296 water stations with fuel , 
repairs, and spare parts critical to maintaining network functionality under increased demand.  
Ninety-seven per cent of collective shelters are receiving WASH support. Displaced people inside collective shelters have 
received a total of 114,273 hygiene kits, 68,705 menstrual hygiene kits, and 1,533 cleaning kits. WASH partners have also 
provided 3,765,725 litres of potable water and 110,770 m³ of water to collective shelters through water trucking, addressing 
immediate drinking water needs in sites with limited or disrupted water supply.  
The revised appeal reflects a shift from immediate emergency assistance towards sustaining water service delivery in areas 
with high concentrations of displaced populations and vulnerable host communities. Increased investments in fuel, 
rehabilitation, operations and maintenance, water trucking, and sanitation facilities . 
 Logistics and Telecommunications (LTC) 
As of 11 June 2026, the Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) continues to support humanitarian logistics 
operations across Lebanon through coordination, common services, and information management. Access constraints 
persist, with operations relying heavily on the Port of Beirut and the Al Masnaa border crossing, where congestion, security 
risks, and transshipment requirements continue to delay cargo movements.  
The LTC continues to provide free transport to partners and shared storage for temporary warehousing and consolidation. 
Coordination of regional convoys from Jordan remains ongoing. The LTC is compiling pipeline cargo data from multiple 
partners to strengt hen coordinated convoy planning and is defining a formal system to prioritise cargo from the Jordan 
Corridor.  
On telecommunications, the cluster is exploring options to conduct a follow -up assessment to update the connectivity and 
risk analysis carried out in March and April.  
Operational conditions continue to affect convoy movements, as seen with the postponed LTC movement to Zefta and 
Saida – South Governorate (planned for 9 June). Despite that, the LTC convoys reached Sarafand (South Governorate) on 
5 June and Rmeish (Nabatieh Governorate) on 11 June. Overall, the LTC has conducted 26 convoys within Lebanon and 
three regional convoys starting  from Jordan to Lebanon , transporting around 4,103 m³ and stor ing around 2,064 m² in 
humanitarian cargo.

Access and Civil-Military Coordination 
Humanitarian access in Lebanon remains highly constrained and increasingly reliant on notification mechanisms as security 
risks persist despite the declared and renewed ceasefire. Humanitarian movements to areas South of the Litani River (SLR), 
as well as parts of the area North of the Litani River (NLR), particularly Nabatieh Governorate, continue amid heightened 
insecurity and operational unpredictability.  
With support from the Humanitarian Notification System (HNS), humanitarian partners continue to facilitate access to 
conflict-affected and hard-to-reach areas across southern Lebanon and other impacted regions. Between 2 March and 11 
June, 203 humanitarian  movements were supported through the HNS 2. Of these, 170 missions south of the Litani River 
were facilitated through established notification arrangements and coordination with UNIFIL, in line with prevailing 
operational modalities. Between 26 May and 9 June, 22 movements — primarily to Tyre Dist rict, the South Governorate 
were cancelled and one was postponed due to security concerns and recent developments.  
While most missions were completed, several faced operational challenges, including delays, postponements, restricted 
operational windows, route constraints, communications disruptions, GPS spoofing, unexploded ordnance (UXO) 
contamination, and cancellations linked to evolving security conditions. Some missions also encountered nearby hostilities 
and ongoing military activity during implementation.  
These movements enabled the delivery of critical life-saving assistance, including bread, fresh produce, flour, safe drinking 
water, fuel, medical supplies, and core relief items. Where feasible and requested, humanitarian actors also supported the 
relocation of vulnerable individuals from high-risk areas to safer locations.
