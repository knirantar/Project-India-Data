REPORTED NEEDS AND RESPONSE FOR NEW ARRIVALS
The needs of the majority of IDPs in a location refer to the total IDP population 
who may have arrived at any time, while new arrival needs refer to IDP flows 
who arrived in a specified period of time (e.g. week 04). Concerning the former, 
most IDPs needed food (63%), while others needed drinking water (19%), 
shelter (6%) and livelihoods (5%). 
With regard to new arrivals from 05 to 08 July, the main reported needs were 
shelter and food (96% each), NFIs and health (91% each), nutrition (90%) and 
water services (80%). Response levels during the assessed days were very low 
compared to reported needs, with assistance reported for sanitation (5%), heath 
(2%) and nutrition services (1%).

METHODOLOGY AND CONTEXT
LIMITATIONS
This round of Emergency Trend Tracking (ETT) was initiated in June 2026 to 
monitor displacement due to conflict, as well as the climatic impacts of the Super 
El Niño phenomenon, which is expected to cause significant displacement toward 
the end of the year.
DTM teams collect data in five districts: Baidoa, Dayniile, Diinsoor, Doolow, and 
Kahda.
ETT is a crisis-based tool that tracks sudden displacement triggered by specific events 
or emerging crises. Its objective is to support the prioritization of humanitarian 
response and enable partners to deliver rapid assistance. Based on previous shock-
induced displacement patterns, the humanitarian community expects continued 
movement toward urban areas in search of services. Consequently, ETT coverage 
focuses on main urban centers and surrounding villages within each assessed 
district.
Data is collected through phone calls with a network of key informants 
(KIs) across all locations recorded in the DTM masterlist, developed over 
several years of in-person data collection. Data collection takes place weekly, 
from Sunday to Wednesday. It includes information on new arrivals, the 
number and demographics of IDPs, reasons for displacement, intentions, 
humanitarian assistance received, and priority needs, among others.  
The data is collected through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and remotly since 
June 2026, consequently the findings should be considered as estimates. Most 
indicators are also captured for the “majority of the people displaced within a 
location/because of a specific factor”. For example, the place of origin assessed 
for the new arrivals in one location, is the place of origin of the majority of these 
new arrivals. The detailed information on all places of origin is not collected and 
reported. For the reasons for displacement, although the ETT breaks down the 
new arrivals per main cause, IDPs may be  displaced because of a combination 
of factors. The exercise covers the main urban centers and surrounding areas 
in each district, consequently none of the districts have a full coverage. The GPS 
coordinates collected can be approximations and are not always exact locations.

DEFINITIONS
New arrivals: number of individuals who arrive in the settlement a given week. 
This number doesn’t represent new IDPs as new arrivals may be primary but 
also secondary displacements. 
Urban settlements: neighborhoods located in the main administrative town of 
each district. 
Rural settlements: villages outside of cities and towns. 
IDP sites: camp and camp-like settings hosting IDPs.
