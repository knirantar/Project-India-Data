Circular and Sustainable Warehouse
IMPROVING
through better structural design, energy-
efficient lighting, and enhanced inventory 
management systems; 
REDUCING
by minimizing water and energy consumption;
RECYCLING
by segregating and channeling waste streams 
such as cardboard, plastics, and metals into 
appropriate local recycling systems. 
A circular warehouse minimizes environmental 
impact by using resources efficiently, reducing 
waste, and keeping materials in use while 
supporting operations.

Circular Economy logic
From damaged and unused MSU* components to operational Warehouse infrastructure
01 | Context
Recovered MSU components 
assessed for reuse
Inventory and material assessment from MSU poles and metallic tubes
Circular logic
Recover → Redesign → Build → Reuse in operations
The approach extends material life while reducing the 
need for new structural components.
* MSU : Mobile Storage Unit         Source: Chad Engineering team project brief, May 2025  | Prepared for experience sharing
Technical design features
Warehouse structure and passive cooling elements
02 | Design
Warehouse configuration
o 10m wide, 44m long and 3.50m high.
o Structure built using recycled old metallic bars from MSUs.
o Five ventilation windows on each side, each 0.4m x 0.6m.
o Two main doors of 5m x 4m to allow large truck access.
o Reinforced concrete foundation with starter columns and base plates for the main vertical frames.
Passive cooling approach
• Two-segment roof with a 0.6m 
ventilation gap.
• Aluminum-zinc external wall 
cladding.
• Internal lining with cellulose foam 
and plywood.
• Painted internal finishes for 
usability and durability.

Additional circular value from the same recycled MSU material stream
04 | Shelving
Assembly of modular shelving components
 Load-testing with heavy materials
Shelving features
▪ Built from recycled MSU metallic parts.
▪ Approximate dimensions: 3m high x 7.5m long.
▪ Detachable design with three compartments.
▪ Can be disassembled, transported and reassembled based on warehouse needs.
▪ Designed for practical assembly by non-specialized labour.

Why a thermal mapping and what it can answer ?
▪ How temperature varies between entrance, center, walls and roof-
adjacent zones.
▪ Whether higher layers or shelf levels experience greater heat 
exposure.
▪ Whether design features are producing measurable improvements 
compared with standard structures.
▪ What monitoring configuration should be maintained for sensitive 
commodities.
▪ Validated temperature-performance profile.
▪ Evidence-based communications package for circular warehousing.
▪ Operational recommendations for commodity placement and 
monitoring.
▪ Replication considerations for other hubs and field operations.

Bauchi Warehouses Case Studies
• 3 WH sites assessed for solar PV 
implementation
•  Sites: Galadima, Fertilizer and Gidan 
Gona Warehouses
• Leveraged GIZ support for energy 
audits pro-bono
• Objective: Improve sustainability, 
reduce emissions & environmental 
impact, achieve cost savings and 
strengthen operational resilience.
• Systems sized based on actual load 
profiles
Galadima Warehouse: High Impact Investment
17 kWp PV + 20 kWh battery
83.4% solar coverage
Annual PV production: 29,397 kWh
IRR: 17.1%
Payback period: 5.54 years
85% reduction in diesel consumption
78% reduction in CO₂ emissions
