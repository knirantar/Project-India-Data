PROTECTION RISKS 
Attacks on civilians and other unlawful killings, and attacks on civ ilian 
objects: In Jonglei, Upper Nile, and parts of Unity, between 226,000 and 280,000 
civilians have been displaced, facing severe secondary protection risks. Vulnerable 
groups, including the elderly, women, children, and individuals with disabilities, are 
particularly affected, requiring mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) 
and facing dangers such as gender -based violence, family separations, and 
exposure to unexploded ordnance. 
Child recruitment and association of children in armed forces and groups:  
Children and youth frequently become involved with armed groups during inter -
communal conflicts in affected areas of the country. The causes include structural 
factors like income generation, community needs, and ineffective coping strategies, 
exacerbated by displacement. In the current conflict in Jonglei and Upper Nile, there 
is si gnificant recruitment of children by armed forces, along with a decline in 
adherence to International Humanitarian Law by these groups.  Between April and 
December 2025, 482 verified grave violations were documented against 322 
children in South Sudan. Boys accounted for 70% of affected children, girls for 27%, 
and 3% were of unknown sex. The ratio of violations to children indicates that some 
children were subjected to multiple violations. 
The most prevalent violations were recruitment and use (33%) and kil ling and 
maiming (33%), together accounting for two-thirds of all verified cases. Recruitment 
and use predominantly affected boys and reflect continued child association with 
armed actors. The high proportion of killing and maiming underscores children ’s 
sustained exposure to active hostilities and explosive violence. 
Gender-based violence: A secondary risk associated with the displacement and 
assaults on civilians. As they move and reside in overcrowded displacement shelters 
while seeking livelihoods many women and girls face challenges due to deteriorating 
community structures.  However, there  is a n exponentially growing concern 
regarding the rise in reports of Conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV). With the 
current conflict settings, th is has been brutality extreme. In 2025, rape and gang 
rape were the most frequently documented forms of CRSV compared to 2024, 
accounting for 81 per cent of all incidents. Specifically, rape accounted for 51 per 
cent of the total number of all incidents, while gang rape accounted for 30 per cent. 
Forced marriage followed with 7 per cent of incidents. Sexual slavery represented 6 
per cent. Attempted forced marriage with 2 per cent. Other forms of CRSV, each, 
accounted for 1 per cent of the total incidents respectively. 
Discrimination and stigmatization, denial of resources, opportunities, 
services and/or humanitarian access: Humanitarian actors operational in Jonglei 
and Upper Nile have on occasion witnessed access constraints as a tactic of 
besiegement and a driver of displacement, as well as an instrument for collective 
punishment. While some areas are accessible, guarantees are difficult to achieve 
and life-threatening incidents to humanitarian staff have been recorded.

POPULATION IN NEED 
Conflict displacement severely impacts individuals who are forced to flee, 
creating a demand for critical support in food, shelter, sanitation, and 
protection. The UNHCR evaluation among internally displaced persons (IDPs) 
in Akobo reveals significant vulnerabilities, particularly among households led 
by women (72.6%), due to conflict -related male casualties. The typical 
household size is six, with many containing elderly individuals (59%) who serve 
as caregivers. Additionally, 14.5% of households include people with 
disabilities, struggling with accessibility and support needs, while at -risk 
children represent 8.2% of the population, often unaccompanied or facing 
exploitation. Women, particularly those at risk (30.8%), are most protection 
cases, with repor ts of gender -based violence (GBV) found in 4.1% of 
households. Health vulnerabilities affect 8.5% of households due to chronic 
medical issues. The demographic data underscores reliance on humanitarian 
aid and the necessity for protection -sensitive programs to address the needs of displaced women and elderly caregivers 
while mitigating social exclusion risks. 
URGENT NEEDS AND MODALITIES 
To address the needs of at-risk displaced individuals, the Protection Cluster recommends the temporary implementation of 
a Minimum Service Package  (MSP) that includes low complexity case management, cash for protection, individual 
protection assistance, psycho-social support, and protection risk monitoring. It emphasizes enhancing these services with 
tailored actions like family tracing, reunification, and establishing safe spaces for Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs) and Women 
and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFSs). During the initial deployment, the cluster advises targeting at least 30% of the displaced 
population for MSP and monitori ng referral mechanisms for potential scaling. It also highlights the necessity for robust 
clinical services for women, including 24/7 access to Post -Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) and emergency contraception. 
Strengthening protection desks and implementing mobile outreach teams, along with improved protection assessments and 
increased trained case workers, are crucial. Resources such as Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) and cash for 
protection should support vulnerable households, especially those led by w omen, using assessment data to guide 
programming decisions. 
MINIMUM SERVICE PACKAGE FOR LIFE-SAVING PROTECTION RESPONSE 
People in imminent danger or who have recently faced a traumatic event require swift intervention by protection service 
providers to safeguard their safety and facilitate recovery. Case management is a critical ongoing effort that interrupts 
violence and guides clients in improving their circumstances. Conversely, cash for protection offers immediate, one-off aid 
to address acute safety risks, differing from multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) that focuses on broader needs. Individual 
Protection Assistance (IPA) combines essential non-food items and protective information to meet urgent needs, especially 
crucial for displaced populations facing explosive hazards. Psychosocial support services (PSS) provide vital mental health 
interventions to help individual s cope with trauma. Protection actors must assess risks swiftly to facilitate access to 
necessary services, utilizing referral methods when specific services are unavailable. Mine action land release processes 
are vital for ensuring safe land utilization and enabling humanitarian aid delivery. Family tracing and reunification (FTR) are 
critical for restoring connections among separated family members, particularly for children at risk. Where safe spaces for 
protection services are lacking, temporary safe sp aces for women and children are established to deliver psychosocial 
support, protection assistance, and vital information, while also facilitating referrals for specific needs. These safe space s 
play a crucial role in delivering specialized protection services, even if they are not lifesaving in themselves.
