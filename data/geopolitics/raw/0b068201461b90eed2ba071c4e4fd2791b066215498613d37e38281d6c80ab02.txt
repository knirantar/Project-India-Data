    NATIONALITIES 
In The Gambia, tensions between farmers and herders – particularly in the Central River Region South (CRR-S), Central River Region North (CRR-N), North Bank Region (NBR), and Lower River Region (LRR) – have 
intensified due to climate change, environmental degradation, and socio-economic pressures. Reduced rainfall and desertification have increased competition over land and water resource, placing both agricultural and 
pastoral livelihoods under stress. 
Transhumance, the seasonal movement of livestock in search of pasture and water, remains a key livelihood strategy. However, it is increasingly challenged by resource scarcity and cross-border movements, mainly 
between. The Gambia and Senegal, which place additional pressure on host communities. 
The International Organization for Migration (IOM), through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), has developed the Transhumance Tracking Tool (TTT). The TTT monitors transhumance movements through a 
network of trained enumerators and key informants, collecting data on livestock flows, routes, and related risks. This approach aims to strengthen the capacity of national and local stakeholders to better understand 
transhumance dynamics, support evidence-based decision-making, and contribute to conflict prevention and early warning mechanisms.
This report presents findings from data collected between December 2025 and March 2026 across ten Flow Monitoring Points (FMPs) in CRR-S, CRR-N, NBR, and LRR. Data collection was conducted through direct 
observation and interviews by DTM enumerators, with financial support from the United Nations Peacebuilding Fund (PBF).
Transhumance movements observed during the reporting period follow seasonal patterns, with herders – primarily from Senegal as well as within The Gambia – moving during the dry season in search of water and 
pasture. These movements are often strategically coordinated, with family members traveling ahead to assess security conditions before herds are relocated.
During the reporting period, a total of 2,222 herders were recorded across the four regions, with the highest numbers observed in CRR-N and CRR-S. The majority of herders (90%) were Senegalese nationals, while 10 
per cent were Gambian. Similarly, 90 per cent of recorded livestock movements were cross-border, highlighting the strong regional dimension of transhumance in The Gambia.

In-country movements represented 9 per cent (5,289 animals), indicating that a notable share of 
transhumance takes place within The Gambia. The most frequently recorded route was from 
Sinchu Chedoyel to Jareng (413 animals). Other common routes included Sambang to Karantaba 
(400 animals) and Sukuta to Wassu (338 animals), pointing to regular livestock movements across 
districts within the Central River Region (North and South).
These patterns suggest that pastoralist routes are shaped by seasonal conditions and the 
availability of pasture and water. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of established 
transhumance corridors in The Gambia and the long-standing reliance of pastoral communities 
on predictable movement pathways.
Cross-border transhumance within the Senegambia region is a significant herding practice. Over the four-month 
period from December 2025 to March 2026, a total of 52,436 animals and 1,960 herders were recorded moving 
between Senegal and The Gambia.
These movements represented 88 per cent of all tracked animals and 88 per cent of all tracked herders. The flow 
was strongly directional: 76 animals and six herders moved from The Gambia to Senegal (1% of tracked animals 
and less than 1% of tracked herders), while 52,360 animals and 1,954 herders moved from Senegal to The 
Gambia (88% of tracked animals and 87% of tracked herders).
The high volume of movements from Senegal into The Gambia suggests that herders are entering The Gambia to 
access seasonal resources, including available pasture and water points. By contrast, outward movements from 
The Gambia to Senegal were minimal during the reporting period.

Evolution of Observed Flows
Observed herd departures show a clear monthly pattern, increasing significantly from 276 animals in June 2025 to a peak of 30,087 animals in January 2026, before declining over the following two months to 3,923 animals 
in March 2026. This trend suggests a strong seasonal movement pattern, with departures rising steadily before reaching their highest level at the start of 2026.
This pattern reflects a sharp rise to the January 2026 peak followed by a decline. The latest monthly flow was 61 per cent lower than the previous month, confirming the recent slowdown in departures.
Projected returns suggest that most herds are expected to return after more than two months (over 8 weeks), representing 43 per cent of animals. The second-largest category is “does not know,” accounting for 38 per 
cent of animals, which indicates a high level of uncertainty around return timing. Among the known shorter return periods, the largest share is expected between 6 and 8 weeks (around 2 months), covering 13 per cent of 
animals. Overall, this suggests that herd returns are likely to be concentrated over a longer timeframe, while uncertainty remains significant for a large proportion of movements.

Methodology
Data were collected through direct interviews, field observations, and key informant interviews 
conducted by DTM enumerators, and triangulated with information obtained from a network of 
informants, including community members.
Following the transhumance baseline and infrastructure mapping, movements were recorded and 
monitored across 10 designated transhumance flow monitoring points: 3 in North Bank Region, 2 in 
Central River Region-North (CRR-N), 4 in Central River Region  South (CRR-South) and 1 in LRR.
The Data collected included transhumant herders’ country and region of departures, intended 
destinations, date of departures, estimated date of arrival, herd species, and the number of animals 
recorded at each monitoring point..
The data were analyzed to quantify transhumance movements and to identify key trends, patterns, and 
findings related to seasonal herd mobility in the region
The results were subsequently compiled into this report to provide evidence-based analysis of 
transhumance movements and return patterns.
