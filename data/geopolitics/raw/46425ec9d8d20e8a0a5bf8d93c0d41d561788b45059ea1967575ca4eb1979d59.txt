FOOD INSECURITY / ACUTE MALNUTRITION
Eastern Africa remains one of the world's most severe humanitarian hotspots, with 48.5 million people in need 
of assistance in 2026. Conflict, displacement, climate shocks, disease outbreaks, and food insecurity continue 
to drive needs, while the potential cross-border spread of Ebola from the DRC poses an additional regional risk. 
Food insecurity worsened during the second quarter , with 40.5 million people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or 
worse levels of food insecurity, 1.6 million more than in the first quarter. The largest increases were reported in 
South Sudan, Kenya, Sudan, and Tanzania. Although the number of acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 
months declined from 9 million to 8.3 million, malnutrition levels remain critically high.  
Internal displacement fell slightly from 16.9 million to 16.7 million people , largely due to returns in Sudan. 
However, nearly 13,000 people were newly displaced in Burundi, mostly as a result of climate-related hazards. 
The regional refugee population increased from 5.9 million to 6 million, driven mainly by increases in South 
Sudan, Uganda, and Kenya.
Public health risks remain significant. Cholera cases more than doubled from 5,500 to about 12,400 in the 
second quarter, while 457 mpox cases were reported, primarily in displacement-affected and flood-prone 
areas. Although the Ebola outbreak remains concentrated in the DRC, the risk of regional spread remains 
high.
The projected 2026 El Niño is expected to worsen humanitarian needs. Below-average rainfall and extreme 
heat through September may be followed by flooding during the October–December rainy season, threaten-
ing livelihoods, food production, water supplies, infrastructure, and essential services.
At the same time, restrictive operating environments and the crisis in the Middle East are compounding 
humanitarian needs across the region. Disruptions associated with the Middle East crisis are driving up fuel, 
transport, and food costs; disrupting supply chains; and placing additional strain on already fragile econo-
mies and food systems. Despite these challenges, humanitarian partners continue to deliver life-saving 
assistance amid severe funding shortfalls, with humanitarian appeals only 21–33 per cent funded by 
mid-year.
