Situation Report 
Chad Humanitarian Response 
1-31 May 2026
Highlights 
● Refugee arrivals from Sudan slow amid border insecurity: By 30 May 2026, a total of
928,818 refugees had arrived from Sudan since April 2023, with Ouaddaï hosting the largest
number.1 Arrivals declined in May, likely due to increased border insecurity, including a 26
May drone strike in Al-Tina.2
● Insecurity and funding shortfalls increase protection risks in Lac: In Lac Province,
non-state armed group (NSAG) attacks on 4 and 6 May triggered new displacements and a
state of emergency. Funding gaps have also forced humanitarian actors to scale back
services, limiting access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and protection services. A
security audit across 25 sites revealed that 96% of sites lacked night lighting and 68%
lacked latrines, heightening gender-based violence (GBV) risks.
● Intercommunal tensions restrict access to essential services in Wadi Fira: Tensions
between the Zaghawa and Tama communities persist in Wadi-Fira Province, with clashes in
Birak and Marassabré resulting in casualties. The violence undermines social cohesion,
restricts population movement, and hinders access to SRH and GBV services.
● Senior UNFPA leadership visits Chad: A high-level UNFPA mission visited N’Djamena,
Ouaddaï, and Wadi-Fira to engage with government authorities, assess humanitarian needs,
convene donor meetings, and review ongoing interventions.
● 90% funding gap: As of May 2026, UNFPA has only received 10% of funding it requires for
Chad, severely restricting life-saving SRH and GBV services for vulnerable women and girls.

Case management: UNFPA provided comprehensive psychosocial support and medical care to 
survivors through partners and specialized centres. All survivors received psychosocial support, with 
further medical and legal assistance provided as needed. 
Capacity building: In Ouaddaï province, UNFPA’s implementing partner, CIAUD, trained 45 service 
providers (including 17 women) across the medical, psychosocial, legal, and security sectors on GBV 
case management.  
Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS): During the month of May, UNFPA maintained services in 
11 WGSSs across Ouaddaï and Wadi-Fira provinces through its two implementing partners, CIAUD 
and AFJT. Twenty (20) additional safe spaces in Ouaddaï are managed by other organizations under 
tents provided by UNFPA.

Funding Status 
As of the end of May 2026, UNFPA 
has secured US $1.9 million 
toward its $18.7 million 
requirement. Of the funding 
received, $500,000 comes 
directly from UNFPA Emergency 
Funds and $1.4 million comprises 
of funds rolled over from 2025 
from the Women's Peace and 
Humanitarian Fund and the 
UNFPA Humanitarian Thematic 
Fund. This leaves a staggering 
funding gap of 90%.  
UNFPA is urgently appealing for 
critical funding for Chad to 
address the scale and urgency of the 
humanitarian needs, particularly for 
women and girls who require life-saving 
SRH and protection services.
