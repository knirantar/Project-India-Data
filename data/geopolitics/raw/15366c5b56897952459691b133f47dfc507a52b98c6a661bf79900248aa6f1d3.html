Coast Guard Monitors Chinese Vessels in the Arctic

July 20, 2026 | By U.S. Coast Guard |
×

The Coast Guard recently monitored two Chinese research ships as they transited north through the U.S. exclusive economic zone and over the U.S. extended continental shelf in the Bering Sea.
On Watch
A crew member assigned to the Coast Guard cutter Munro observes the oil tanker Bella 1 in the North Atlantic Ocean, Jan. 6, 2026. The Munro's crew recently monitored Chinese vessels transiting through the U.S. exclusive economic zone and over the U.S. extended continental shelf in the Bering Sea.

The Chinese research ships Xue Long and Xue Long 2 transited the Arctic, marking the first time this year that Chinese vessels were tracked transiting this area. In response, the Coast Guard cutter Munro executed Operation Frontier Sentinel to actively monitor the vessels during their northbound transit. 

The operation was designed to protect American sovereign rights, ensure consistency with international law and position the Coast Guard to rapidly respond to any detected malign activity. 

While foreign vessels are permitted to operate within the U.S. exclusive economic zone and over the U.S. extended continental shelf, the Coast Guard remains vigilant to ensure their activities comply with established international laws. In particular, conducting marine scientific research within this area would require advance permission from the United States and subsequent sharing of the information collected.
On the Move
The Chinese research ship Xue Long transits north through the U.S. exclusive economic zone and over the U.S. extended continental shelf in the Bering Sea, July 12, 2026. In recent years, the Coast Guard has observed heightened activity in the U.S. Arctic and anticipates a continued and increased presence in the region this summer.

In recent years, the Coast Guard has observed heightened activity in the U.S. Arctic and anticipates a continued and increased presence in the region this summer. 

"Alongside the Department of War, the Department of State, and our international partners, we are actively responding to competitor activities across an increasingly contested Arctic," said Rear Adm. Bob Little, U.S. Coast Guard Arctic District commander.
