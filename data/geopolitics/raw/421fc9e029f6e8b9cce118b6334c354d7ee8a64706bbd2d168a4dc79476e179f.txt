SITUATION OVERVIEW 
• The Dominican Republic, like other Small Island 
Developing States in the Caribbean, is highly exposed to 
severe weather events such as hurricanes, flooding, and 
droughts, which disrupt livelihoods, damage 
infrastructure, and strain food systems. Ecosystem 
degradation further reduces natural protection against 
extreme events. Around 25 percent of households are 
highly vulnerable to climate shocks, disproportionately 
affecting poor and hazard‑prone communities. 
Territorial inequalities leave rural and border provinces 
with fewer opportunities and higher poverty rates, while 
53.4 percent of employment is informal. Global food 
price volatility, dependence on remittances, and 
localized migration pressures compound these risks, 
intersecting with persistent micronutrient deficiencies 
and the double burden of malnutrition 
disproportionately affecting women, children, and 
excluded groups. These vulnerabilities are exacerbated 
by global market instability linked to the Middle East 
crisis, driving food price volatility, energy cost increases, 
and supply chain disruption, raising concern for 
national food security and household resilience.  
• WFP’s strategy links national planning with local action 
to address recurrent shocks systemically. Evidence and 
data guide interventions, building government capacity 
so central decisions translate into community 
protection. Climate services and risk management tools 
are embedded in social protection, enabling 
anticipatory action. Pre‑positioning, disaster risk finance 
ensure rapid response when systems are strained. 
Platforms such as ANACAONA and the DR–Haiti corridor 
exemplify collective resilience.

OPERATIONAL UPDATES 
Emergency response 
• WFP conducted a targeted household assessment in 
the provinces of Valverde and Monte Cristi following 
the floods that affected northwestern Dominican 
Republic in late April, in coordination with Plan 
International and Civil Defense. 1,253 households 
were surveyed to identify those most affected and 
vulnerable, using a prioritization methodology that 
considered socio-economic vulnerability, household 
composition, exposure to flood impacts, livelihood 
losses, social exclusion and the presence of 
individuals with specific needs. Based on this 
assessment, 1,001 households were selected to 
receive cash assistance, ensuring assistance reaches 
those facing greatest needs.  
Adaptative Social Protection 
• WFP, through the USG-funded Cuenca Resiliente 
project and in partnership with INTEC University and 
UNICEF, launched the first Diploma in Adaptive 
Social Protection in the Dominican Republic to 
strengthen national capacities through education 
and collaboration with academia. The programme 
provides specialized training on adaptive social 
protection, disaster risk management, and 
anticipatory action, fostering a new generation of 
professionals equipped to address climate-related 
risks. By institutionalizing this knowledge within 
national institutions and universities, the initiative is 
expected to contribute to more resilient and 
proactive social protection systems. 
• WFP convened and supported a High-Level Meeting 
of the Adaptive Social Protection Technical Working 
Group (Mesa PSA), bringing together the Social 
Policy Cabinet (GCPS), SIUBEN, INDOMET, Civil 
Defense, Supérate, UNDP and other key partners to 
strengthen coordination on climate risk 
preparedness. Participants discussed the potential 
impacts of a forecasted moderate-to-strong El Niño 
event, including drought risks and pressure on 
agriculture and water resources, while noting that 
despite an expected below-average hurricane 
season, the country remains vulnerable to high-
impact storms and flooding. The meeting promoted 
the use of climate information and anticipatory 
action approaches, and highlighted the launch of 
the Dominican Republic’s first parametric insurance 
scheme for social protection. By strengthening 
coordination among institutions responsible for 
social protection, climate services and disaster risk 
management, the Mesa PSA is helping to align 
preparedness efforts and support more timely, 
coordinated and anticipatory responses to climate-
related shocks. 
Disaster Risk Management and climate 
change 
• WFP, in partnership with the Ministry of 
Environment and Natural Resources, officially 
launched the Dominican Republic’s participation in 
the third cohort of the Adaptation Fund Climate 
Innovation Accelerator (AFCIA), WFP’s global climate 
innovation incubator designed to identify, test and 
scale innovative solutions to climate adaptation 
challenges. WFP engaged government institutions, 
UN agencies, NGOs, foundations and development 
partners in a collaborative process to identify 
priority challenges requiring innovative approaches, 
with a particular focus on food security, disaster risk 
management and adaptive social protection. By 
connecting national stakeholders with AFCIA’s 
innovation support platform, the initiative is 
expected to accelerate the development and scaling 
of locally driven solutions that strengthen resilience, 
support climate adaptation and enhance the 
capacity of vulnerable communities to cope with 
climate-related risks. 
• WFP and the Dominican Institute of Meteorology 
(INDOMET) delivered a hands-on technical 
workshop to strengthen institutional capacities in 
the operational use of ANACAONA, a 
hydrometeorological monitoring and risk analysis 
platform that supports anticipatory action and 
evidence-based decision-making. The training 
brought together forecasting, research and regional 
center specialists from INDOMET to enhance their 
skills in the interpretation of meteorological 
products, georeferencing of monitoring stations, 
generation of precipitation maps, and data 
exchange and validation processes. The workshop 
strengthened collaboration between WFP and 
INDOMET, identified priority improvements to the 
platform. These efforts are expected to improve the 
quality and accessibility of hydrometeorological 
information, supporting more timely risk analysis, 
early warning and anticipatory action to reduce the 
impacts of climate-related hazards on vulnerable 
populations.

Nutrition 
• WFP supported the launch of the Simplified Course 
on Monitoring Fortified Foods, jointly organized with 
the Ministry of Public Health and the Food 
Fortification Technical Committee, comprising the 
General Directorate of Medicines, Food and Health 
Products (DIGEMAPS), the Technical Secretariat for 
Food and Nutrition Security (SETESSAN), the Ministry 
of Public Health (MISPAS), WFP, and the Institute of 
Nutrition of Central America and Panama (INCAP). 
The course will focus on strengthening national 
capacities to monitor fortified rice and wheat flour, 
two key vehicles for the delivery of essential 
micronutrients, while enhancing quality assurance 
mechanisms across the national food fortification 
programme. By equipping government institutions 
with the knowledge and tools required to effectively 
oversee compliance with fortification standards, the 
initiative is expected to improve the effectiveness of 
food fortification efforts and contribute to better 
nutrition outcomes for vulnerable populations. 
• WFP participated in the “Más Salud” Health Fair 
organized by the Ministry of Public Health in 
Hermanas Mirabal Province, promoting healthy 
diets and positive nutrition practices as part of its 
Social and Behaviour Change (SBC) strategy. 
Through the interactive storytelling activity “Julie and 
the Rainbow of Foods,” presented by the character 
Calife, WFP engaged children and caregivers in 
learning about dietary diversity, nutritious food 
choices and healthy eating habits. By using an 
engaging and family-centered approach to nutrition 
education, the initiative is expected to reinforce key 
nutrition messages, encourage the adoption of 
healthier dietary practices, and contribute to 
improved nutrition outcomes among vulnerable 
communities over the long term. 
• WFP conducted community outreach sessions in 
Bajo Yuna as part of the implementation of 
nutrition-sensitive interventions under its 
Anticipatory Action Strategy, promoting food 
security, healthy diets and preparedness measures 
among vulnerable communities. WFP engaged 
community members in discussions on nutrition 
practices and actions that can help reduce the 
impacts of climate-related shocks on food security 
and nutritional well-being. By integrating nutrition 
into anticipatory action programming, the initiative 
is helping communities strengthen their 
preparedness for future shocks, while promoting 
healthier dietary practices and protecting vulnerable 
households from the adverse effects of climate-
related disruptions. 
• WFP participated in the national commemoration of 
World Food Safety Day alongside the General 
Directorate of Medicines, Food and Health Products 
(DIGEMAPS), reaffirming its commitment to 
strengthening food safety systems and supporting 
national institutions responsible for ensuring the 
availability of safe and nutritious food. The event 
highlighted the importance of robust food safety 
standards, quality control and regulatory oversight 
as essential components of food security and 
nutrition, while recognizing the efforts of national 
institutions to protect consumers and ensure access 
to safe and nutritious foods.
