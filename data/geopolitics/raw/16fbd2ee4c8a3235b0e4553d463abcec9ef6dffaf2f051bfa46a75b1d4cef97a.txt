IMPLEMENTATION OF PROJECT ACTIVITIES 
 
Meteorological situation  
The synoptic process that causes abnormal heat in Central Asia is called a summer thermal depression. 
This process is the formation of persistent high -pressure ridges (anticyclones) that block the 
movement of air masses, impede cloud formation, and cause warm air to descend, leading to severe 
overheating of the ground layer. According to long -term observations, the duration of a summer 
thermal depression in Central Asia ranges from 2-3 to 5-7 days, and in some years can last up to 1.5-2 
weeks. In recent decades, global warming has been influencing the situation —the overall increase in 
average global temperature leads to more frequent and intense heat waves, which are becoming 
longer and more extreme. As in other areas of Central Asia, this process in Kyrgyzstan is characterized 
by the development of a high-altitude ridge, which intensifies the flow of hot tropical air from Iran and 
Afghanistan into Kyrgyzstan through Uzbekistan.

Heatwave event in July 2025 
Aero-synoptic maps from 14 –15 July, 2025 showed that a high -altitude baric and thermal ridge had 
spread to southwest Asia in the troposphere. Analysis of 10 -day forecast products from Global 
Forecasting Centres (ECMWF-9 km, GFS -22 km, and ICON -12 km) showe d that an air mass from the 
south and southwest will arrive in Kyrgyzstan on 19 July 2025, causing a prolonged heatwave, especially 
in the southern regions. More detailed analysis of actual information and forecast products (Figs 1 and 
2) showed that the daily maximum temperature in the southern regions of Kyrgyzstan will reach trigger 
levels. It was therefore decided to activate EAP No. 2 for a heatwave in the southern part of Kyrgyzstan.

OPERATIONAL STRATEGY 
SUMMARY OF EAP IMPLEMENTATION 
Host National Society 
Background and Context 
In July 2025, the Kyrgyz Republic faced a forecasted period of abnormally high temperatures posing 
significant health and protection risks to vulnerable populations, particularly children, older persons, 
persons with disabilities, and low-income families. In response to early warning information provided 
by the national hydrometeorological service, the Red Crescent Society of the Kyrgyz Republic (RCSK) 
activated its Early Action Protocol on Heatwave (EAP) in line with its anticipatory action framework. 
The activation aimed to reduce heat-related morbidity and mortality through early, coordinated, and 
targeted humanitarian action. This report outlines the actions taken, coordination mechanisms, 
coverage, challenges encountered, and key outcomes of the activation. 
Trigger and Decision to Activate 
On 15 July 2025, the RCSK received official forecast data indicating a sustained increase in temperature 
to abnormal levels. According to the forecast, temperatures were expected to exceed established 
trigger thresholds between 20 and 24 July 2025, fulfilling the activation criteria defined in the EAP. 
Based on this information, the decision was made to activate the protocol. Official activation letters 
were issued by the President of the RCSK and disseminated to: 
• Movement partners (IFRC and ICRC), 
• Regional branch directors, 
• All government institutions identified in the protocol as key actors (MoES, MLSC). 
Immediately following activation, operational coordination and field-level preparations were initiated. 
Geographic Scope of the Activation 
The activation targeted three regions identified as being at highest risk: 
• Osh Oblast (Kara-Suu and Uzgen region) 
• Jalal-Abad Oblast (Suzak region) 
• Batken Oblast (Leilek and Kadamjai region) 
These regions were selected based on forecasted temperature intensity, population density, and 
vulnerability indicators.

On 16 July, the Red Crescent Society initiated rapid operational preparations, including: 
• Informing suppliers of the urgent need for food and hygiene kits; 
• Dispatching pre-positioned psychosocial support (PSS) kits, umbrellas, information materials, 
and awareness stands from the central warehouse from Bishkek; 
• Engaging suppliers of air-conditioning equipment for installation in social institutions. 
Most coordination and planning were conducted in online and hybrid formats to ensure rapid 
mobilization across regions. 
Formation of Field Teams 
In the regional branches of Osh, Jalal -Abad, and Batken, multiple operational teams were formed, 
consisting of staff and volunteers: 
• Information and Awareness Teams – responsible for public awareness sessions in crowded 
public spaces such as parks, squares, theatres, and community centers. 
• Verification Teams – responsible for beneficiary verification and coordination with social 
protection authorities. 
• Logistics and Technical Teams – responsible for packaging kits, transporting items, and 
installing equipment.
10 
A total of 172 volunteers were engaged in information dissemination activities alone. 
Beneficiary Identification and Verification 
Following activation, official requests were sent to the Ministry of Labour and Social Care for lists of 
vulnerable households registered in the national social protection system. Priority categories included: 
• Families with three or more children under 16; 
• Families with children with disabilities; 
• Households where one or both caregivers are persons with disabilities. 
Verification teams conducted: 
• Phone-based verification from regional offices; 
• Follow-up through social workers where beneficiaries were unreachable; 
• Final eligibility checks against protocol criteria. 
Verified beneficiaries were then invited to scheduled distribution points.

Following the completion of distributions conducted between 15 and 20 July 2025, the RCSK carried 
out an Exit Survey and  Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise to assess the relevance, quality, 
accountability, and impact of the assistance provided. Exit survey was conducted during distribution. 
The monitoring was conducted between 4 and 14 August 2025 across all three targeted regions.

Adequacy and Duration of Assistance 
367 respondents were asked how long the assistance lasted or was expected to last: 
• 2–2.5 weeks: 59.95% of respondents 
• Less than 2 weeks: 32.43% of respondents 
• Up to 1 month: remaining respondents 
Overall, the majority considered the assistance adequate for short-term household support during the 
heatwave period. 3 
 
Recipients responded very positively to the food, hygiene, and PSS kits, as well as to the umbrellas, 
fans, seating areas, and tents which were established during the distribution, highlighting their 
usefulness, quality, and the significant comfort and protection they offered during the extreme heat 
period.

CHALLENGES AND LESSONS LEARNED  
Distribution Strategy and Safety Measures 
Due to extreme heat conditions, distribution modalities were adapted to reduce health risks: 
• Distribution to beneficiaries was conducted in the evening, when temperatures were lower;
16 
• Packaging and preparatory work took place early in the morning, ending by 11:00 a.m.; 
• Activities were paused during peak heat hours (11:00–15:00). 
Specific arrangements included: 
• In Jalal-Abad, distributions were organized in shaded park areas in cooperation with municipal 
authorities; 
• In Batken, distributions were held in selected social institutions with controlled entry and exit 
flows; 
• Across all locations, tents, benches, water coolers, solar -powered fans, and fans  with fogging 
system were installed to ensure safety and comfort. 
 
Transport and Logistics Restrictions 
Due to extreme heat, temporary restrictions were imposed on major national roads, particularly 
affecting heavy vehicles during daytime hours. 
This led to: 
• inability to use large transport trucks; 
• need to rebook smaller vehicles at short notice; 
• delays in delivery schedules; 
 
Human Resource Constraints 
The activation coincided with the summer period, during which: 
• many experienced staff members were engaged in other operations; 
• the National Disaster Response Team could only be partially activated; 
• additional personnel had to be mobilized from headquarters. 
 
In some cases, staff had to take on multiple roles beyond their standard responsibilities. 
 
Summary of revisions to the operation 
In the Early Action Protocol for Heatwave, approved on 18 July 2023, the following actions were initially 
planned: 
• Provision of food, hygiene, and child -focused psychosocial support (PSS) kits for 2,500 
households (estimated 12,500 people). 
• Provision of PSS kits for children under 16 living in social institutions — residential care homes 
and children with disabilities (approximately 700 children in residential institutions for orphans 
and 1,300 children living at orphanages and boarding schools for children with disabilities). 
• Provision of air conditioners to social institutions, including residential institutions for orphans, 
rehabilitation centres for children with disabilities, and care homes for elderly people (45 units, 
reaching an estimated 700 orphans, 1,300 children with disabilities, and 1,700 elderly persons). 
 
Following the introduction of the new IFRC -DREF Procedures, including for Early Action Protocols, 
several key revisions were made: 
• The number of activations allowed under an EAP increased from one to two, enabling two 
separate early action responses within the five year lifespan of the EAP. This effectively doubles 
the potential reach and scale of the intervention. 
• As part of the readiness phase, additional stocks will be procured to ensure adequate supplies 
for a second activation. 
 
As a result of these changes, the overall budget has been revised from CHF 499 151 to CHF 786 520. 
The adjustments include:
