Highlights 
 
▪ Sudan: Armed clashes and near -daily drone strikes continued across Darfur and Kordofan States, 
affecting multiple towns including Dilling, Kadugli, Bara, El Rahad, and Abu Zabad. In North Kordofan, 
commercial vehicles and telecommunications infrastructure sustain ed damage in Tendalti village. 
Road insecurity along key routes including the Um Rawaba –El Rahad corridor  is increasingly 
disrupting humanitarian access and supply lines in Central Darfur. In late February, an attack on 
Misteriya in Kebkabiya locality (Nor th Darfur) triggered significant new displacement of over 3,000 
people within Kebkabiya locality and caused damage to residential buildings. 
 
▪ Central African Republic (CAR):  On 20 February, a high -level government delegation led by the 
Ministers of Humanitarian Action and Territorial Administration visited Birao to strengthen cooperation 
with humanitarian partners and reaffirm the Government's commitment to durable solutions for forcibly 
displaced people and host communities. The delegation engaged with host communities, Sudanese 
refugees, and I nternally Displaced Persons (IDPs to discuss challenges related to reception, 
protection, and access to basic services amid continued a rrivals from Sudan and visited UNHCR -
supported infrastructure , including a health post, water points, and livelihood projects.  The visit 
underscored the need to support local authorities in developing inclusive local development plans that 
strengthen resilience and foster sustainable refugee integration. Identified by the Government as a 
development hub, Birao will require strong er partnerships with development actors and predictable, 
multi-year funding to ensure a comprehensive and sustainable response for  both refugees and host 
communities. 
 
▪ Chad: On 23 February, the Chadian government announced the complete closure of its border with 
Sudan in response to repeated and reported armed incursions into Chadian territory, citing the need

interview conducted by Ann Curry. A press release featuring Kristin Davis was also published and is 
available to read here. 
 
▪ Uganda: The security situation in Kiryandongo Settlement, home to the largest population of 
Sudanese refugees, continued to deteriorate, driven by economic hardship and underlying ethnic 
tensions, with UNHCR and partners scaling up community -based protection, conflict mitigation 
initiatives, and livelihood support to ease tensions and enhance social cohesion.

Situation and Operational Response 
 
SUDAN 
 
Highlights 
▪ The Chad–Sudan border crossing at Tina (North Darfur) was closed during the reporting period 
due to heightened tensions in the border area. The Adre crossing in West Darfur remains open 
for humanitarian convoys, staff, and fuel; however, bureaucratic imped iments are causing delays 
that are undermining the timely delivery of life-saving assistance to vulnerable populations. 
▪ In Khartoum and Northern States, the security situation remained generally calm, though the 
operational environment is increasingly complex. A significant influx of returnees is placing 
pressure on already strained basic services, and deteriorating economi c conditions continue to 
heighten protection risks for affected populations. 
▪ White Nile State remains broadly stable with no major disruptions to humanitarian operations. 
However, unconfirmed drone strikes reportedly targeted military facilities west of Kosti, and a 
growing influx of newly displaced persons from the Kordofan States  is adding pressure on 
available services.  In Blue Nile State, armed clashes were reported across multiple locations, 
resulting in damage to a school and residential homes. Civilian displacement was recorded, with 
some movements observed toward the Ethiopian border. 
▪ In Kassala, tensions escalated on 12 February at the West Airport gathering site, where a 
landowner and associates destroyed shelters, confiscated water containers, and fired gunshots 
into the air. UNHCR appealed to authorities to ensure that any site mana gement decisions  
including planned closures , adhere to inter -cluster guidance and protect affected households. 
Following the incident, 83 households dispersed within Kassala town, while 28 relocated to the Al 
Karama (Onion Factory) gathering site. Further site closures are anticipated by April. 
 
Population Movements and Registration 
In February, approximately 2,000 refugees and asylum -seekers primarily from South Sudan, Eritrea, and 
Ethiopia arrived in Sudan. They are currently hosted across White Nile, Blue Nile, Kassala, and Gedaref 
States.

By end of February, authorities reported that over 30,000 Sudanese nationals had entered Sudan through 
the Argeen and Eshkeet crossing points in Northern State. 
Protection 
The North Darfur protection hotline received nearly 70 calls from IDPs and refugees seeking information 
on food and health service availability, as well as raising concerns about insecurity. Women at Shangil 
Tobaya IDP camp reported incidents of sexual violence and movement restrictions. 
 
In South and East Darfur, protection teams conducted close to 200 key informant interviews across IDP 
camps and refugee settlements, identifying gaps in health, WASH, education, and cash assistance. 
Findings were shared with partners to inform response efforts. 
 
UNHCR trained 54 staff (15 men/39 women) from the Nubia Health Organization , a community-based 
health provider on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in Northern State. The session 
covered SEA definitions, core PSEA principles, reporting obligations, and survivor -centered support, with 
active participation from clinical, technical, and support staff. 
 
UNHCR and the Commission of Refugees (COR) continued delivering protection services to refugees and 
asylum-seekers in Gedaref camps, providing counselling to 120 refugees on documentation, movement, 
return, resettlement, and food assistance. 
 
Child Protection 
In East Darfur, UNHCR and partners visited over 100 unaccompanied and separated children and their 
foster families across El Ferdous and Kario camps, identifying needs related to shelter conditions and 
education access. Three group psychosocial support and  recreational sessions were held across both 
sites, reaching 320 refugee children through structured games and sports activities to support emotional 
wellbeing. 
 
In Kassala State, UNHCR partners delivered assistance and activities for children at the Wad Sharife 
reception centre and child-friendly spaces in Shagarab, Wad Sharife, and Kilo 26 refugee camps, reaching 
approximately 570 children through support service s, structured activities, and case management 
interventions. 
 
One at-risk refugee child in Babikri camp, in Gedaref State, received medical care and counselling and 
was safely reunited with their family. 
 
GBV Prevention and Response 
A GBV awareness session was conducted at the Altawidat multipurpose community centre (MPCC) in 
Sharq Alnil locality, Khartoum State, reaching approximately 150 participants. The session aimed to 
strengthen community understanding of GBV and the role of fam ilies in supporting and protecting 
survivors. 
 
In North Darfur, a UNHCR partner documented 15 GBV cases, with most incidents occurring along the 
Tawila–El Fasher road as women and girls travelled to check on their homes. Additional cases were 
recorded in Sortoni IDP camp and Al Lait refugee settlements . All survivors were referred to for medical 
care and received psychosocial support. 
 
In East Darfur, 31 GBV survivors were identified across Kario camp and El Ferdous refugee settlement. 
In Gereida IDP camp, South Darfur, five survivors received psychosocial support and/or were referred to 
for medical treatment. 
 
Four GBV incidents were reported across refugee camps in Aj Jabalain and As Salam localities , in White 
Nile State. Survivors received health, medical, psychosocial, and in-kind assistance. 
 
Partners conducted seven awareness and dialogue sessions on bullying, trafficking, and child marriage in 
Wad Sharife and Shagarab refugee camps, reaching over 100 participants , the majority of whom were 
women and adolescent girls in Kassala State.

Community-based protection 
Multi-purpose community centres (MPCC) 
In February, over 9,000 IDPs and refugees accessed protection services and activities through MPCCs 
across North, East, South, and Central Darfur States. Services delivered by UNHCR, partners, and other 
providers included GBV awareness sessions, recreation al activities, counselling, psychosocial support, 
referrals, and community-led initiatives. 
 
In Red Sea State, more than 125 forcibly displaced people received counselling at MPCCs in Port Sudan, 
with referrals provided for cash assistance, food support, and mental health and psychosocial services. In 
White Nile State, over 700 refugees participat ed in psychosocial support sessions and community 
protection activities across Dabat Bosin and Kor Al Waral refugee camps. 
 
Community protection and capacity-building sessions reached more than 350 displaced persons, covering 
psychological support, life skills, conflict mediation, and community-based dispute resolution in Khartoum 
and Port Sudan. 
 
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) 
In Khartoum, approximately 40 IDPs and host community members participated in two psychosocial 
support awareness sessions at the Marabie Al Shreef MPCC in Sharg Alneel locality, focusing on 
Psychological First Aid and the role of families in reducing psychological distress. 
 
Community-based Protection Networks (CBPNs) 
In Kassala State, a UNHCR partner reestablished CBPNs in Shagarab and Wad Sharife refugee camps 
and provided refresher training to members on identifying protection concerns and safe reporting and 
referral pathways. Throughout the month, CBPNs delivered over 20 awareness-raising sessions reaching 
more than 200 participants and identified 21 protection cases , 11 in Wad Sharife and 10 in Shagarab , 
involving elderly persons at risk, individuals with disabilities or serious medical conditions, and SGBV 
survivors, all of whom were referred for specialized assistance. 
 
In South Darfur, a UNHCR partner conducted two refresher training courses for 30 CBPN members in 
Gereida and Buram, covering CBPN roles, reporting, GBV case management, and referral pathways.  
 
Health and Nutrition 
In Gedaref State, health facilities served nearly 8,000 outpatients, six of whom required emergency care 
at Gedaref Teaching Hospital. Nutrition screening identified over 50 children under five with acute 
malnutrition, including three severe cases, and 10 pregnant and lactating women were flagged as being 
at nutritional risk. 
 
Across White Nile and Blue Nile refugee camps, health facilities delivered approximately 31,000 outpatient 
consultations, with around 67% serving refugees and asylum -seekers and the remainder serving host 
community members and IDPs. The most common illnesses recorded were respiratory infections, malaria, 
acute diarrhoea, and skin infections. Mental health consultations totalled approximately 300 in White Nile 
State and 29 in Blue Nile State during the month. 
 
In Gedaref and Kassala States, UNHCR rolled out the Global Distribution Tool (GDT) to strengthen 
biometric verification and accountability in WFP's cash -based food distribution process, covering the 
January–February 2026 cycle. 
 
Shelter and non-food items (NFIs) 
UNHCR partners distributed NFI kits to displaced families across East and North Darfur States, reaching 
1,000 families in Khazan Jadeed town and Sha'ria locality (East Darfur), and 2,500 families in Melit, Saraf 
Omra, and Kutum (North Darfur).  
 
A further 600 NFI kits were distributed to IDP households and newly arrived at the Goz Al Salam site. Kits 
across all distributions comprised blankets, mosquito nets, solar lamps, plastic sheets, kitchen sets, 
sleeping mats, and jerry cans. 
 
In Gedaref State, 13 refugee households received core relief items, and 24 households received 
emergency shelter kits consisting of plastic sheeting and basic construction materials to reinforce or

establish temporary shelters. This assistance addressed immediate household and shelter needs, helping 
to improve living conditions for the families reached. 
 
Water, Health, and Sanitation (WASH) 
Refugees and asylum -seekers in Gedaref and Kassala States received an average of 25 litres of water 
per person per day in refugee camps, exceeding the minimum standard for daily water provision.  
 
In Kassala State, soap rations were distributed to over 150 newly arrived refugees and asylum-seekers at 
the Wad Sharife reception centre, and personal hygiene kits were provided to 22 women and girls of 
reproductive age. In Red Sea State, UNHCR supplied drinking water to the Alsadaga gathering site in Port 
Sudan, which hosts approximately 167 households. 
 
Partners ensured access to safe drinking water for over 500,000 refugees and host community members 
in White Nile and Blue Nile States. A one -year supply of water treatment chemicals was delivered to all 
refugee camps in both states to sustain water qualit y and reduce public health risks. In Blue Nile, 1.5 km 
of internal distribution pipelines were upgraded to improve water pressure and reduce queuing, and 50 
latrines were desludged, restoring functionality and improving hygiene conditions for approximately 2,500 
refugees. 
 
Livelihoods 
In Port Sudan, 30 women from IDP and host community households completed practical bakery training 
at the Alwihda MPCC, delivered by a UNHCR partner with support from the Alwihda MPCC Women's 
Association. The training aimed to enhance vocational skills and strengthen participants' self-reliance. 
 
In White Nile State, ten Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) were established across Um 
Sangour and Khor Al Waral refugee camps to strengthen financial inclusion, promote savings and access 
to small loans, and support income-generating activities to enhance refugees’ self -reliance, enrolling 200 
participants (184 women/16 men). Each group received toolkits to support savings, loan management, 
and financial record-keeping. Complementary livelihood and food security training reached refugees and 
host community members across both camps: 50 people were trained in fish -catching and sustainable 
fishery management, 50 in home gardening, and 40 in food drying and preservation techniques, equipping 
participants with practical skills to strengthen household food security and diversify income sources.  
 
 
CENTRAL AFRICAN REPUBLIC (CAR)  
 
Highlights 
▪ Despite relative calm in Birao, the security situation across Vakaga , north-east of CAR remained 
volatile. Reported incidents, including violence involving security forces on the Sikikédé –
Mangagne road and intercommunal clashes — reflected a persistently worrying protection 
environment. Civilians remained highly exposed to physical assault, c ompounded by the 
widespread availability of bladed weapons and insufficient community conflict -resolution 
mechanisms. 
▪ In Korsi, Birao, UNHCR and the Government conducted registration for 100 Sudanese refugees 
from 30 families originating from Nyala and El Fasher, Sudan, during the last two weeks of 
February. With fighting in Sudan ongoing and road conditions between Birao  and the border 
improving following the end of the rainy season, additional arrivals are anticipated. 
Population Movement and Registration  
As of 28 February, CAR hosted nearly 42,000 Sudanese refugees from approximately 13,100 households, 
an 11 per cent decrease compared to the 47,000 individuals registered as of 15 December 2025. This 
decline is primarily attributable to a physical verification exercise conducted jointly by the Government and 
UNHCR in Korsi, following observations of spont aneous departures  and prolonged absences over the 
preceding period. 
 
Protection  
UNHCR and its partner Comité International pour l'Aide d'Urgence et le Développement  (CIAUD) 
continued protection monitoring in Korsi and surrounding host communities. A joint multisectoral 
assessment mission was conducted in Am-Dafock, encompassing field visits and community consultations 
across the villages of Matala, Amkourmaye, Dongoré, Am-Dafock, and Am-Sissiya.
7 
 
 
These activities enabled the documentation of 63 protection incidents. Violations of property rights  
particularly extortion and illegal taxation were the most prevalent, followed by physical assaults and one 
incident involving loss of life. Most incidents were attributed to non -state armed groups, underscoring the 
continuing insecurity faced by both refug ees and host communities. Geographically, incidents were 
concentrated in Birao, Ouanda -Djallé, Am -Dafock, and Ouandja, reflecting persistent protection risks 
across key areas of Vakaga prefecture. Adult men constituted the majority of victims, largely due to their 
heightened exposure while seeking livelihoods. 
 
In response, UNHCR and partners strengthened monitoring, referral, and advocacy efforts, while 
reiterating the need to reinforce GBV and child protection services and further support community -based 
protection structures to enhance prevention and response capacity. 
 
GBV prevention and response 
UNHCR and CIAUD continued GBV prevention and response activities across Vakaga prefecture. 
Reported incidents of violence against women and girls included physical assault, psychological violence, 
and denial of resources, with many occurring in domestic se ttings. All survivors received psychosocial 
support in safe spaces. 
 
CIAUD also conducted community -based awareness -raising sessions on the causes, harmful norms, 
risks, and reporting mechanisms related to GBV. Strengthening the meaningful engagement of men and 
boys remains critical to fostering a more inclusive and sustain able community approach to preventing 
sexual violence. 
 
Accountability to the affected population (AAP)  
UNHCR and CIAUD continued to strengthen AAP in Korsi through ongoing feedback collection, referrals, 
awareness-raising, and complaint follow -up. Feedback received in February was primarily related to 
WASH and shelter services. All concerns were systematically referred to relevant actors and monitored to 
facilitate timely and appropriate responses. 
 
Two hygiene awareness sessions were facilitated by community focal points and protection monitors in 
Korsi, reaching 66 participants (17 men, 21 women, 13 boys, 15 girls). The diverse participation enabled 
inclusive discussions on individual, household, and community hygiene practices, strengthening 
participants' understanding of the links between personal hygiene, environmental cleanliness, and the 
prevention of waterborne and infectious diseases. 
 
Child protection  
UNHCR and partners continued child protection activities in Korsi, focusing on children's rights, 
psychosocial well-being, and social cohesion. Three mobile Child -Friendly Space sessions were held in 
the Korsi neighbourhood of Birao, providing 135 refugee children (81 boys, 54 girls) with a safe 
environment supporting their psychosocial well-being and protection. 
 
The participation gap between boys and girls highlights the need to strengthen community outreach and 
address sociocultural barriers and domestic responsibilities that may limit girls' engagement, with a view 
to achieving more balanced participation. 
 
Education 
UNHCR and its partner the Centre de Support en Santé Internationale (CSSI), in collaboration with the 
Academic Inspectorate of Vakaga prefecture, continued supporting the integration of refugees into the 
national education system. French language courses w ere provided to Sudanese refugee students to 
prepare them for the Central African education system, where French is the medium of instruction.  
Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) 
Construction of semi -durable shelters for people with specific needs is ongoing in Korsi. To date, 150 
shelters have been completed, providing vulnerable households with safer and more dignified living 
conditions.

Health and Nutrition 
Reproductive health services continued in Korsi, with 318 women receiving family planning, gynecological, 
prenatal, and postnatal consultations. Twenty -five births were recorded during the period, all assisted by 
qualified birth attendants at the hospital. 
 
Nutrition screening was conducted for 600 children aged 6 –59 months, identifying 68 cases of moderate 
acute malnutrition (MAM) and 27 cases of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), all of whom received 
treatment. 
 
 
CHAD 
Highlights 
▪ With armed clashes intensifying across Darfur and Kordofan  characterized by ground fighting, 
artillery shelling, drone strikes, criminal activity, and intercommunal violence , and rising tensions 
near the Tina crossing along the Chad –Sudan border following the border closure, UNDSS 
recommended the temporary suspension of all UNSMS movements to Tiné, which was lifted on 2 
March 2026 once security conditions were deemed conducive for humanitarian operations.  
Activities at the Guichet Unique in Tiné, eastern Chad, where new arrivals receive initial screening, 
health pre -screening, protection assessments, and basic assistance were also suspended. 
Despite this, Sudanese refugees continued to arrive through unofficial entry points, accessing the 
transit centre located approximately 5 kilometers from the border, where registration, medical 
screening, light NFI assistance, and support for persons with specific needs were provided to the 
extent possible. Following processing, refugees were relocated from the transit ce ntre to safer 
sites inland. 
▪ Growing insecurity along the border and inland prompted a high -level mission by the Chadian 
Defense and Security Forces (FDS), led by the Minister of Armed Forces, to eastern Chad , 
specifically the Ouaddaï region in February 2026. As part of this operation, the FDS initiated 
systematic household weapons searches across Eastern Chad. UNHCR and humanitarian 
partners engaged with authorities to define clear operational modalities for a ny searches 
conducted within refugee sites, ensuring compliance with protect ion standards, humanitarian 
principles, and the safety of refugees and staff.  
Population Movements and Registration 
Refugee arrivals into Chad continued to rise, with most newly arrived refugees entering through the Oure 
Cassoni border in Ennedi East (51 per cent), followed by Tiné in Wadi Fira (33 per cent), Adré/Adikong in 
Ouaddaï (12 per cent), and Sila (4 per cent). 
 
Despite the official border closure, Sudanese individuals continue to cross into Chad. Monitoring teams 
report a stable but declining trend in arrivals through the Wadi Fira, Ennedi East, and Ouaddaï entry points, 
where protection partners maintain a presence to monitor, assist, and direct new arrivals. 
 
In February, 4,704 new Sudanese arrivals were recorded in eastern Chad , an 80 per cent decrease 
compared to the 8,444 arrivals in January. The decline is likely attributable to heightened insecurity along 
the Sudan–Chad border, marked by armed clashes and drone strikes that are forcing refugees onto longer, 
unofficial, and mor e dangerous routes. More than 85 per cent of new arrivals are women and children, 
and approximately 13 per cent are individuals with specific needs. 
 
The cumulative number of registered refugees since the El -Fasher attack in late October 2025 is now 
35,590 individuals (13,292 families).  
 
While most recent arrivals linked to the El -Fasher crisis had previously been recorded in Ennedi East, 
Wadi Fira, and Ouaddaï, a new influx was reported in February in Ademour, Sila. A joint mission 
comprising the Commission Nationale d'Accueil de Réinsertion des Réfugiés et des Rapatriées (CNARR), 
the Red Cross of Chad (CRT), and UNHCR conducted on 20 February confirmed this population 
movement. Authorities estimate approximately 1,30 0 new arrivals, mainly women and children from 
various parts of Darfur are currently concentrated at the Ademour entry point or dispersed within Adé.

Relocation  
In February, UNHCR supported the relocation of 7,464 refugees, a 48 per cent increase compared to the 
5,046 individuals relocated in January 2026. Movements focused on transferring refugees from insecure 
border areas to new, former, and expanded sites in e astern Chad, where shelter, water, and sanitation 
services, aim to ensure dignified living conditions. 
 
The sharp deterioration of security along the Wadi Fira border prompted UNHCR and partners to expedite 
the relocation of 4,232 refugees to the Touloum site extension. Additional households were relocated to 
Oure Cassoni (Ennedi East) and the new sites of D ougui and Kouchaguine -Moura (Ouaddaï), with the 
dual aim of reducing exposure to cross-border threats and ensuring access to essential services. 
 
Since April 2023, UNHCR and partners, with the support of the Government of Chad, have relocated 
613,636 refugees from border entry points to safer inland sites, significantly contributing to improved 
protection and service delivery for newly arrived populations. 
 
Protection 
GBV prevention and response    
In February, reported GBV cases comprised physical assault (33 per cent), emotional violence (33 per 
cent), rape (16 per cent), denial of resources (11 per cent), forced marriage (4 pe cent), and sexual assault 
(3 per cent). Survivors received multisectora l support including psychosocial counselling, case 
management, and referrals to health, legal, security, and justice services. Most alleged perpetrators were 
unknown to survivors, with many incidents occurring during firewood collection, a daily activity t hat 
exposes women and girls to isolated, under -secured areas. Additional cases of sexual and physical 
violence were reported during flight from Sudan, particularly from areas controlled by non -state armed 
groups, while other perpetrators included intimate partners and family members. 
 
Prevention efforts intensified across sites and host communities, with partners conducting 34 mass 
awareness sessions reaching 5,132 people (3,336 women, 565 men, 821 girls, 410 boys). Capacity -
building activities included training 19 service providers in Iriba on the clinical management of rape, 60 
volunteers across Farchana, Arkoum, and Aboutengué on GBV concepts, 12 peer educators, and the 
engagement of 54 Engaging Men in Accountable Practices ( EMAP) participants on prevention roles and 
challenges. 
 
As a risk mitigation measure, ecological charcoal was distributed to 1,400 households in Oure-Cassoni in 
the presence of local authorities, CNARR, UNHCR, and partners RESI Chad and NIRVANA. Each 
household received six 10-kg charcoal bags, one improved stove, and one cooking bag, aimed at reducing 
GBV risks linked to firewood collection. 
 
GBV Sub -Working Groups in Ouaddaï, Wadi Fira, and Ennedi East held six coordination meetings 
throughout February, ensuring consistent information -sharing and harmonized approaches among 
partners. Key operational challenges persist ed, including the absence of dedicated workspaces or 
listening centers in newly established reception sites, insufficient latrines, inadequate lighting, and a lack 
of alternatives to firewood, all of which heighten GBV risks for women and girls. 
 
Child Protection 
In February, child protection partners continued identifying unaccompanied and separated children, as 
well as other children at risk, across all refugee reception sites in eastern Chad. In Iriba, a meeting with 
judicial authorities on 12 February initiated  discussions on appointing a focal point to the Détachement 
Intégré de Sécurité (DIS) panel, with the aim of better integrating refugee children into national systems 
and strengthening the management of children in conflict with the law. Additional consult ations focused 
on preventing child abandonment and infanticide in Wadi Fira, and on establishing an early -warning 
mechanism. 
 
Key achievements during the month included the following: 286 children (130 girls, 156 boys) were 
registered with the civil registry, securing legal identity and access to essential services; 724 children at 
risk received multisectoral support ranging from health and protection assistance to foster placement and 
school reintegration; protection teams conducted 126 Best Interest Assessments (BIAs) and completed 4 
Best Interest Determinations (BIDs); social and recreational activities reached 19,268 children;  134

children received material assistance including NFIs, and clothing; and community volunteers sensitized 
5,016 people on child protection, hygiene, and positive parenting. 
 
Capacity-building efforts were reinforced through two targeted trainings.  UNHCR and Jesuit Refugee 
Service (JRS) trained 18 caseworkers in Ouaddaï on Child Protection Case Management and the Best 
Interests Procedure. A second training held in Iriba from 23 to 26 February brought together 20 participants 
from JRS, INTERSOS, World Vision, Humanity & Inclusion, CRT, NIRVANA, and the Ministry of Women 
and Early Childhood. Both sessions aimed to strengthen child protection standards and procedures, 
improve documentation, enhance consistency of care, and reinforce referral pathways among partners.  
 
Health and Nutrition 
In February, the health response in eastern Chad continued to rely on strong coordination between 
humanitarian partners, UN agencies, and provincial health authorities. Seventeen partners worked 
alongside UNFPA, WHO, UNICEF, and WFP to support refugee site s. Health facilities recorded 79,046 
curative consultations, with the leading morbidities being acute respiratory infections, malaria, and 
diarrheal diseases. Maternal health services registered 2,709 live births and 2,478 assisted deliveries, 
while 53 hom e births  mainly in Gozamir, Arkoum, and Marassabré , prompted ongoing community 
sensitization efforts. 
 
Vaccination and nutrition services showed both progress and constraints. A total of 6,690 children were 
vaccinated against measles (11,359 since January), representing a 30 per cent increase driven partly by 
the arrival of new refugees. Nutrition programs admitted 3,617 children aged 6–59 months, including 1,625 
with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 1,992 with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) , an 8 per cent 
decrease from January, while persistent supply shortages for MAM continued in Wadi Fira, Ennedi E ast, 
and Ouaddaï. Mental health and psychosocial support services assisted 1,272 patients, primarily 
presenting with epilepsy, chronic psychosis, depression, and trauma -related disorders. An additional 400 
patients were referred to secondary care, with tra uma-related surgical needs , 56 per cent linked to stab 
injuries, representing most referrals. 
 
Disease surveillance intensified as measles alerts increased. In Sila province, 257 cases were recorded, 
including 34 among refugees and two deaths in host communities, prompting plans for a mass vaccination 
campaign in Koukou district. In Adré, 66 suspect ed cases were reported, including 11 confirmed. 
Coordination structures remained active across Ouaddaï, Wadi Fira, and Sila, with progress made on 
digitalizing the Who is Where, When, doing What (4Ws) mapping tool for mental health services. Partners 
provided critical support across several areas, from UNFPA's distribution of caesarean kits to WFP's six -
month partnership agreements and NGO contributions for maternal care, mental health, and upcoming 
vaccination campaigns. 
 
Persistent operational gaps include insufficient human resources, limited funding, nutrition supply ruptures, 
weak logistics infrastructure including ambulances, shortages of mental health specialists, and inadequate 
laboratory and reproductive health supp lies, underscoring the need for sustained investment in staffing, 
financing, and infrastructure. 
 
Cash-based Interventions (CBI) 
In February, UNHCR assisted 317 individuals through education and shelter interventions.

dedicated to learner registration and enrolment across both camps, with classroom instruction 
commencing the following week. In line with guidance from the Ministry o f General Education and 
Instruction (MoGEI), all schools registered both continuing and new learners simultaneously, ensuring 
coordinated access to education for refugee children. 
 
Health  
UNHCR and the Maban County Health Department convened the first monthly health, nutrition, and WASH 
coordination meeting at the UNHCR office. Discussions focused on partner updates and aligning activities 
with the Maban 2026–2028 strategy to strengthen local healthcare and WASH syst ems for refugees and 
host communities. The meeting will be held monthly as a key platform for enhanced coordination and 
responsive service delivery within South Sudan's complex health landscape. 
 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) 
In Maban, Upper Nile State, UNHCR WASH partners ensured water delivery across all four camps during 
the reporting period. Daily supply levels stood at 9 litres per person per day (l/p/d) in Doro, 13 l/p/d in 
Gendrassa, 10 l/p/d in Yusuf Batil, and 12 l/p/d in Kaya. While these levels remain below the Sphere 
standards, supply ensured basic access for drinking, cooking, and hygiene. Through community -driven 
sanitation initiatives, 134 new household latrines were constructed across the four camps, enhancing 
sanitation conditions and empowering communities to take an active role in hygiene practices.  
 
In Jamjang, Pariang County, UNHCR, partners, and refugee community leaders completed a general soap 
distribution across the camps from 24 –27 February, reaching 17,386 households (94,777 beneficiaries). 
Refugees expressed appreciation for the assistance, particularly noting the relief  for women and 
schoolgirls following months without soap distribution. 
 
In Pamir, Pariang County, UNHCR and Action Africa Help International (AAH -I) WASH teams initiated 
construction of 100 emergency communal latrines at blocks 61 and 62 to ensure safe sanitation access 
for new arrivals, following delivery of essential logs and poles by the local authority supplier. In addition, 
two shared latrines for persons w ith specific needs (PSNs), supported by the Korea International 
Cooperation Agency ( KOICA), were constructed in the same blocks to serve the most vulnerable new 
arrivals. 
 
Shelter and Core Relief Items (CRIs) 
In Aweil, a total of 38 households (190 individuals) received full CRI kits comprising blankets, sleeping 
mats, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, buckets, collapsible jerrycans, and solar lamps, including 20 households 
(98 individuals: 37 men and 61 women) supp orted by UNHCR. In addition, 112 households (587 
individuals) received sleeping mats, with 57 households (300 individuals) covered to address stock 
shortages from January and 55 households (287 individuals) receiving complementary assistance.  
 
UNHCR and partners provided emergency shelter and CRIs to new arrivals from Yida transit and Pamir 
reception centres through multiple distributions. A total of 532 households (1,439 individuals) received 501 
emergency shelters and family tents, alongside f ive transitional shelters for persons with specific needs, 
supporting 11 highly vulnerable individuals with improved protection and dignified accommodation.  
 
Settlement planning activities supported the allocation of 525 shelter plots, including 141 re -demarcated 
plots and the maintenance of 4 k ilometers of access roads linking Jamjang town to Ajuong Thok. CRI 
distributions further reached 730 households (2,157 individuals) with essential items, including sleeping 
mats, blankets, mosquito nets, jerrycans, buckets, and soap. 
 
Cash Assistance 
UNHCR in Maban has begun updating the CBI beneficiary lists in preparation for upcoming livelihood and 
community empowerment cash distributions targeting 1,344 households. In parallel, UNHCR Camp 
Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM)  in Maban supported Humanity & Inclusion to deliver

Livelihoods 
UNHCR Maban (Bunj Sub -Office) completed the surveying and mapping of 4,050 acres of agricultural 
land across the four refugee camps in Maban, comprising 2,050 acres in Doro, 600 acres in Batil, 600 
acres in Gendrassa, and 800 acres in Kaya. The surveyed areas are now ready for the next phase, which 
will include plot demarcation, boundary marking, and the preparation of internal road layouts to support 
access, land management, and organized cultivation. 
 
UNHCR Maban initiated community consultations focused on establishing transparent and equitable 
criteria for plot allocation ahead of the upcoming farming season. Engagements with camp leadership and 
community representatives emphasized inclusive decision -making. Agricultural land will be managed 
through a block farming system coordinated by community Sheikhs and Umdas to promote organized 
cultivation and shared resource management. 
 
UNHCR, the Commission for Refugee Affairs (CRA), refugee leaders, refugee traders, and host 
community representatives, participated in the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) -led consultation to 
introduce three proposed livelihood projects in Wedweil Settlement: bread baking, oil production, and bar 
soap production. The consultation resulted in agreement to name the bakery “Wedweil Bakery,” designate 
the Women’s Centre for bread and oil production, and allocate the Youth Centre for soap production. NRC 
will provide production materials for the initial two-week period, construct dedicated facilities (four-partition 
concrete structures at the Women’s Centre and iron -sheet structures at the Youth Centre), and deliver 
training to recruited staff. The projects will o perate under refugee leadership through a seven -member 
Bakery Management Committee, supported by an operational team overseeing day -to-day activities. 
Implementation will commence upon completion of facility construction by NRC.  
 
In partnership with Relief International, UNHCR Maban launched a call for Expressions of Interest to 
expand livelihood opportunities targeting at least 4,000 refugee households. The initiative prioritizes 
business development, agricultural production, and climate-smart agriculture, with a commitment to 
ensuring that women comprise at least 60% of beneficiaries. The programme aims to strengthen 
household resilience and promote sustainable income-generating opportunities across Maban. 
 
 
 
 
UGANDA 
Highlights 
▪ As of 28 February, Uganda hosted a total of 93,383 Sudanese nationals, predominantly originating 
from Khartoum (42,270), South Darfur (14,048), North Darfur (11,330), South Kordofan (4,537), 
and Al Jazirah (1,573).  
▪ Kiryandongo refugee settlement accommodated the largest share of this population, hosting 
78,957 Sudanese nationals. To note, u niversity-level education was the most reported  
qualification among the registered population, with 24,934 individuals holding a degree.  
Population Movement and Registration  
In February 2026, Uganda registered 261 Sudanese individuals, comprising 172 refugees and 89 asylum-
seekers, the majority of whom were women and children. 
 
Protection  
Refugee Status Determination (RSD) 
On 5 February 2026, the Government of Uganda issued a directive lifting prima facie refugee recognition 
for new asylum-seekers from Sudan, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and South Sudan, transitioning 
to individual RSD procedures. The Government clarified that the decision is not intended to alter Uganda's 
open-door policy, and that individuals previously granted refugee status will retain their protection in 
accordance with national law and Uganda's international obligations. 
 
UNHCR was formally notified of the directive and continues to engage closely with the Office of the Prime 
Minister (OPM) to support its implementation in line with Uganda's legal framework and protection 
commitments. Asylum -seekers are now registered, issued asylum -seeker certificates, and relocated to 
settlements to access basic services pending the outcome of their individual status determination.
21 
 
Voluntary Return 
On 20 February, the Embassy of Sudan in Uganda facilitated the repatriation of 128 Sudanese nationals 
to Khartoum. The group comprised 125 refugees and three members of the Higher Committee of Voluntary 
Return, with the flight prioritizing individuals with chronic medical conditions. 
 
Legal and physical protection (LPP) 
Peace mentors convened to review emerging conflict trends in Kiryandongo settlement, identifying key 
drivers including parental neglect, ethnic tensions, high adolescent school dropout rates, reports of covert 
youth violence training, and increasing land -related disputes. Participants emphasized the need for 
structured follow-up actions to strengthen community-level conflict mitigation mechanisms. 
 
Child Protection 
During a monitoring visit to a children's remand facility in Kiryandongo, more than 10 children in conflict 
with the law were identified, with cases including one murder and ten aggravated robbery charges. Four 
boys were reported to have escaped from the f acility, and their whereabouts remain unknown. 
Assessments for identified cases have commenced to inform case management plans aimed at supporting 
their wellbeing during remand and facilitating future reintegration. 
 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) 
In Kiryandongo, functional handwashing coverage stood at 41.5 per cent across a refugee population of 
165,909, reflecting ongoing challenges in access to soap and water at handwashing points. A total of 20 
open defecation sites were identified across clust ers, highlighting persistent gaps in sanitation practices. 
Addressing these gaps will require strengthened follow -up, sustained community sensitization, and the 
provision of supportive materials such as slabs and latrine digging tools to improve sanitation  uptake.
