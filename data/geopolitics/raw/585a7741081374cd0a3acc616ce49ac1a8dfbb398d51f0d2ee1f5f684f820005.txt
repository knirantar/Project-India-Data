Description


According to local goverment, a tropical depression over the East Sea intensified into Typhoon No. 1 (Maysak) on 2 July 2026. As of 08:00 (local time) on 3 July, the typhoon was located approximately 110 km south of Hainan Island (China), moving westwards at around 5 km/h with maximum sustained winds of 62–74 km/h (Force 8) and gusts up to Force 10. The storm is forecast to strengthen as it approaches the Gulf of Tonkin, bringing strong winds, rough seas and heavy rainfall to northern Viet Nam.


Ahead of the storm, prolonged heavy rainfall from 29 June to 2 July caused localised flooding and landslides in Lao Cai Province, disrupting transport on several national highways. Around 34 households remain at risk of landslides, while damage to transport infrastructure is estimated at VND 1.7 billion (approximately USD 66,000). Additional rainfall is forecast, with authorities warning of an increased risk of flash floods and landslides due to saturated soil conditions.


As of 3 July, no deaths, injuries or significant damage have been reported. Authorities continue to monitor the situation, particularly the risk of flash floods, landslides, localised flooding and hazardous sea conditions.

Actions taken by National Society


General


Other


Summary


VNRC Headquarters is closely monitoring the evolving situation in coordination with the Viet Nam Disaster and Dyke Management Authority (VDDMA), provincial Red Cross chapters and local authorities. Provincial chapters in potentially affected areas have been requested to monitor the situation, maintain communication with local authorities, verify potential impacts and ensure readiness to support emergency response if required. VNRC National Disaster Response Teams remain on standby, and further updates will be provided as additional information becomes available.


Actions taken by others


The Ministry of Agriculture and Environment has issued an official directive requesting provincial authorities and relevant agencies to: •Monitor the development of the typhoon and disseminate timely early warnings. •Guide fishing vessels and marine transport to safe shelter. •Prepare evacuation plans for communities in coastal, low-lying and landslide-prone areas, where required. •Inspect reservoirs, dykes, drainage systems and construction sites. •Maintain 24-hour emergency operations and ensure readiness of response personnel and equipment under the "four-on-the-spot" principle. Road clearance operations are ongoing in Lao Cai Province, while local authorities have installed warning signs and are maintaining round-the-clock monitoring in high-risk areas.
