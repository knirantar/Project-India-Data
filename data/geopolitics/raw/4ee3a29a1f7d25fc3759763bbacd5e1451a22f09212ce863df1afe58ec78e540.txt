Data  collected  by  Insecurity  Insight  shows  that  since  08  April  2026  and  12  May  2026,  violence  against  health  care  across  the  occupied  Palestinian  territory  has  continued.  This  includes:  
●  At  least  27 attacks  on  health  care  recorded  during  this  period.  ●  Continued  exposure  of  health  facilities,  health  workers,  and  medical  transport  to  violence  despite  reported  efforts  to  reduce  hostilities.  
These  figures  highlight  the  ongoing  vulnerability  of  health  care  in  contexts  where  international  humanitarian  law  protections  are  not  consistently  upheld.  Since  07  October  2023,  at  least  3,291 attacks  on  health  care  have  been  recorded  across  Gaza  and  the  West  Bank.  The  growing  use  of  drones  has  emerged  as  a  threat  to  health  care  delivery  in  the  occupied  Palestinian  territory,  with  more  than  154 recorded  incidents  affecting  health  workers,  facilities,  transport,  and  service  provision.  The  impact  on  specific  health  services  is  also  particularly  concerning.  At  least  61 maternal  health  services  have  been  damaged,  disrupted,  or  otherwise  affected  by  violence  and  coercive  measures,  limiting  access  to  care  for  pregnant  women,  mothers,  and  newborns.

Other  resources  Insecurity  Insight  has  released  a  report examining  how  the  conflict  between  Iran,  Israel,  Lebanon  and  the  United  States  has  impacted  emergency  medical  services  across  the  Greater  Middle  East  region.   
Key  findings  ●  The  highest  number  of  incidents  affecting  emergency  medical  services  were  recorded  in  Lebanon  ●  Nearly  three  quarters  of  incidents  were  attributed  to  Israeli  forces  ●  Sequential  strikes  on  emergency  responders  were  documented  in  Iran,  Iraq  and  Lebanon.  ●  Emergency  responders  in  southern  Lebanon  reported  delays  and  heightened  risks  during  rescue  operations  
Key  take  aways  ●  Medical  care  is  protected  in  conflict  ●  Emergency  responders  are  particularly  vulnerable  in  conflict  ●  Attacks  on  emergency  responses  undermine  the  capacity  of  the  health  system  to  support  the  injured     Explosive  weapons  are  being  increasingly  used  in  conflict,  causing  severe  harm  to  civilians  and  critical  health  infrastructure.  Insecurity  Insight  has  documented  3,075  incidents  in  which  hospitals,  clinics,  and  pharmacies  were  damaged  or  destroyed  by  explosive  weapons  between  1  January  2016  and  4  November  2025.  This  report examines  cases  of  hospital  attacks,  analysing  social  media  posts  and  comments  and  how  these  discussions  contributed  to  harmful  content  that  helped  to  undermine  the  humanitarian  space.  For  Gaza,  some  comments  introduced  a  framing  that  weakens  IHL  protection  by  treating  the  issuance  of  a  warning  or  evacuation  order  as  a  form  of  legal  justification  for  military  action,  while  contextualising  the  area  as  a  legitimate  conflict  zone. 
This  document  comprises  threats  and  violence  as  well  as  protests  and  other  events  affecting  the  delivery  of  and  access  to  health  care.  It  is  part  of  the  Attacks  on  Health  Care project  by  Insecurity  Insight.
