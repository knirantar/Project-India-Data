Highlights


▪ Sudan: Armed clashes and near-daily drone strikes continued across Darfur and Kordofan States, affecting multiple towns including Dilling, Kadugli, Bara, El Rahad, and Abu Zabad. In North Kordofan, commercial vehicles and telecommunications infrastructure sustained damage in Tendalti village. Road insecurity along key routes including the Um Rawaba–El Rahad corridor is increasingly disrupting humanitarian access and supply lines in Central Darfur. In late February, an attack on Misteriya in Kebkabiya locality (North Darfur) triggered significant new displacement of over 3,000 people within Kebkabiya locality and caused damage to residential buildings.


▪ Central African Republic (CAR): On 20 February, a high-level government delegation led by the Ministers of Humanitarian Action and Territorial Administration visited Birao to strengthen cooperation with humanitarian partners and reaffirm the Government's commitment to durable solutions for forcibly displaced people and host communities. The delegation engaged with host communities, Sudanese refugees, and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs to discuss challenges related to reception, protection, and access to basic services amid continued arrivals from Sudan and visited UNHCRsupported infrastructure, including a health post, water points, and livelihood projects. The visit underscored the need to support local authorities in developing inclusive local development plans that strengthen resilience and foster sustainable refugee integration. Identified by the Government as a development hub, Birao will require stronger partnerships with development actors and predictable, multi-year funding to ensure a comprehensive and sustainable response for both refugees and host communities.


▪ Chad: On 23 February, the Chadian government announced the complete closure of its border with Sudan in response to repeated and reported armed incursions into Chadian territory, citing the need to prevent the expansion of the conflict, protect the population, and safeguard national integrity. Humanitarian exemptions were granted subject to prior authorization. This decision followed a Rapid Support Forces (RSF) offensive on 22 February against the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) in Tina, Sudan, during which the RSF also attacked a post of the Chadian Defense and Security Forces (FDS) across the border in Tiné, Chad. During the clashes, stray bullets struck the premises of the UNHCR’s "Guichet Unique," which is one-stop service center for refugees, including the meeting room, which was unoccupied at the timeThe closure of the Chad–Sudan border has significant implications for UN agencies and partners, restricting cross-border humanitarian access and supply routes, delaying aid delivery and staff movement, while also limiting refugee inflows, complicating protection monitoring, and increasing pressure on existing sites and already overstretched services in eastern Chad.


▪ Egypt: On 8 February, UNHCR engaged with the Sudanese Embassy to identify areas for strengthened coordination. Discussions with the Sudanese Ambassador covered access to public services, voluntary return initiatives, and ongoing support programmes, including medical assistance delivered with partners and efforts to streamline documentation processes. On 11 and 24 February, in response to issues raised by the Emergency Fund for Refugee Response (EFRR) and the Refugee Advocacy Group in their respective meetings, the UNHCR Representative outlined ongoing efforts to expand registration capacity, including through digital tools such as the MyUNHCR platform and reiterated continued engagement with the Government of Egypt as part of the transition to the new asylum law. Both engagements underscored the importance of improved information-sharing, regular coordination, and timely follow-up for individuals with specific protection needs.


▪ Ethiopia: On 27 February, the UNHCR Representative conducted a high-level mission to Assosa, reviewing protection, service delivery, and solutions programming at the Ura refugee site which currently hosts 14,000 refugees. The site follows an integrated settlement approach, whereby services such as schools and health facilities are designed to serve both refugees and host communities, promoting inclusion, strengthening local systems, and supporting early self-reliance.


▪ Libya: On 11 February, UN agencies and representatives from relevant Libyan government ministries held technical consultations on the Libya chapter of the 2026 Sudan Regional Refugee Response Plan (RRP) at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Tripoli. The meeting fostered an open and collaborative discussion focused on critical issues such as data management, the impact of the Sudan crisis on Libya, and areas for strengthened UN–government collaboration. Following the consultations, UNHCR received formal feedback from the government and the Sudan RRP was virtually launched on 18 February.


▪ South Sudan: UNHCR South Sudan concluded a joint mission featuring Goodwill Ambassador Kristin Davis, High-Profile Supporter Ann Curry and private donor Rachelle Lopp, focused on the Sudan response, with particular attention to the experiences of women and girls. The visit to Renk and to several projects in Juba aimed to raise the visibility of the crisis and included a PBS NewsHour interview conducted by Ann Curry. A press release featuring Kristin Davis was also published and is available to read here.


▪ Uganda: The security situation in Kiryandongo Settlement, home to the largest population of Sudanese refugees, continued to deteriorate, driven by economic hardship and underlying ethnic tensions, with UNHCR and partners scaling up community-based protection, conflict mitigation initiatives, and livelihood support to ease tensions and enhance social cohesion.
