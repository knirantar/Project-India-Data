INTRODUCTION
The Protection Monitoring System (PMS) 
continues to provide timely and systematic 
evidence on protection risks aﬀecting 
displaced populations across Gaza. Through a 
combination of Key Informant Interviews (KII) 
and Focus Group Discussions (FGD), the 
system captures both quantitative trends and 
qualitative insights to inform protection 
analysis and response planning. This 
snapshot presents an overview of protection 
risk severity scores, evolving risk trends since 
the start of monitoring in August 2024, and 
key protection concerns raised by aﬀected 
communities during the most recent 
reporting period.
Methodology
During this reporting period, data collection was conducted through structured interviews with key informants 
representing diverse community groups and sectors, and through facilitated focus group discussions engaging 
displaced populations across multiple locations. A central role in data collection was played by the Emergency 
Protection Responders Network under the umbrella of the Protection Cluster in Gaza, whose trained ﬁeld teams 
carried out the majority of KIIs and FGDs, ensuring timely, community-based data collection under challenging 
ﬁeld conditions. The data collection tools applied were developed based on the Protection Analytical Framework 
(PAF), enabling comprehensive monitoring of protection risks aligned with Global Protection Cluster standards.

Qualitative Analysis
 KEY PROTECTION RISKS IDENTIFIED BY THE       
COMMUNITIES
• Food insecurity and reduced access to food: 
Participants reported increasing diﬃculties in 
accessing food, including bread, following the 
suspension of community kitchens that many 
displaced households had relied upon as a primary 
source of daily meals. Continued price increases, 
rising debt levels due to ﬁnancial hardship, and 
diminishing household resources were reported to 
further limit access to food and other basic needs.
• Limited access to safe drinking and domestic 
water: Participants reported severe shortages of 
safe drinking water and highlighted the burden 
associated with collecting, transporting, and storing 
water for household use.
• Inadequate shelter conditions, overcrowding, and 
lack of privacy: Many tents have deteriorated due 
to prolonged use and exposure to weather 
conditions. Rising temperatures further exposed 
these limitations, with families reporting insuﬃcient 
protection from heat. Overcrowding and lack of 
privacy continued to negatively aﬀect living 
conditions across displacement sites. 
• Sanitation, Environmental Health Hazards, and 
Infestation: Communities reported increasing 
numbers of rodents, insects, and lice within 
displacement sites, linked to inadequate waste 
management, open sewage, and uncovered 
wastewater pits. Deteriorating sanitation conditions, 
combined with rising temperatures, were perceived 
as increasing exposure to disease and undermining 
dignity and safety. Participants expressed particular 
concern regarding the impact on children, older 
persons, persons with disabilities, and individuals 
with pre-existing health conditions.
• Barriers to accessing humanitarian assistance and 
civil documentation: Participants reported 
challenges related to obtaining or renewing 
identiﬁcation documents, as well as concerns 
regarding the non-recognition of newly issued 
documents by some humanitarian actors. 
Diﬃculties associated with digital assistance delivery 
mechanisms, including telecommunications 
disruptions, loss of assistance codes, and 
complications linked to digital wallet systems, were 
also reported.
• Explosive ordnance and war remnants: The 
presence of unexploded ordnance and explosive 
remnants of war (ERW)in and around displacement 
sites continued to raise protection concerns. 
Communities reported receiving awareness 
sessions; however, the proximity of hazardous items 
to populated areas, particularly locations 
frequented by children, remained a source of   
concern. Limited awareness of reporting mechanisms 
and access to survivor assistance services may 
further increase protection risks for aﬀected 
population
•"Yellow Line" Risks and Access Impact: 
Communities located near the so-called "yellow" 
and "orange" lines reported signiﬁcant safety and 
security risks, including killings and injuries 
attributed to Israeli military operations. These 
conditions have also severely hindered 
humanitarian partners' access to aﬀected areas, 
resulting in critical gaps in service delivery for 
residents in these locations.
IMPACTS ON THE POPULATION
 • Psychological distress and reduced sense of 
stability: Participants described increasing levels of 
stress, anxiety, and frustration associated with the 
inability to meet basic household needs, prolonged 
displacement, and uncertainty regarding future 
assistance. Feelings of helplessness and concern 
regarding future living conditions were frequently 
reported.
• Health impacts and increased exposure to 
disease: Poor environmental conditions, limited 
access to safe water, and inadequate sanitation 
services were reported to increase exposure to 
health risks, including skin diseases, insect 
infestations, and other hygiene-related concerns. 
Participants also associated rodent and insect 
infestations with declining health and wellbeing 
within displacement sites.
• Loss of dignity and deteriorating living conditions: 
Overcrowding, lack of privacy, inadequate shelter 
conditions, and challenges accessing suﬃcient 
water for daily use were reported to negatively 
aﬀect dignity and quality of life, particularly during 
periods of high temperatures.
•Impacts on children: Children were described as 
being increasingly aﬀected by food insecurity, 
limited access to nutritious food, reduced 
opportunities for recreation, and inadequate living 
conditions. Caregivers also expressed growing 
concerns regarding children's emotional wellbeing 
and overall development.
• Increased strain on vulnerable groups: Older 
persons, persons with disabilities, and individuals 
with pre-existing health conditions were reported to 
face heightened challenges due to barriers in 
accessing services, deteriorating environmental 
conditions, and increased dependence on 
caregivers and community support.
COPING MECHANISMS
•Reduction of food consumption: Participants 
reported reducing meal frequency and portion sizes in 
response to food shortages, rising prices, and limited 
ﬁnancial resources. Caregivers, particularly women, 
were reported to frequently prioritise children and 
other family members when food was scarce, often 
reducing their own consumption. Children were also 
perceived as being particularly aﬀected due to their 
nutritional needs
• Growing debt and credit purchases: Households 
described purchasing food and other necessities on 
credit, accumulating debt. Food expenditures were 
prioritized over other essential household needs.
• Increased dependence on humanitarian assistance: 
Communities reported increasing reliance on 
humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs, 
particularly following the loss of other food sources 
and livelihood opportunities.
• Reliance on family, neighbours, and community 
support: Participants described depending on 
relatives, neighbours, and community members for 
ﬁnancial assistance, food sharing, information, and 
practical support.
• Resource sharing and support for vulnerable 
households: Families reported sharing available 
resources and supporting vulnerable neighbours to 
help address immediate needs and service gaps.
• Seeking alternative sources of water and basic 
supplies: In some locations, households reported 
spending signiﬁcant time and eﬀort collecting water 
or searching for alternative sources of food and 
essential household items.
  COMMUNITY CAPACITIES
• Communities highlighted the continued role of 
neighbours, extended family members, and 
community networks in supporting vulnerable 
households and helping address urgent needs. 
   They described assisting older persons, persons with 
disabilities, and households facing ﬁnancial, social, or 
mobility-related challenges through practical support 
and referrals. They also reported relying on informal 
communication channels and community networks to 
share information regarding available assistance, 
distributions, and services.
   Despite deteriorating conditions, communities 
continued to demonstrate resilience by mobilising 
available resources, supporting one another, and 
adapting collectively to service gaps and resource 
shortages.
