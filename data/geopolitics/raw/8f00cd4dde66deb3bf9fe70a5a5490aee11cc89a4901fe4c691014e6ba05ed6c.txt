Humanitarian Situation and Scale-Up in Akobo County 
Fragile and volatile situation 
The humanitarian situation in Jonglei State, 
particularly in Akobo County, remains fragile 
and volatile. While relative calm has prevailed 
in recent days, localized violence, persistent 
protection concerns and continued population 
movements are driving humanitarian needs 
and placing additional pressure on already 
overstretched services. The health situation 
remains particularly concerning. Akobo County 
Hospital is currently serving an estimated 
267,000 people, more than five times its 
intended capacity, and faces severe constraints 
in infrastructure, water supply and medical 
equipment. At the same time, Akobo West has 
virtually no functional health services, leaving 
large segments of the population without 
access to basic healthcare. Schools face critical 
teacher shortages, and WASH conditions are 
deteriorating due to the lack of solar power for 
the area’s only water pumping station.
Findings from recent assessments in Chuil in 
Nyirol County, Yuai in Uror County, and Paguir 
in Fangak County in Jonglei State highlight 
widespread distress and significant coping 
challenges. Early and forced marriage has 
emerged as a key concern, alongside notable 
gaps in mental health and psychosocial support 
(MHPSS) and child protection services. In high-
risk areas, communities continue to rely on 
informal coping mechanisms due to limited 
humanitarian coverage. The collapse of local 
markets, driven by conflict and looting, has 
severely constrained access to food supplies.
High-level mission to Akobo Town
On 4 June, the Humanitarian Coordinator in South 
Sudan, Ramanathan Balakrishnan, together with 
the UNHCR Regional Director, Mamadou Dian 
Baldé, conducted a mission to Akobo Town to 
engage local authorities, humanitarian partners, 
communities and UNMISS on the security and 
humanitarian situation. Discussions focused 
on access constraints, protection risks and 
ongoing response efforts for conflict-affected 
populations. The delegation commended 
local authorities for supporting humanitarian 
operations in Akobo and surrounding areas, 
while underscoring the urgent need to ensure 
safe passage and protection of humanitarian 
convoys, particularly along contested road 
routes and the Sobat River, to facilitate timely 
assistance to Akobo East and West. The 
delegation also called for guarantees to prevent 
further destruction and looting of humanitarian 
assets, reportedly carried out mainly by local 
youth. Sustained security assurances will be 
critical to maintaining operations, particularly 
during the rainy season.

In addition to displacement, a significant returnee population 
was reported in Akobo Town and surrounding counties in May, 
many arriving from Tiergol, Ethiopia. Partners are verifying these 
figures. These return movements are largely driven by the dire 
living conditions in refugee-hosting areas. According to IOM/
Displacement Tracking Matrix, the return process is fraught with 
challenges, as many individuals are relocating to regions where 
the South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) have recently 
established control, in search of safety and basic services rather 
than permanent resettlement. Sustained United Nations Mission 
in South Sudan (UNMISS) presence and patrols are critical to 
deter violence, protect civilians and humanitarians, and maintain 
early warning systems in high-risk areas. On 3 June, the Relief 
and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Jonglei State urged 
humanitarian partners to resume operations in Uror, Nyirol and 
Akobo West Counties. Local authorities committed to ensuring 
the safety and security of humanitarian personnel, assets and 
supplies as partners resume operations in areas previously 
affected by violence.
Akobo hospital operates at five times its capacity 
Akobo County Hospital continues to face extreme operational 
pressure. The facility is currently serving an estimated 267,000 
people, nearly five times its intended capacity of approximately 
50,000 people. Critical gaps persist, notably the availability of 
only one functional delivery bed, unreliable water supply, limited 
infrastructure, and a non-functional generator. Akobo hospital 
urgently requires additional medicines, Inter-agency Emergency 
Health Kits (IEHKs), oxygen support, power restoration support, 
cholera preparedness supplies, and surge staffing to sustain the 
rapidly increasing caseload. IEHK supplies remain prepositioned 
in Bor awaiting delivery. UNICEF is providing support to restore 
the hospital’s power supply. Akobo West remains largely without 
functional health services. WHO is working with three partners 
including Medair, CIDO, and MSF to support emergency reopening 
of primary health-care centres.
Scale up of emergency response in Akobo East
As of 4 June, Akobo County is calm, and humanitarian partners 
have rapidly expanded their operations. Nearly 30 humanitarian 
organizations are active in the area. Over the past three weeks, 
more than 60,000 of the targeted 80,000 people in Akobo East—
equivalent to 75 per cent—have received food assistance, including 
over 15,000 people through emergency food distributions. 
Nutrition interventions have supported more than 6,000 women, 
while over 30,000 vulnerable people have received high-energy 
biscuits. Verification and general food distributions targeting 
conflict-affected populations, including internally displaced 
people (IDPs) and returnees, are ongoing.
This scale-up has been made possible through a substantial 
logistical effort. More than 200 metric tons of supplies have been 
delivered through UNMISS-supported convoy operations, while 
over 60 UNHAS flights have transported approximately 430 metric 
tons of humanitarian cargo and more than 200 personnel into the 
area. UNHAS flight frequency has increased to three rotations per 
week. Additional support has included the delivery of 4 metric 
tonnes of medical supplies to Akobo hospital on 27 May and 1.2 
metric tonnes of sexual and reproductive health kits in the past 
week.
Partners are increasingly expanding interventions across sectors, 
including agriculture, fishing, health, nutrition, water, sanitation 
and hygiene (WASH), education, protection and non-food 
assistance. Plans are underway to extend integrated health and 
nutrition services to peripheral facilities in neighbouring payams. 
Schools in Akobo Town have reopened and learner registration is 
ongoing; however, the continued absence of teachers remains a 
major constraint to education provision.
Priority humanitarian actions include sustained support 
for returnees, faster delivery of supplies and increased UN 
Humanitarian Air Service flights to Akobo. However, partners are 
concerned that the delivery window is closing rapidly as the rains 
have already begun and will soon intensify, making road cargo 
movement impossible.
Convoy delivering over 300 metric tonnes of supplies arrives 
in Akobo Town
On 31 May, a 33-truck convoy organized by WFP and the Logistics 
Cluster delivered more than 210 metric tonnes of food and nutrition 
assistance, alongside 100 metric tonnes of relief items. The 
Logistics Cluster in Bor has also begun loading additional relief 
supplies for onward river transport to Akobo via Malakal along the 
Sobat corridor, as road access remains largely impassable due 
to flooding and poor conditions. Key routes, including the Pibor–
Likuangole–Akobo eastern corridor and the Pathai–Ayod route in 
northern Jonglei, are currently inaccessible.
Protection concerns continue to worsen
Protection monitoring reports indicate increasing incidents of child 
abduction, conflict-related sexual violence, rape and recruitment 
into armed groups perpetrated by parties to the conflict. As of 
April, UNICEF had identified more than 500 unaccompanied 
children across Jonglei State, although the actual number is likely 
to be significantly higher.
“As we were fleeing from the fighting and aerial attacks, we 
were ambushed by gangs, and I got separated from my two 
youngest daughters. They are just 9 and 6 years. I didn’t see 
where they went and I didn’t see them harmed. I have had no 
news since, and I don’t know where they are. I am desperate 
to find my girls again”, Yang Chol, mother of 4 children.

Humanitarian access impediments constraints 
aid operations
Humanitarian access across South Sudan remained constrained 
during the reporting period, particularly in Northern Jonglei, Upper 
Nile, Unity, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria. Between 
21 and 28 May, a total of 19 access-related incidents were 
documented, compared with 10 the previous week, reflecting a 
significant increase in humanitarian access constraints and 
operational risks. Cumulatively, 327 humanitarian access 
incidents recorded between January and May 2026, representing 
nearly 74 per cent increase compared to the same period in 2025.
Increased violence against humanitarian personnel
On 25 May, reports indicate that five contractors including  
one foreign national and four South Sudanese nationals were 
allegedly killed during fighting between armed groups in Kendila 
in Morobo County, Central Equatoria State while working on a 
bridge construction project reportedly supported by a UN agency. 
At least 19 fatalities recorded since January, alongside 183 
incidents affecting personnel and humanitarian assets.
On 26 May, two humanitarian staff members conducting a 
humanitarian distribution mission were reportedly abducted by an 
armed group in Yei County, Central Equatoria State. The incident 
occurred after the vehicle transporting the team was stopped by 
the armed actors, who subsequently took the two staff members to 
an unknown location. The abducted staff members were released 
on 27 May. However, both individuals were reportedly subjected 
to severe physical torture during captivity. In a separate incident 
in Yei County on 1 June, two humanitarian staff members were 
abducted while undertaking a field mission and safely released 
after a day. Between 29 May and 1 June 2026, two incidents 
involving the attack and robbery of humanitarian staff were 
also reported in Mundri West County, Western Equatoria State. 
During the same period, UN personnel in Kapoeta South County, 
Eastern Equatoria State were reportedly stopped at gunpoint and 
robbed of their personal belongings. These incidents highlight the 
growing security risks faced by humanitarian actors and are likely 
to constrain humanitarian access, disrupt field operations, and 
increase operational costs.
Humanitarian access restrictions
Pressure on humanitarian organizations operating in opposition-
controlled areas to relocate services to government-controlled 
areas persisted. Authorities in Nasir County reportedly instructed 
humanitarian organizations to relocate Area Managers to Nasir 
town, with a strict deadline of 29 May to confirm compliance with 
headquarters.
Government-appointed local authorities of Longochuk County, 
operating from Mathiang, reportedly denied a humanitarian 
assessment mission access to Udier, where approximately 
22,590 conflict-displaced people are sheltering and are reported 
to be in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. The denial of 
access prevented humanitarian actors from conducting needs 
verification and assessment activities, likely delaying the delivery 
of critical life-saving assistance and services. The restriction 
also affected the distribution of food assistance that had already 
been prepositioned in Mathiang for onward delivery to affected 
populations in Udier, potentially exacerbating humanitarian 
vulnerabilities among displaced households.
Additional incidents included labour authorities in Maban 
County reportedly directing humanitarian organizations to 
deduct 10 percent from casual labourers’ daily wages, looting 
of a humanitarian health facility in Doro Refugee Camp, theft of 
nutrition supplies from an NGO warehouse in Renk, and threats 
by SSPDF personnel against humanitarian staff in the Malakal 
former PoC site  linked to child protection activities supported by 
UN agencies.
In Jonglei State, on 20 May, the SPLA-iO Commissioner in 
Akobo East warned humanitarian actors to vacate Akobo West, 
potentially affecting over 94,000 conflict-affected people and 
halting assistance. In Unity State, increased checkpoints and 
ambushes along key routes, including an attack on a UN agency 
convoy, have heightened risks and further constrain humanitarian 
access. In Eastern Equatoria, interference with humanitarian 
movements has caused operational delays and raised safety 
concerns.
Insecurity and deteriorating road conditions disrupt aid 
operations
In Jonglei State, continued insecurity and deteriorating road 
conditions significantly disrupt humanitarian operations. 
Ongoing clashes between opposition forces and the SSPDF in 
Nyirol and Uror counties disrupted and delayed the delivery of 
critical humanitarian assistance to hundreds of thousands of 
conflict-affected and displaced populations. Violence targeting 
humanitarian personnel and assets also persisted, further 
undermining humanitarian operations. In addition, the onset of 
the rainy season has rendered key supply routes increasingly 
impassable, creating major logistical challenges for the 
movement of humanitarian personnel and relief commodities. 
These constraints are impeding efforts to deliver life-saving 
assistance to vulnerable populations in Northern Jonglei counties 
and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area, where humanitarian 
needs remain acute.

Health risks: disease outbreaks and Ebola 
preparedness
High risk of Ebola importation
South Sudan remains at high risk of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) 
importation following the evolving Bundibugyo Virus Disease 
outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and 
Uganda. Preparedness and readiness activities continue under 
the leadership of the Ministry of Health with support from WHO, 
Health Cluster partners, and humanitarian actors.
Reassessment of national readiness against the WHO checklist 
was conducted on 4 June. Preliminary results indicate the 
improvement in readiness to 45 per cent from the previous 
assessment (29 per cent against the WHO checklist) conducted 
on 18 May; however, further efforts are needed, including Infection 
Prevention and Control (IPC), contact tracing, and surveillance. 
Also, strengthening the sub-national level readiness and 
preparedness remains critical. Fifteen priority counties have been 
identified, including Yei, Morobo, Kajo-Keiji, Tambura, Torit, and 
Juba. All the EVD response pillars are now activated. The Minister 
of Health and a high-level technical delegation from South Sudan 
participated in the High-Level Cross-Border Meeting in Uganda on 
22 and 23 May.
The Ministry of Health has activated daily coordination meetings 
to ensure continuous monitoring, planning and operational 
readiness. WHO and partners continue to support the Ministry 
of Health to strengthen surveillance, IPC, points of entry 
preparedness, case management readiness, and community 
engagement, particularly in high-risk border and movement 
corridors. Despite these efforts, significant gaps remain, 
particularly in water, sanitation, and hygiene services, which 
are critical for both cholera control and Ebola preparedness.  
Government allocates vehicles to support response efforts
On 2 June, the Senior Presidential Envoy for Special Programs, 
Adut Salva Kiir, has donated 10 vehicles to support national 
Ebola preparedness and response efforts in the country. In 
addition, on 26 May, the Ministry of Health announced a 90-
day budget of US$7.3 million to boost preparedness and 
response measures against a potential Ebola outbreak.  
Worsening cholera outbreak
The cholera outbreak continues to worsen. Cholera remains a 
major concern, with more than 104,813 cases reported nationwide 
since 2024. In the last 14 days of reporting (onset from 19 May to 1 
June), 660 new cases and three deaths (CFR: 0.5%) were reported 
in five counties. Most of these cases came from Rubkona (465, 
70.5%), Mayom (131, 19.8%), Renk (40, 6.1%) and Panyijiar (16, 
2.4%). The outbreak is increasingly concentrated in Unity, Lakes 
and Upper Nile states, largely driven by conflict and poor water, 
sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions, particularly among 
displaced, returnee and refugee populations. The conditions in 
Bentiu IDP camp remain particularly worrying given population 
density and persistent WASH challenges. In the past seven days 
(26 May-1 June), a total of 221 new cholera cases and two deaths 
(Case Fatality Rate: 0.9%) were reported across five counties, 
representing a 38.1 per cent decrease compared to the previous 
week (358 cases). Unity State remains the most affected, 
accounting for 34 per cent of all cholera cases, and a recent 
outbreak in the Bentiu IDP camp, home to over 100,000 people. On 
31 May, 62 new admissions were recorded in Bentiu IDP camps, 
with 25 cases at Bentiu State Hospital CTC and 14 at Yaoanyang 
CTC—an increase from 29 May (24, 14, and 9 cases, respectively). 
Additionally, on 31 May, a severely ill and dehydrated patient was 
referred from Mayom County to Bentiu. The patient died upon 
arrival at the Bentiu IDP camp Cholera Treatment Centre. IOM 
is scaling up cholera response in Bentiu IDP Camp. Also, MSF 
complements IOM’s response by providing desludging services, 
solid waste management, and a community clean-up campaign.  
Flood preparedness and early impact
Flooding has already begun affecting vulnerable communities in 
the country. In Koch County, Unity State, rising river water levels 
flooded the area, impacting between 1,800 and 2,000 people, 
destroying over 300 shelters as well as food stocks and basic 
infrastructure. Response capacity remains limited.
While preparedness activities are underway, operational capacity 
remains limited as the country enters the peak rainy season. 
Flooding is expected to further complicate humanitarian access, 
increase disease risks and place additional strain on already 
vulnerable populations and response systems.
Flood preparedness efforts are ongoing through coordination 
between OCHA and the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs 
and Disaster Management. The national Flood Taskforce, 
co-chaired by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission 
(RRC) and OCHA has been activated.  It is supported by the 
National Coordination and Operations Centre (NCOC). The 
2026 flood Mitigation and Response plan is being developed.  
Funding Update
Despite generous contributions from donors, funding levels 
remain insufficient to respond to simultaneous shocks across 
conflict, displacement, food insecurity, flooding, and disease 
outbreaks.The UN Emergency Relief Coordinator, Tom Fletcher, 
announced an allocation of $12 million from the Central 
Emergency Response Fund (CERF) to support people affected by 
conflict-induced displacement and food insecurity in South Sudan 
on 13 May. 
The funding will enable the United Nations and its humanitarian 
partners to deliver life-saving multi-sector assistance, to 
approximately 200,000 people. Assistance will target Akobo 
County in Jonglei State, as well as areas in Upper Nile State and 
other priority locations. 
The South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF) is also preparing a 
complementary allocation addressing food insecurity. Separate 
US$12.99 million CERF allocation approved for UNHAS to sustain 
humanitarian air operations through October 2026.
