REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH SUB-WORKING GROUP
RHSWG 
In Lebanon, the Reproductive Health Sub-Working Group (RHSWG) was established in 2013 under the Health Sector. 
The Ministry of Public Health (MOPH) leads the RHSWG where the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) serves 
as the co-lead. The RHSWG members include national and international non-governmental organizations (NGOs), 
academics, relevant ministries, and UN agencies who coordinate to standardize and improve all aspects of sexual 
and reproductive health (SRH) programming such as community awareness, service quality, accessibility, and 
coverage. The RHSWG develops and follows up on the implementation of a yearly action plan that contributes to the 
objectives laid out in the Lebanese Response Plan (LRP).

●The risk for “out-of-hospital” deliveries is projected to increase with UNHCR’s withdrawal 
of its support to maternal and neonatal services at hospitals as of Dec 1, 2025.

women who are unable to afford the healthcare costs associated with delivery. 
●The RHSWG deployed the “Field Assessment KOBO Questionnaire” in Sep 2025 for use by 
outreach health workers to improve the operational monitoring of 'out-of-hospital' deliveries 
and enhance health monitoring and service planning for mothers and newborns in Lebanon. 
To ensure sustainability, the RHSWG is working with MOPH on integrating the questionnaire 
into the MOPH mobile health application (MERA).
Community Midwifery Intervention Workshop
●In July 2025, the RHSWG facilitated a workshop to discuss how to standardize existing 
community midwifery interventions and empower midwives to deliver essential SRH 
services to vulnerable women at the community level.
●The workshop shed light on ongoing community midwifery interventions following a stock 
taking exercise by the RHSWG.
●Major recommendations for community midwifery interventions included: standardizing 
services, payment, and expanding implementation of health information systems.
●Requirements for advancing midwife-led birthing was recommended as a possible solution 
for decreasing the cost of institutional delivery.

Breast Cancer Awareness Campaign 2025: Free Mammograms Across Lebanon
Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent cancers among women worldwide, and despite its 
seriousness, early detection through timely and regular screening continues to be the most powerful 
tool for improving survival and treatment success. Recognizing this, the Ministry of Public Health 
(MoPH) has launched its annual Breast Cancer Awareness Campaign, offering free mammograms 
at a network of selected hospitals across Lebanon until the end of the year. This nationwide 
initiative aims to motivate women to prioritize early screening, as identifying abnormalities at an 
early stage greatly increases the chances of effective treatment and reduces the risk of 
complications.
Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCCs) are playing an essential role in strengthening the campaign’s 
reach. Across all regions, PHCC teams are actively referring eligible women to participating 
hospitals, conducting awareness sessions, and engaging with communities to explain the 
importance of regular breast exams. Through both educational efforts and hands-on support, these 
centers are ensuring that women—especially those in underserved areas—have access to accurate 
information and essential preventive services.
By covering the cost of mammograms and removing ﬁnancial barriers that often delay early 
diagnosis, the MoPH is reaﬃrming its commitment to women’s health and emphasizing the 
importance of preventive care within Lebanon’s healthcare system. The campaign not only 
promotes early detection but also empowers women to take charge of their health, ensuring that no 
one is deprived of life-saving screening due to economic constraints.

CAMPAIGNS
The health sector in Lebanon continues to deliver equitable quality healthcare services 
targeting vulnerable Lebanese and non-Lebanese individuals including displaced Syrians, 
Palestinian Refugees from Syria (PRS), Palestine Refugees in Lebanon (PRL), migrants, and 
displaced individuals of diverse nationalities. The health sector supports improved access to 
comprehensive primary healthcare which includes sexual and reproductive healthcare among 
other essential health and nutrition services.
Analysis
As of Q3 2025, there is a 19% increase in the total number of subsidized Antenatal Care (ANC) 
consultations provided in 2025 at Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCCs) compared with 2024. 
This reﬂects ongoing health partner efforts to enhance access to maternal healthcare at 
supported PHCCs which helps contribute to decreased maternal and neonatal mortality.
 
Challenges 
Impact of decreased funding and hostilities:
The health sector is still recovering from the devastation inﬂicted by the hostilities with 13 
PHCCs reported as either fully damaged or closed due to damage. 11 of these are in the 
Nabatieh Governorate (mainly Bent Jbeil and Marjeyoun Districts), with the remaining 2 PHCCs 
in Tyre and Baabda Districts. 
Due to funding cuts, there is reduced access to essential sexual and reproductive health 
services in 18 PHCCs (health sector, July 2025). Vulnerable groups, including low-income 
Lebanese families, refugees, and migrants, are unable to pay out-of-pocket.

Reduced access to maternal care (antenatal and postnatal) throughout pregnancy increases 
the risk of maternal morbidity and mortality. The UNFPA estimates that over the course of 1 
year, 8,500 vulnerable pregnant women will have reduced access to essential maternal health 
services (40% of those affected are due to the 13 damaged PHCCs while 60% are due to 
defunding at 18 PHCCs). Furthermore, UNFPA estimates that due to defunding at 18 PHCCs, 
14,000 women will have reduced access to essential SRH services, including family planning.
Post-December 2024 Arrivals from Syria:
Post-2024 arrivals who ﬂed following the change of the regime are mostly residing in Akkar and 
Baalbak El Hermel. Those present at informal collective shelters live with poor WaSH conditions 
which put women and girls at increased risk of UTIs and reproductive health tract infections. 
Health partners serving these extremely vulnerable populations report barriers to access that 
include legal status preventing them from seeking services at hospitals located beyond 
Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) checkpoints, transportation costs, and lack of awareness of 
available services (most importantly subsidized deliveries at hospitals). 
Some projects serving post-2024 arrivals primarily through primary health satellite units (PSUs) 
are coming to an end in Q4 2025. Further support is needed to ﬁll the gaps of defunding and 
expand SRH service provision to aid the health sector recovery following the hostilities and 
respond to the SRH needs of Syrian newcomers.
In Q3 2025, health partners continued to subsidize deliveries at 35 supported hospitals. 
New projects announced by health partners in September include: Caritas covering high risk 
pregnancies across Lebanon under LHF funding; Caritas covering all pregnancies in Nabatieh 
and South under UNFPA/KOICKA funding; PUI’s project focusing on adolescent pregnancies in 
Beirut and Baabda under SIDA funding; MdM extending their support to end of December 2025 
for low risk deliveries through the midwife-led birthing center and high risk pregnancy support 
at RHUH, and ACF’s extension of its coverage of high-risk pregnancies in Bekaa and Baalbak-El 
Hermel to end of December 2025.
Even though several health partners have stepped in to cover more deliveries, mainly high-risk 
pregnancies, these projects have low targets, cover all populations, and many close at the end 
of 2025. While some health partners (ACF, Caritas, and PUI) have secured funding under LHF 
and ECHO for 2026 to cover high risk pregnancies. The current and secured interventions are 
estimated to cover only 40% of the estimated refugee high risk pregnancies in need of support 
for institutional deliveries as of Dec 1, 2025. Also, commitment towards covering low to 
medium-risk pregnancies for vulnerable populations and speciﬁcally refugees remains 
lacking.
As of December 1st, refugees will no longer be covered by UNHCR including acute emergency, 
delivery, and NICU/PICU conditions. Without UNHCR support, the health sector estimates 
approx. 16,500 vulnerable women and 1000 neonates will face ﬁnancial barriers to accessing 
safe hospital deliveries & NICU services increasing the risk of both maternal and neonatal 
mortality.
Further support is needed as the health sector continues to advocate with donors and partners 
to expand support to essential maternal and neonatal services at hospitals to cover the 
expected gap as of Dec 1, 2025.

Globally, 1 in 3 women are subjected to physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner or sexual 
violence from a non-partner (WHO).
The Q3 2025 Gender-Based Violence Information Managment System (GBV IMS) ﬁndings indicate 
that reported rape incidents constituted 3% of the total reported GBV cases, reﬂecting a consistent 
proportion with that recorded in Q2 2025 (3%). Although total reported GBV cases decreased by 
4% in Q3 compared to Q2 2025, the percentage of reported rape incidents increased in Bekaa (4% 
in Q3 2025, compared to 3% in Q2 2025) and in Beirut/Mount Lebanon (5% in Q3 2025, compared 
to 4% in Q2 2025).
Sexual assault incidents decreased across all regions (10% in Q3 2025 compared to 14% in Q2 
2025). However, the percentage of reported psychological/emotional abuse has signiﬁcantly 
increased in Q3 2025, especially in the South. Partners attribute this to an increase in verbal sexual 
assault and abuse, which are reported under this category according to the global classiﬁcation of 
GBV incidents.
The MOPH PHENICS reports a 60% increase in beneﬁciaries of CMR services when compared to 
Q2 2025. This increase should not be interpreted solely as an rise in rape cases; it is likely also 
attributable to an increase in survivors reaching services and improved reporting by facilities. 
● 7/14 of MOPH CMR facilities have reported on cases for Q3 2025. The facilities are 
included in Nabatieh, Baalbak, Zahle, Beirut, and Tripoli districts. 
● Key statistics include:
o Approx. 55% (43% in 2024) of reported survivors receiving CMR services at CMR 
clinics are children
o Approx. 65% of reported survivors receiving CMR services at CMR clinics are 
female
o Approx. 75% (57% in 2024) of reported survivors sought services at CMR facilities 
after > 72 hours of the rape incident. 
Challenges
● Delayed referral to CMR facility, beyond the critical 72 hours window indicated for essential 
time-sensitive medical and forensic services
● MOPH discontinued Daher El Bashq Gov. hospital due to mishandling a pediatric rape case 
in August 
● Multiple barriers to timely access to forensic services (ﬁnancial, fear of stigma, need to 
sensitize forensic doctors on CMR services…)  
● Community awareness of CMR services remains low 
In Q3 2025, health partners supported all 14 CMR facilities (8 hospitals and 6 PHCCs) to offer 
free-of-charge CMR services to rape survivors of all nationalities.

mobile units that operate in various communities across Lebanon. These services targeted 
vulnerable groups, including Syrian refugees, IDPs, people with disabilities, and the LGBTQI 
community. Support varied between Comprehensive SRH services and Information.

Breaking the Silence — Empowering Women Through Integrated GBV and SRH Services
Mariam, a 32-year-old Syrian refugee living in the Bekaa Valley, had endured years of 
emotional and physical violence from her husband. With three young children and no 
income, she felt trapped in silence and fear. The ongoing economic crisis and displacement 
had left her isolated, with no access to medical or psychological care.
One day, while attending a community awareness session organized by SALAMA under the 
ECHO/UNFPA project, Mariam learned about the availability of conﬁdential GBV case 
workers and reproductive health services. Encouraged by the outreach worker’s empathy and 
reassurance, she decided to visit SALAMA’s clinic for the ﬁrst time.
At the clinic, Mariam was warmly received by the GBV case worker, who provided a safe and 
conﬁdential environment for her to share her experience. She was immediately linked to the 
midwife and gynecologist for a medical consultation after reporting symptoms of pelvic pain 
and fatigue. The medical team conducted a full SRH assessment and provided treatment 
and counselling on family planning options to help her regain control over her body and 
reproductive choices.
In parallel, the psychologist and caseworker offered emotional support and developed a 
tailored safety and recovery plan for her. Over the next few weeks, Mariam attended several 
psychosocial support sessions, where she began to rebuild her conﬁdence and self-worth. 
The team also referred her for legal consultation regarding her protection options.
 “I never imagined that I could feel safe again, ” Mariam said. “At SALAMA, I found care, 
respect, and a chance to start over. ”

Promoting Early Detection and Health Equity: Awareness Campaigns Across Lebanon
During September and October, Amel Association International carried out a series of breast 
health awareness campaigns across its primary healthcare centers in Beqaa, 
Baalbek/Hermel, Mount Lebanon, South Lebanon, and Nabatieh. The initiative aimed to 
equip community members with accurate information on self-examination, early detection, 
and the beneﬁts of timely screening for improved health outcomes.
The sessions fostered an open and supportive environment where participants could 
discuss concerns, ask questions, and access reliable guidance. As a direct result, around

These campaigns highlight Amel’s continued commitment to advancing gender-responsive 
healthcare and ensuring equitable access to information, prevention, and early diagnosis for

Médecins du Monde (MdM) continued to advance the midwifery-led model of care at the 
Birth Center of Raﬁk Hariri University Hospital (RHUH) under the WILADA project. A notable 
increase in Normal Vaginal Deliveries at the Birth Center was recorded, rising from 32 in July 
and 32 in August, to 50 in September. This positive trend is signiﬁcant in a university hospital 
context where an OBGYN–led model remains predominant, reﬂecting growing conﬁdence in 
midwife-led and women-centered care.
Throughout Q3, 103 NVDs were conducted at the Birth Center, with 96% led by midwives, 
while residents/OB-GYNs intervened only as needed. Episiotomy rates remained low (Q3 
average: 9.6%), and per RHUH protocols, midwives do not perform suturing independently 
but manage most ﬁrst- and second-degree tears with supervision or joint involvement as 
required.
MdM also supported safe care pathways, contributing to 267 timely referrals of high-risk 
pregnancies to the Delivery Suite or specialized services. These results highlight improved 
access, strengthened midwifery practice, and a gradual shift towards safe, evidence-based 
midwifery-led intrapartum care within a tertiary hospital setting.

Supporting Women and Girls in Conﬂict-Affected Areas: ACF’s MHM Campaign in South 
Lebanon
Access to safe and digniﬁed menstrual hygiene management (MHM) remains a critical 
challenge for women and adolescent girls living in conﬂict-affected areas. Recognizing this 
need, Action Against Hunger (ACF) launched a targeted campaign to distribute MHM kits to 
vulnerable populations in Nabatiyeh and Bent Jbeil.
This initiative was implemented in close collaboration with local municipalities to ensure that 
distributions were both accessible and culturally appropriate. Each kit contained essential 
hygiene items designed to help women and girls manage their menstrual health safely and 
with dignity. In addition to distributing MHM kits, ACF also provided Multiple Micronutrient 
Supplements (MMS) to adolescents, aiming to improve their nutritional status and overall 
well-being.
Beyond the distribution, ACF prioritized health awareness and education. These sessions 
covered topics such as menstrual hygiene, reproductive health, nutrition, avoiding unhealthy 
habits, stress management, and general self-care practices. These sessions aimed to break 
taboos, promote knowledge, and empower women and girls to make informed decisions 
about their health and well-being.
One adolescent girl shared her thoughts after the session, saying:
“The session was very insightful and important for every young woman. It taught me a lot about 
the menstrual cycle, helped me understand my body better, and gave me the conﬁdence not to 
fear or feel embarrassed about something so natural.”
