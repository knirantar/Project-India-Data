Women and girls in Somalia continue to experience heightened exposure and safety risks to Gender Based 
Violence as the first quarter of 2026 unfolds. Conflict, climate displacement have created a challenging 
environment, particularly in high-density Internally Displaced Population (IDP) hubs and conflict affected 
districts including Banadir (Daynile and Kahda), Lower Juba (Kismayo and Afmadow), Gedo (Doolow, 
Luuq, Belet Xaawo, Garbahaarey, Baardheere, and Ceel Waaq), Bay (Baidoa, Qansax Dheere, Hudur, and 
Diinsoor), Mudug (Hobyo, South Galkacyo, and Jariban), and Hiraan and Middle Shebelle (Belet Weyne, 
Jowhar, Adan Yabaal, Mahaday, Balcad, and Cadale).1

The Gender Gap Analysis
Specific GBV Risks (OXFAM Analysis)
There is a stark “gendered divide” in how people experience systematic failure in the above regions. 
While women fear for their lives and bodies, men are more likely to report issues with the distribution 
of resources.
• Women & Girls (61 percent) of cases reported focuses on physical safety. This includes threats 
encountered while performing daily chores (collecting water/firewood) and risks within the home.
• Men & Boys (39 percent) of cases reported focuses on aid and services. This includes grievances 
regarding aid diversion and failures in how services are delivered.

Gender Based Violence Situation -Ist Quarter 2026  
Specialized GBV services are currently in a state of crisis due to drastic funding cuts. Closures of GBV 
facilities including Women and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) and GBV One stop centers (GBV OSCs); and 
the reduction of GBV service staff due to severity of funding gaps. These were primary inhibitors to 
access to service.   
Out of a total of 120 registered GBV centers, 66 (55%) are currently closed or non-operational. This includes 
Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS) and One-Stop Centers (OSCs). In remote areas, the culture of 
silence persists not only due to stigma but due to the physical absence of any confidential reporting 
mechanism.  The reduction in GBV specialized services is the most urgent barrier to survivor recovery.

Key Challenges
• Massive funding cuts across the county have forced 55% of GBV facilities to close, leaving survivors 
with nowhere to go for confidential help.
• While cash assistance has helped, it is currently not enough to reach the vast number of women in 
need.
• Reporting of sexual harassment remains dangerously low because many survivors fear social stigma 
or do not know how to report safely.

Urgent action
• Well-lit and lockable latrines in IDP camps (WASH, Camp committees)
• Sustained use of cash assistance through case management to meet urgent needs (CASH ACTORS)
• Need to support the re-opening of women and girls’ safe spaces. (GBV Actors)
• Broader dissemination of PSEA messaging among key clusters (Food, WASH, Health)
Women and girls in Somalia remain at high risk of gender-based violence due to ongoing conflict, droughts 
floods and multiple displacements. While the 2026 Talk to the Loop report showed a high level of reported 
protection concerns, the actual capacity to help these survivors has been severely weakened.  These 
risks are mainly due to food insecurity, and weak physical and economic security. 
 
Service limitation because of funding cuts and closure of GBV service sites impact on the ability of GBV 
survivors to access services. Increased response of cash assistance through case management in the 
4th quarter of 2025 and the first quarter 2026 improved GBV survivors’ ability to access services; however, 
this is still inadequate for the large population of women and girls with GBV, and protection needs.  PSEA 
reporting is still low because of stigma, lack of confidence or ignorance of existing reporting channels.
