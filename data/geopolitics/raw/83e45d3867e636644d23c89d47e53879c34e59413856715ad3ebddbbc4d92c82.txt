By FFWC's evening (18:00) update on 11 July, the picture had moved on: seven stations on five rivers in six districts were flowing

metres above danger level in the afternoon before easing slightly by evening.27 A special BMD warning issued the same day 
forecasts another 100–300 mm of rain through 12 July, with river levels expected to keep rising and flooding continuing across 
parts of the Hill Tracts.26 Looking ahead 24–48 hours, FFWC flags fresh risk of danger-level flooding on the Muhuri, Feni, Selonia 
and Halda rivers in Feni, Chattogram and Khagrachhari districts, with low-lying river-side areas of Lakshmipur and Noakhali also 
at risk of brief inundation; on the Sarigowain, Jadukata, Someshwari and Bhugai-Kangsha rivers across Sylhet, Sunamganj, 
Netrokona, Sherpur and Mymensingh; on the Surma-Kushiyara system, where conditions in low-lying parts of Sylhet and 
Sunamganj may worsen slightly; and on the Teesta, Dharla and Dudhkumar rivers in the north (Nilphamari, Lalmonirhat, Rangpur, 
Kurigram and Gaibandha).27 
While rainfall has begun to ease in Chattogram city and floodwater is receding in some upazilas, runoff from upstream areas is 
pushing floodwaters into new downstream areas, and conditions have deteriorated sharply in Satkania and Lohagara (Chattogram) 
and in Banshkhali and Chakaria (Cox's Bazar). A new and separate flood emergency has also opened in the north-east, where the 
Dhalai river embankment breached in Kamalganj upazila, Moulvibazar, on the night of 8 July. Habiganj district is also being 
closely monitored as the Khowai river remains above danger level. District-level figures for all of these areas are set out in the 
table below. 
The human toll continues to climb, and different sources report different totals depending on their reporting window and method. 
Media reports place the national death toll at no fewer than 44 people as of the evening of 11 July3; UNHCR reports 15 Rohingya 
refugees killed and 4,307 people left homeless across the 33 Rohingya camps in Cox's Bazar7. BDRCS presents these side by side 
rather than reconciling them into one number.  
NAWG's Flash Update #2, issued on 11 July 2026, revises this upward: an estimated 1,074,534 people across 10 districts are now 
affected, with 26 casualties, around 36,500 people temporarily displaced, 1,700 shelters activated, and roughly 3,300 water points 
and 10,700 latrines damaged nationwide.26

Other district context 
● Cox's Bazar (camps) – updated to 11 July, 18:00 (BDRCS/IFRC Situation Report 6): across all 33 camps, 401 weather-
related incidents between 5 and 11 July have affected 8,000 households (35,897 people), damaged 3,838 shelters and 
displaced 8,578 individuals; 15 fatalities and 19 injuries have been recorded to date. Within the 8 BDRCS priority camps 
(11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19), 138 incidents have affected 2,246 households (10,743 individuals), damaging 1,771 
shelters and 61 WASH facilities, with 7 deaths from landslides.28 
● By 11 July, 18:00, flooding had spread to 71 unions and 4 municipalities in the host community, affecting an estimated 
136,631 people. 8 deaths have been recorded in the host community: 5 from landslides in Cox's Bazar Sadar and Chakaria, 
1 from a collapsed mud wall in Pekua, and 2 from drowning in Chakaria and Maheshkhali. Cox's Bazar district 
administration has paid compensation to 4 of 5 host-community deceased families' households and 2 of 7 injured 
residents, and has 200 CPP volunteers on standby for landslide rescue.28 
● Bandarban: entire district remains high landslide risk; Sangu and Matamuhuri rivers above danger level at multiple points; 
all tourist sites closed until at least 12 July; leave cancelled for district staff on 11–12 July.3,20 
● Chattogram: floodwater receding in the city and in Raozan, Rangunia, Fatikchhari and Hathazari, but conditions have 
worsened in Satkania and Lohagara; a coastal embankment section in Banshkhali has collapsed, allowing saline water 
further inland.3,12 
● Rangamati: 126 separate landslide incidents over four days, the highest of any district, though damage has mostly been 
structural rather than to life.3,14 
● Khagrachari: entire district remains high landslide risk; Chengi and Maini rivers rising; Dighinala–Longudu route and 
sections of the Khagrachari–Rangamati road remain cut.14 
● Moulvibazar: Dhalai river embankment breach in Kamalganj upazila (night of 8 July) flooded Islampur, Madhabpur and 
Adampur unions across 4 upazilas; the 1 recorded death was a person swept away by floodwaters in Rajnagar upazila.4,21 
● Habiganj / Sylhet / Sunamganj: The Khowai River in Habiganj remains above the danger level, and the BDRCS field unit 
has been placed on alert alongside existing standby units in Sylhet and Sunamganj. According to the 11 July NDRCC 
forecast, renewed flood risk is expected over the next 24–48 hours for the Surma–Kushiyara river system, with the highest 
concern for Doarabazar, Tahirpur, Bishwambharpur, Chhatak Upazila, and Markuli Point in Sunamganj District, as well 
as Fenchuganj Upazila, Sylhet City and surrounding low-lying areas in Sylhet District. Rangpur Division, particularly the 
Teesta and Dharla river basins, is also forecast to face possible danger-level flooding during the same period.²⁴,22 
● Regional connectivity, markets and landslide risk (NAWG, 11 July): road connectivity remains badly disrupted in South 
Chattogram alone, 241.31 km of roads have been damaged; the Bandarban–Ruma and Bandarban–Thanchi roads are 
blocked; the Chattogram–Ramgarh highway is underwater at Hathazari; and the Khagrachhari–Rangamati road is cut at 
Mahalchhari, leaving Baghaichhari and Bilaichhari residents stranded. The Chattogram–Cox's Bazar rail line remains 
suspended near Shomser Para, Kalurghat (resumption expected around 13 July), and Sajek Valley is closed. Markets 
remain non-functional in flood-affected parts of Bandarban and Khagrachhari, while markets in Chattogram, Cox's Bazar 
and Rangamati are functioning. Landslide risk remains high in Bandarban (Sadar, Lama, Naikhongchhari), Chattogram 
(Banshkhali, City Corporation wards 7, 8, 9 and 14), Cox's Bazar (Sadar, Teknaf, Ukhiya), and in Khagrachhari and 
Rangamati districts.26

GOVERNMENT AND OTHER ORGANIZATIONS' ACTIONS 
● Updated relief picture (MoDMR, as of 10 July, via NAWG Flash Update #2): across Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, 
Rangamati, Khagrachhari and Bandarban, the government has distributed BDT 5,785,000 in cash, 643.4 metric tons 
of rice and 28,015 dry food packets and has a further BDT 6,200,000 in cash and 1,314.6 metric tons of rice 
prepositioned for release.26 
● The Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief's latest report, released on 11 July Saturday evening, said 1,022,963 
people have been affected by the disaster triggered by days of heavy rain and hill runoff.29 The ministry's assessment 
shows Chattogram has been the worst-hit district in terms of the number of affected people. Flooding and waterlogging 
across 16 Upazilas have affected 759,530 people, with 188,648 families marooned.Cox's Bazar recorded the second-
highest level of damage, with 158,027 people affected across 10 Upazilas and 39,506 families stranded. Among the 
other affected districts, 27,220 people have been affected in nine Upazilas of Khagrachhari, 3,524 people in nine 
Upazilas of Rangamati, 8,350 people in seven Upazilas of Bandarban, 38,172 people in four Upazilas of Moulvibazar 
and 28,140 people in three Upazilas of Habiganj. An international wire report carried the same day put the national 
death toll at 44 and said flooding had disrupted life and stranded 267,918 households across the seven worst-hit 
districts, broadly in line with the ministry’s own figures.29 
● A second round of relief was approved on 9 July: Tk 2.5 million for Chattogram, Tk 2 million for Cox's Bazar, and 
Tk 1 million each for Rangamati, Khagrachhari and Bandarban, along with additional rice allocations, on top of the 
Tk 1 million and 200 tonnes of rice given to each of the five districts on 7 July. 
● The Ministry of Chittagong Hill Tracts Affairs approved a special GR rice allocation of 1,600 metric tonnes for flood- 
and landslide-affected people across Rangamati (500 MT), Khagrachari (800 MT) and Bandarban (300 MT). 
● An additional Tk 10 million from the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief and Tk 20 million from the Prime 
Minister's Relief Fund have been allocated for the wider affected area. 
● With support from Save the Children Bangladesh, YPSA has distributed Multipurpose Cash (MPC) assistance of Tk 
6,000 each to 100 vulnerable households in Banshkhali and Chattogram City, with a further 100 households planned. 
● The Bangladesh Anticipatory Action Framework for Landslides remains active, having released approximately USD 
1.6 million to support an estimated 309,873 people across 67 unions in 14 upazilas, led by WFP, Save the Children
Bangladesh, FAO and Caritas Bangladesh, alongside a parallel USD 12.2 million riverine flood framework covering 
15 northern and central districts. 
● The Flood Forecasting and Warning Centre (FFWC) continues to monitor river levels across the Chattogram, Sylhet, 
Mymensingh and Rangpur divisions and expects gradual improvement in the south-east over the next 24–48 hours, 
while flagging fresh flood risk in northern and north-eastern districts. 
● Cox's Bazar district administration has paid cash compensation to 4 of 5 host-community deceased families (BDT 
25,000 each, BDT 100,000 total) and to 2 of 7 injured host-community residents (BDT 15,000 each, BDT 30,000 
total); 200 CPP volunteers are on standby for landslide rescue in risk-prone areas.16 
● Bandarban district administration has suspended tourism district-wide until 12 July, cancelled staff leave for 11–12 
July, appointed additional magistrates, and formed dedicated vigilance and relief teams alongside police, army, ansar 
and fire service personnel.20
