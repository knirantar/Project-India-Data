Reporting period: May 2026 
 
The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) was activated as the Emergency 
Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) on 31 October 2023 in response to the crisis in Gaza. This Situation Report 
provides a monthly update on LTC telecoms activities. All references to the LTC in this report relate specifically 
to the telecommunications area of the cluster .

Humanitarian Overview 
Humanitarian conditions in Gaza remained extreme and life-threatening throughout May, with most of 
the population displaced and living in overcrowded, inadequate shelters amid ongoing insecurity and 
intermittent hostilities. 
Humanitarian operations were severely constrained by access restrictions, insecurity, limited entry 
points, and shortages of fuel, spare parts, and critical supplies, resulting in inconsistent aid delivery 
and increasing risks to essential infrastructure systems. Kerem Shalom remained the only operational 
crossing for humanitarian cargo, with Zikim partially reopened for northern entry, while all other 
crossings, including Rafah and Kissufim, remained closed.  
Despite sustained humanitarian efforts, significant funding gaps, access limitations, and widespread 
infrastructure damage prevented the scale-up of assistance, leaving conditions deteriorating with no 
immediate prospect of recovery .

Telecommunications Overview 
Telecommunications services in Gaza remain critically disrupted due to extensive infrastructure 
damage, ongoing hostilities, and severely limited access for repairs. An estimated 75 percent of 
network infrastructure has been destroyed since October 2023. 
Mobile data services remain unavailable, with only intermittent 2G voice coverage in limited areas. 
Many sites rely on overstretched generators, increasing the risk of permanent equipment failure and a 
potential full communications blackout without urgent entry of spare parts and consumables. 
Damage to fibre backbones and microwave links has forced operators to rely on short-range, 
improvised solutions with limited reliability. The use of Starlink devices remain s prohibited under 
national regulations and due to lack of authorised coverage. 
Humanitarian operations continue to depend on a small quantity of satellite phones, VHF radio, and in-
person coordination, while operators face persistent equipment shortages and movement restric tions. 
Communities rely on private generators, informal charging points, and improvised Wi-Fi hotspots 
where minimal connectivity is available. 
LTC Activities  
Coordination 
In May, LTC Telecoms in Palestine continued to adapt its response in Gaza to sustain critical 
communications for humanitarian operations under highly constrained conditions. Priorities remained 
focused on expanding secure connectivity, maintaining power and network functionality, and 
advocating for the entry of fuel, spare parts, and essential ICT equipment to ensure continuity of 
services. 
Engagements during May focused on strengthening coordination and advocacy. LTC Telecoms 
engaged with private sector telecommunications actors to support connectivity solutions, cluster-
related activities, and contingency planning for the West Bank. Discussions with the Association of 
International Development Agencies (AIDA) further addressed constraints on equipment entry into 
Gaza, with follow-up planned to support engagement with relevant authorities. 
On 06 May, LTC Telecoms and WFP met with the Humanitarian Military Interaction Access Unit (HMI) 
to review import procedures for ICT equipment and related approval requirements. Updates on the 
approval process were shared and are being formalized by WFP, while follow-up continues on pending 
approvals. 
On 07 May, LTC Telecoms and WFP conducted a site assessment of the UNDP building in Gaza City to 
support plans for re-establishing inter-agency presence in northern Gaza. While the structure was 
assessed as suitable, significant enabling works are required, including cabling, electrical preparation, 
and ICT installation, with follow-up actions underway. 
LTC Telecoms also participated in the Education Cluster sub-national meeting on 07 May to present on 
telecoms activities and challenges, with further technical discussions planned. Additional coordination 
on 10 May included engagement with private sector operators on connectivity and contingency 
planning, alongside the completion of fuel repayments to telecommunications actors through in-kind 
arrangements with UNOPS and WFP.

Internet connectivity  
Humanitarian operations in Gaza remained critically dependent on Paltel as the sole functioning 
Internet Service Provider (ISP), with no alternative or backup systems such as fibre, microwave links, 
or VSAT available, leaving agencies without redundancy to mitigate disruptions. 
In May, LTC Telecoms engaged Jawwal/Paltel to address connectivity challenges, operational 
requirements, and options to reduce connectivity costs for humanitarian hubs, with follow-up actions 
planned. 
LTC Telecoms also strengthened connectivity at the Palestinian Non-Governmental Organization 
(PNGO) Gaza City location by refurbishing and configuring a donated computer as a dedicated security 
and user management appliance. Training for the PNGO IT Manager on system commissioning, 
maintenance, backup, and recovery is planned, supported by detailed documentation. 
Security Communications  Systems 
LTC Telecoms and partners continued advancing the 2026–27 interagency Security Communications 
Plan by consolidating VHF repeater data, integrating recent assessments, and supporting the 
standardisation of UN radio configurations. With the risk of a full internet outage, strengthening radio 
communications as a reliable fallback remained a priority, alongside UNDSS requests for agencies to 
submit full radio inventories for inclusion in the master plan. Proof-of-concept testing of the recently 
upgraded digital system is underway following discussions with UNDSS on 07 May. 
LTC Telecoms coordinated with the Palestine Red Crescent Society and the International Committee of 
the Red Cross to support repairs to the VHF network. 
On 13 May, a VHF coverage assessment along the Gaza City–Zikim convoy route was completed, with 
recommendations issued to improve signal coverage and reliability. 
A two-year Long-Term Agreement (LTA) with Track24, a satellite-based provider of vehicle and 
personnel tracking services, was established for the period 07 May 2026 to 06 May 2028 to support 
real-time tracking inside the Gaza Strip, enhancing operational safety. 
Between 17 and 18 May, LTC Telecoms configured service laptops for VHF equipment and 
humanitarian hub operations, while also providing technical guidance to support site configuration in 
North Gaza. Additional SCS training was delivered in Khan Younis on 17 May, including sessions in 
Arabic, to strengthen frontline capacity to maintain critical communications.

Funding 
LTC Telecoms requires USD 1 million to sustain critical telecommunications, coordination, and 
information management services in Gaza, in line with the 2026 Flash Appeal for the occupied 
Palestinian territory. 
On 12 May, LTC Telecoms submitted a concept note to WFP Partnerships to explore additional funding 
options for ongoing activities in Gaza.

Challenges 
Fuel Shortages: Inconsistent fuel supply, compounded by ongoing regional escalation, is hindering 
humanitarian operations including telecommunications services. 
Limited telecommunications services: Voice and internet services in Gaza remain limited due to 
widespread infrastructure damage, limited access, import restrictions, and a lack of repair equipment. 
Lack of telecommunications equipment : Hostilities have destroyed key equipment, and ongoing 
import restrictions severely limit LTC’s ability to restore and deliver its planned services. 
Staff safety : Volatile security conditions and movement restrictions continue to compromise the safety 
and security of humanitarian staff and hinder the deployment of LTC services. 
Cybersecurity risks: Cybersecurity threats remain high. Use of tech platforms, including social and 
press media, requires caution. 
Funding : Limited funding continues to constrain LTC’s ability to meet urgent communications needs.
