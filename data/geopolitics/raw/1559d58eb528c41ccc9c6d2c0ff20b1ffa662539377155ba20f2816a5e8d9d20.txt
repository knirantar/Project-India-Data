Under the Migrant Protection Working Group, the National Office for Migration (ONM), the International Organization for Migration (IOM), and the Support Group for 
Repatriates and Refugees (GARR) gather data on the volume of forced return flows at Haiti’s entry points. This information is used to guide appropriate assistance mechanisms 
for individuals who have been forcibly returned. The collected data includes the number of forcibly returned individuals (through systematic counting), the dates of their returns, 
and the countries from which they departed. This report presents an overview of forced return flows observed in June 2026, during which 26,568 people were forcibly returned 
to Haiti, a 3 per cent increase compared to May 2026, making June the month with the highest number of forced returns since the start of 2026. Compared to June 2025, the 
number of forced returns during this month rose by 14 per cent. Furthermore, over the first six months of 2026, the number of forcibly returned individuals amounts to 
141,720, an 11 per cent increase compared to the same period in 2025, signaling an upward trend in forced returns in 2026 compared to previous years. The border crossings 
of Ouanaminthe and Malpasse saw the most significant increases between the first halves of 2025 and 2026, with rises of 58 and 165 percent, respectively. Explore the complete 
dataset collected since 2021 via the interactive dashboard.

METHODOLOGY:
This data collection is part of the Flow Monitoring component of the Displacement Tracking Matrix 
(DTM). The objective of Flow Monitoring is to gather information on the volume and characteristics 
of migrants observed at Flow Monitoring Points (FMPs). These points are selected in consultation 
with local authorities and partners and based on the contextual analysis carried out by IOM teams. 
With regard to forced returns to Haiti, these are the arrival points for forcibly returned persons: the 
main arrival points are the airport and the port of Cap Haïtien, the four official border posts 
between Haiti and the Dominican Republic (Belladère, Ouanaminthe, Anse-à-Pitres and Malpasse), as 
well as, occasionally, the port of Les Cayes and the airport of Port-au-Prince.
At each of these points, two main data collection methods are deployed: Flow Monitoring Registry 
(FMR) and individual surveys (Flow Monitoring Survey – FMS). The FMR produces data on the scale 
of movements, including the number of persons forcibly returned, dates of return, countries of 
departure, and points of arrival in Haiti. The FMS, for its part, collects more detailed information on 
the individuals concerned, through interviews conducted with a sample, in order to better 
understand their profiles, migration history and intentions, and to inform response accordingly. This 
report is based on FMR data collected at arrival points between 1 and 30 June 2026.
Limitations: the data presented pertains to forced return flows, and does not constitute individual 
monitoring of forcibly returned persons. Thus, the same person who experiences multiple forced 
returns is counted with each new episode of forced return. FMS interviews, conducted with a 
sample of over 43,000 forcibly returned persons between 2023 and 2025, showed that 53 per cent 
of them had already experienced multiple forced returns (see the interactive dashboard on the 
profiles of forcibly returned persons).
