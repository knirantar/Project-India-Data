The following is a near-verbatim transcript of today’s briefing by Stéphane Dujarric, Spokesman for the Secretary-General.

UN Interim Force in Lebanon
All right, thank you for your understanding for this delay. As you know, we have some pretty unpleasant news coming from Lebanon, where another one of our UN peacekeepers was killed.  Sergeant Milovan Jovanović of Serbia’s 27
th
 Mechanized Battalion died early this morning from critical injuries sustained when a mortar shell hit his position near Marjayoun, in southern Lebanon.
The Secretary-General 
condemns
 the killing of our peacekeeping colleague. He expresses his deepest condolences to the family, to the friends and colleagues of the peacekeeper who died, and to the Government and people of the Republic of Serbia.
Sergeant Milovan Jovanović would have turned 37 on 6 June.  He was a son, he was a husband and a father to a son and a daughter.  This was his first peacekeeping deployment.  He arrived in Lebanon at the end of January of this year, and he died while fulfilling his duty in the service of peace under the blue flag of the United Nations.
Meanwhile, despite all of this, our United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) colleagues continue to monitor the situation in the south of the country and carry out their mandated tasks despite the ongoing challenging security environment.  Yesterday, UNIFIL detected 69 violations of Lebanese airspace across both sectors.  Peacekeepers reported approximately 25 air strikes by Israel Defense Forces (IDF) jets and 2 rocket attacks by an IDF helicopter.
Additionally, peacekeepers reported approximately 569 trajectories of projectiles fired from IDF positions south of the Blue Line and within the mission’s area of operations.  A further 14 launches were detected from north to south, presumably by Hizbullah, including one crossing the Blue Line.  Separately, two UNIFIL positions near Blat in Sector East were struck by munition ricochets, causing property damage.
Just going back to the incident that killed our colleague Sergeant Jovanović.  Two of his colleagues during the same incident, one from El Salvador and one from Spain, were also injured.  They are reported in stable condition and are receiving care. We of course wish a speedy recovery.
Since March, seven peacekeepers serving with UNIFIL have now been killed following the escalation of hostilities, and several more have been wounded.  We reiterate that attacks on peacekeepers must stop.  They are grave violations of international humanitarian law and of Security Council resolution 1701 (2006), and as we have been saying, these killings may amount to war crimes.
UNIFIL has issued a full statement on the incident and on what happened and they will investigate the incident thoroughly.  However, based on an initial assessment, from UNIFIL observations, we understand that the position was struck by indirect fire coming from north of the Litani River.  As soon as we have more information, we will share that with you.  Those responsible must be held to account.  All must respect international law and protect those serving in the cause of peace.
The Secretary-General again urges all actors to respect the cessation of hostilities that was announced on 16 April.  The UN is committed to supporting the diplomatic efforts towards an extension of the cessation of hostilities and the full implementation of resolution 1701 (2006).
This tragic news comes at a time when here at the UN Headquarters tomorrow and this week, we have been holding a series of events to mark the International Day of United Nations peacekeepers.  This day honours peacekeepers past and present and reaffirms our shared responsibility to strengthen their work.
Jean-Pierre Lacroix, [the Under-Secretary-General for Peace Operations], will be our guest tomorrow on the occasion of the International Peacekeepers Day.  He will be our guest at noon at the briefing, and he will start on time.
And also, tomorrow morning, there will be a solemnly event to honour the more than 4,500 peacekeepers who have lost their lives in the cause of peace, including 59 in 2025.  The loss of Sergeant Jovanović today is a stark reminder of the risks peacekeepers take every day to serve communities and help preserve peace around the world.

Lebanon
Also, I want to flag that in Lebanon attacks on healthcare continue at an alarming rate.  Yesterday, a strike in South Governorate hit an ambulance, killing two paramedics and leaving a third critically injured.  Medical personnel, first responders and their facilities and assets must be protected.
We and our partners continue to deliver urgent humanitarian assistance.  To date, more than 684,000 people have received at least one round of emergency cash assistance, helping to cover basic food, water and other essential needs. However, as the escalation has entered its fourth month, funding shortfalls are imperiling our support to the most vulnerable people.
As I’ve told you before, tomorrow, in Beirut, our humanitarian colleague and head of humanitarian operation in Lebanon, Imran Riza, will join the Prime Minister of Lebanon to launch an extended flash appeal to mobilize resources to continue providing life-saving assistance over the next three months.  We reiterate our call for the protection of civilians, including humanitarian and medical personnel; full respect for international humanitarian law; and safe, sustained and unimpeded access for humanitarian workers wherever they are needed.
Meanwhile, on the diplomatic front, we have seen the announcement issued out of Washington, D.C., yesterday on Lebanon and Israel, and I can tell you that we 
welcome
 the announcement of a ceasefire following the fourth high-level trilateral meeting between Israeli and Lebanese representatives on 2 and 3 June that took place in Washington, D.C.  We reaffirm our support for efforts to end hostilities and urge all parties to respect the ceasefire, cease all attacks, and comply with international law at all times.  We are committed to supporting all diplomatic efforts in reinforcing the cessation of hostilities and the full implementation of Security Council resolution 1701 (2006).

Occupied Palestinian Territory
Turning to the Occupied Palestinian Territory:  Our Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) colleagues warn that funding shortfalls are forcing humanitarian partners in Gaza to scale down or suspend critical services.  Nearly halfway through the year, the $4.1 billion requirement identified in this year’s appeal for Gaza and the West Bank remains less than 15 per cent funded.  As a result, humanitarian partners’ ability to plan, pre-position relief and respond effectively is weakening at a time when most of Gaza’s 2.1 million people remain displaced and are heavily relying on our services.
To give some examples:  by the end of last month, four humanitarian partners were forced to start phasing out the trucking of water in Gaza, leaving more than 330,000 people across some 250 sites at risk of losing their primary source of drinking water.  Already, the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) warns that water shortages force families into a trade-off between drinking and maintaining hygiene to prevent disease.
The number of meals provided is also being reduced; as of last week, over 20 partners were providing 678,000 meals every day, down from 1.5 million in mid-March.  Other affected services include support for agricultural recovery, site management, education and the reopening of safe spaces for women ang girls. More services will be suspended without further funding.  We call upon donors to urgently increase their support. Member States are equally urged to press for the lifting of Israeli restrictions that continue impeding our work on the ground.
And just turning to the West Bank, our OCHA colleagues report that 27 herding families were recently forced out of their residential area near the Ein Fera’a water spring, in Hebron, following systematic harassment and intimidation inflicted by Israeli settlers that had taken over a nearby hilltop.  The community comprised 125 people, including 3 people with disabilities and more than 20 who have chronic illnesses.
This is the forty-sixth Palestinian community that has been displaced in full since [January] 2023 due to settler violence and related access restrictions.  Overall, more than 6,000 people have been displaced in this context since January 2023, including more than 2,000 since the beginning of 2026.

Somalia
Turning to Somalia.  I can tell you that the Secretary-General is alarmed about the reports of violence in Mogadishu.  He underscores the urgent need for all stakeholders to resume talks to identify a way forward to avoid reversing the progress seen in Somalia to date, as well as ensuring the protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure.  The Secretary-General calls on all parties to exercise utmost restraint, refrain from any actions that could incite further violence, and resolve political differences through dialogue.
Our colleagues at the UN Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) today noted that the Mission and international partners have repeatedly warned, in their statements and stakeholder engagements, of the risk of repeating the crisis that arose in 2021 over differences regarding the electoral road map, warnings that unfortunately, obviously, were not heeded.  The UN Mission urges all Somali leaders to prioritize the national interest and resume the talks that ended on 15 May.  Somalia’s international partners stand ready to facilitate such a dialogue.

Security Council/Syria
Back here this morning, the 
Security Council
 held a 
briefing
 on the implementation of resolution 2118 (2013) concerning the elimination of the chemical weapons programme in Syria.
The High Representative for Disarmament Affairs, Izumi Nakamitsu, told Council members that significant progress has been made recently.  She explained that at the beginning of May this year, a team from the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) office deployed to Syria was able to state that a “significant amount” of undeclared chemical weapons, related materials, and documentation were discovered in Syria.  These findings are a momentous discovery, she said, not just for Syria, but for international security and the global disarmament regime.
The latest weapons found in May have been transferred to a secure location and consultations on destruction plans are ongoing between the Syrian [Government] and the OPCW.

Ebola
An update on the Ebola response.  In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, our support to national health authorities includes case management, the operation of Ebola treatment centres, as well as the delivery of essential medicines and medical supplies. Our colleagues are also supporting community engagement and strengthened surveillance efforts to help prevent further spread of the virus.  According to the Congolese authorities, the Province of Ituri remains the epicentre of the outbreak, accounting for more than 90 per cent of cases.  But, as we have mentioned, cases were also recorded in North and South Kivu.
We and our partners are facing challenges in responding to Ebola given the location where the outbreak is in the eastern part of the Democratic Republic of the Congo.  Attacks against front-line workers, particularly during safe and dignified burial operations, are hindering the response, while persistent misinformation, rumours and mistrust are undermining the effective implementation of public health measures.
We remain engaged with the authorities and our partners on the ground to address these challenges and to support efforts to bring the outbreak under control as quickly as possible.
In Uganda, our team continues to support Government-led efforts to respond to the Ebola outbreak.  Under the leadership of the Resident Coordinator’ Leonard Zulu, and with the World Health Organization (WHO) leading the technical health response, we and our partners are supporting national authorities to contain the outbreak and prevent further spread.
The Ministry of Health, in collaboration with WHO, is leading contact tracing, surveillance, public awareness campaigns and infection prevention and control measures.  UNICEF is supporting risk communication and community engagement.  Meanwhile, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) is supporting point-of-entry screening, case management and community surveillance.  The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) is supporting surveillance and decongestion efforts in refugee transit and reception centres.
Uganda has expanded screening at 31 priority points of entry, including Entebbe International Airport.  Authorities have also activated rapid response teams, deployed mobile labs, and strengthened screening and monitoring, particularly for international travel and cross-border movements.  Uganda’s Ebola coordination structure remains fully operational and continues to support a national effort.

Cuba
A quick update from Cuba, where our humanitarian colleagues tell us the combined impact of the energy crisis following the US Executive Orders and other sanctions, alongside hurricanes and other natural disasters, is far reaching and expanding daily.  All basic services, from clean water and sanitation to food production and the health sector, have been impacted by the lack of fuel and electricity.  More than 100,000 surgeries have been postponed due to a deep shortage of medicines and medical supplies.
We, along with our partners, have a Plan of Action to assist up to 2 million people.  But, the energy crisis is also limiting our ability to deliver the aid already committed, with dozens of containers of food and medical supplies still sitting in ports due to lack of fuel to get them out of the ports.
This afternoon, our Resident Coordinator, Francisco Pichon; OCHA’s Crisis Response Director, Edem Wosornu; and the Permanent Representative of Cuba will address Member States on the deteriorating humanitarian situation in the country.  At the briefing, we expect to reiterate the Secretary-General’s calls on Member States to facilitate the timely and unimpeded delivery of fuel for humanitarian purposes and other life-saving aid, and to fund the Plan of Action.  The $82 million Plan of Action is only 21 per cent funded.  That event will be on UN WebTV.

Food Security
Just want to flag a new report by the World Food Programme (WFP), “Food Security Under Pressure”, that looks at how rising tensions in the Middle East in recent months are affecting people’s ability to afford food, especially in vulnerable countries.  It focuses on Afghanistan, Somalia and Sri Lanka to show how global problems can quickly affect everyday lives.  WFP says that the crisis is pushing up fuel and food prices, disrupting supply chains, and reducing incomes.  In the three countries that were studied, millions of additional people are at risk of food insecurity, and even those who were previously managing are now struggling.
