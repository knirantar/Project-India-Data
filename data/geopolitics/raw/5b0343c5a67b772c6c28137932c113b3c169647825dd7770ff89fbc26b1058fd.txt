HUMANITARIAN RESPONSE 
Humanitarian partners continue to deliver a coordinated, multi-sector response across Lebanon despite ongoing insecurity, 
repeated displacement, and access constraints. Assistance remains focused on the most vulnerable conflict -affected 
populations, including displaced households living both inside and outside collective shelters. Response activities include 
multi-purpose cash assistance, food support, health  care, nutrition services, protection interventions, education support, 
shelter assistance, WASH services, and site management, with particular attention to women and girls, children, persons 
with disabilities, older persons, and households facing multiple vulnerability factors. 
As displacement patterns remain fluid and return movements cautious, partners are adapting assistance to meet evolving 
needs. Efforts are underway to strengthen targeting through the Emergency Multi -Purpose Cash , expand learning 
opportunities for displaced children, maintain access to primary health  care and nutrition services, and provide protection 
support to individuals exposed to heightened risks. Shelter and site management partners continue to improve living 
conditions in collective shelters while supporting vulnerable households in hosted and rented accommodation. At the same 
time, WASH and food security partners are sustaining critical services to ensure access to safe water, hygiene support, and 
food assistance in displacement settings and affected communities. 
The response is being reinforced through inter -sector coordination, ongoing needs assessments, and additional 
humanitarian financing secured under the revised Flash Appeal. Humanitarian actors continue to operate in a challenging 
environment marked by inse curity, access restrictions, and population movements, while utilizing established civil -military 
coordination mechanisms to reach conflict -affected and hard -to-reach areas , including humanitarian notification system 
(HNS). Partners remain focused on sustaining life -saving assistance, addressing protection risks, and supporting safe, 
dignified, and informed decisions for displaced populations as the situation continues to evolve.  
 Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) 
The Cash Working Group (CWG) continues to plan new disbursements of Emergency Multi -Purpose Cash Assistance 
(EMPCA) to support the most vulnerable conflict-affected households across Lebanon, with a particular focus on displaced 
populations living outside collective shelters. 
A CWG meeting was held on 18 June, during wh ere the targeting methodology was presented to partners. The adopted 
approach is based on a mixed-method strategy designed to ensure equity, combining vulnerability-based, categorical, and 
geographical targeting. Households are prioritized according to specific vu lnerability profiles, including households with no 
working-age adults, single -parent households, those with high dependency ratios, households with two or more children, 
elderly-headed households, and households that include members living with disabilitie s. Households meeting multiple 
vulnerability criteria (three or more profiles) are given priority for assistance.  
In addition, a detailed presentation was delivered on the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA) self -registration data processing 
approach. The Shelter Sector also presented the Cash for Shelter guidance, and the importance of EMPCA reporting was 
strongly emphasized. 
The prioritization criteria will guide the upcoming targeting process. Subsequently, the prioritized list of households will be 
shared with CWG partners for the delivery of subsequent rounds of EMPCA to eligible Lebanese households. EMPCA 
support for refugees will follow the same prioritization approach.

 Education 
As of 22 June, Education Sector partners have reached 88,834 displaced children (62,978 Lebanese and 25,856 non-
Lebanese) through formal education , and a further 5,001 children through non -formal education pathways . To 
support learning at home and in collective shelters, 2,015 education kits have been distributed, individual student kits or 
learning materials have been provided to 2,020 children, and 129 children with disabilities have received assistive 
devices or rehabilitation services. 
Access to safe physical learning spaces remains a critical challenge. Of Lebanon’s 3,341 schools, 446 - including 331 public 
schools, 66 private schools, and 49 technical institutes  - are being used as collective shelters. This impacts the availability 
of an in-person learning and disproportionately affects adolescent girls, children with disabilities.  To help address this gap, 
the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE) is fast -tracking the approval of partner requests to implement 
learning activities directly within collective shelters. As of 22 June, 73 request s were approved across ten partners 
nationwide. Approved activities include learning retention support, the provision of learning supplies, services for children  
with disabilities, facilitation of online learning, and recreational and psychosocial support activi ties. 
 Food Security and Agriculture 
Since the escalation of hostilities on 2 March 2026, Food Security Sector partners have rapidly scaled up life -saving 
assistance for displaced populations across Lebanon. Emergency response efforts initially focused on supporting people in 
collective shelters, with partners delivering more than 14.5 million hot and cold meals  and distributing 149,746 ready-
to-eat (RTE) food kits containing items that require no preparation, ensuring immediate access to food for newly displaced 
households with limited cooking facilities. 
To complement shelter -based assistance, bread distributions have been expanded in coordination with the Ministry of 
Economy and Trade and the Union of Bakeries. Since April, partners have distributed 208,194 bread bundles  through 
a network of 22 bakeries. Discussions are ongoing with the Union of Bakeries regarding a potential second phase of bread 
distributions, subject to the availability of additional funding. 
Outside collective shelters, partners are also providing targeted, one -off in-kind food assistance, particularly in Beirut and 
Mount Lebanon governorates, to highly vulnerable displaced households. These interventions are being implemented in 
coordination with other response actors to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication.  
 
Health 
Health Sector partners provided 440,640 consultations to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and distributed 160,434 
courses of medication through 197 primary health care centres (PHCs). Partners continue to support emergency and 
trauma care, primary health  care services, the provision of essential medicines, disease surveillance, and mental health 
and psychosocial support services. Sustained funding and strengthened protection of health  care services remain critical 
to maintaining service continuity and preventing further deterioration in health outcomes.  
Despite regional political developments since 15 June that raised expectations of de -escalation, the security environment 
in Lebanon remains fragile and unpredictable. Since 15 June, one attack on health care has been reported through the 
World Health Organization (WHO) Surveillance System for Attacks on Health care (SSA), resulting in injuries to two health 
care workers while on duty. This brings the total number of reported incidents since 2 March to 208, with 133 deaths 
and 403 injuries among health care professionals. 
Continued insecurity, population movements, and repeated shelter closures and reopenings are disrupting the continuity of 
health services, the deployment of Primary Health  care Support Units, and overall response planning. Health facilities 
continue to face workforce shortages, supply constraints, and operational challenges, while vulnerable populations 
encounter persistent barriers to accessing care, shortages of essential medicines, limited referral capacity, and reduced 
access to specialized services and hospitalization support. 
Health partners continue to adapt service delivery modalities and  are supporting hospitals managing trauma and burn 
cases. Partners as well are working on strengthening the border health measures and referral systems to mitigate public 
health risks and ensure the continuity of essential health care services.

 Nutrition 
The nutrition response in Lebanon continues to address acute and evolving needs among the most vulnerable population 
groups, with Nutrition Sector partners maintaining the delivery of life -saving interventions across collective shelters and 
affected communities. Sustained efforts to expand nutrition screening, micronutrient supplementation, and Infant and Young 
Child Feeding (IYCF) support remain central to the response, with a focus on preventing the deterioration of nutritional 
status among children under five, adolescent girls, and pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW). 
Since 2 March, 48,797 children under five, adolescent girls, and PBW have been reached with emergency nutrition 
rations and micronutrient supplements, amounting to 734,192 rations and supplements providing at least one month of 
coverage. This includes 993 children aged 6 –12 months residing in collective shelters who received age -appropriate 
complementary feeding kits. In addition, 23,088 children under  five and 4,648 PBW were screened for wasting, enabling 
the early identification and referral of individuals requiring further nutrition support and treatment.  
The national IYCF hotline continues to serve as a critical support channel. Between 2 March and 22 June, it received 791 
calls from caregivers, of whom 663 were referred for specialized IYCF support services . In parallel, under MoPH -led 
Safe Provision of Breastmilk Substitutes (BMS) initiative , 411 infants under one year of age received infant formula  
support following individual assessments conducted by Nutrition Sector partners’ IYCF specialists, resulting in the 
distribution of 4,074 cans of infant formula. 
Complementing these efforts, 18,585 caregivers of children under five participated in counselling and awareness sessions 
on IYCF, nutrition, and Early Childhood Development (ECD), delivered through both in -person and digital platforms. 
 Protection 
The protection situation in Lebanon remains concerning, with displaced populations facing growing vulnerabilities as the 
crisis becomes protracted. Ongoing insecurity, particularly in southern Lebanon and Beirut’s southern suburbs, continues 
to restrict movement and access to services, disproportionately affecting women and girls, children, persons with disabilities, 
and other at-risk groups. 
As of 22 June, 284,714 internally displaced persons and other affected individuals had received protection services, 
more than 63 per cent of whom were women and girls. Services included Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse 
(PSEA), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and child protection awareness activities, psychosocial support for over 87,984 
individuals, case management for 2,110 individuals, protection cash assistance for approximately 11,041 
vulnerable people, dignity kits for more than 34,349 women and girls, and specialized support for 2,053 persons 
with disabilities. 
Despite the 15 June ceasefire announcement, return movements remain cautious and uneven , families continue to 
undertake “go -and-see” visits or maintain contingency arrangements due to ongoing insecurity, housing destruction, 
explosive ordnance risks, limited services, lack of livelihoods, and uncertainty regarding future security developments . 
These factors continue to increase the risk of unsafe returns and prolonged displacement.  
Protection concerns remain particularly acute for people living outside collective shelters, who face reduced access to 
assistance and services. Economic hardship, inadequate living conditions, and limited support are driving negative coping 
mechanisms and increasing psychosocial distress. Child protection risks, including child labour, early marriage, exploitation, 
and disrupted education, remain elevated, while women and girls continue to face heightened GBV risks linked to 
overcrowding, limited privacy, and barriers to specialized services. 
Families whose homes have been destroyed remain particularly vulnerable to prolonged displacement. Case management 
follow-up indicates continued requests for financial assistance to address conflict -related property damage. Partners also 
report increasing psychosocial distress, risks of family separation, unsafe movement, disrupted education, property theft, 
legal access barriers, social tensions, and challenges in referral pathways and follow -up for high-risk cases. 
Partners also report ongoing challenges affecting service delivery, including insecurity, limited outreach capacity, poor 
connectivity, and persistent shortages of essential Non-Food Items (NFI) and WASH items.

Site Management and Coordination (SMC) 
The Site Management and Coordination Sub-Sector held its second coordination meeting,  during which, the coordination 
team presented the SMC costing methodology, the activity and indicator logframe, and provided an update on the rollout of 
basic SMC training sessions.  To strengthen monitoring and reporting across the response, the SMC Sub -Sector has 
requested partners to review and provide feedback on the logframe. 
Following an extensive technical review process, the SMC site management support model and Sub -Sector Terms of 
Reference were endorsed by the Ministry of Social Affairs and shared with partners. This endorsement is expected to 
facilitate the release of fun ding for SMC partners and support the scale -up of site management interventions across the 
response. 
  Social Stability 
This period was defined by ambiguity between diplomatic progress and continued instability on the ground. While the 15 
June announcement raised expectations of de -escalation and prompted cautious initial returns toward the south ern 
governorates, renewed strikes in Nabatieh and Baalbek  governorates on 19 June reversed these movements, generating 
fresh displacement toward Saida, South Governorate and Beirut Governorate.  
Key tension drivers , host community pressure, shelter capacity, and state –citizen relations, all remained at critical level. 
Rising rents, unpredictable population movements, and diverging political readings of the ceasefire announcement  kept 
social tensions elevated. The information ecosystem was highly active, return footage showing widespread destruction, 
Israel's expanded military zone map, and unverified claims fuelling public anxiety. Service stress was visible across water, 
telecommunications, and education, while aid access to border areas remained fragile.  
 
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene 
As of 22 June, the WASH Sector has reached 1,111,564 people across collective shelters, host communities, and out-
of-shelter settings through emergency water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Despite this progress, significant gaps 
remain. Assessment data from the Rapid Technical Assessment (Round 2) identified additional needs to meet minimum 
sector standards. 
Since 2 March, WASH Sector partners have provided 968,000 litres of fuel to water establishments , sustaining public 
water supply services for an estimated 855,797 people. WASH partners further supported 318 water stations with fuel, 
repairs, and spare parts critical to maintaining network functionality under increased demand.  
Displaced people inside collective shelters have received a total of 124,756 hygiene kits, 81,322 menstrual hygiene kits, 
and 1,658 cleaning kits. WASH partners have also provided 3,765,725 litres of potable water and 126,530 m³ of water 
to collective shelters through water trucking , addressing immediate drinking water needs in sites with limited or 
disrupted water supply.

 Logistics and Telecommunications (LTC) 
A total of 29 convoys were delivered so far to hard-to-reach areas, covering a total of 16 locations. NGOs and UN agencies 
alike benefited from the services, which enabled to deliver commodities from all sectors (e.g. WASH, Shelter, Food, Health, 
etc.) in a coordinated manner.  
The Logistics Cluster also delivered a total of 4 convoys from Amman to Lebanon, providing the service to 6 partners. The 
next expected regional convoy (#5) scheduled to arrive in Lebanon on Thursday , 25 June, will deliver hygiene kits for

GENERAL COORDINATION 
Response Monitoring: Initial consolidated monitoring results under the revised Flash Appeal indicate that approximately 
651,500 people have received at least one form of humanitarian assistance  to date. Partners agreed to strengthen 
inter-sector analysis to better understand response overlap, identify remaining geographic and sectoral gaps, and continue 
efforts to strengthen monitoring of Flash Appeal implementation. 
Emergency Rapid Needs Assessment (ERNA): Final ERNA findings continue to inform response prioritization under the 
revised Flash Appeal. Key findings highlighted persistent overcrowding and unaffordable housing, deteriorating conditions 
in collective shelters, continued disruption to children's education, gaps in WASH services, and ongoing protection concerns 
affecting vulnerable groups. The Inter sectoral Coordination Group ( ISCG) will launch a lessons -learned exercise to 
strengthen future rapid inter-agency assessments. 
Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM): REACH is implementing a Humanitarian Situation Monitoring exercise in hard-
to-reach conflict-affected areas. Data collection is pending endorsement from the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA), with 
findings expected in mid-July to support operational decision-making. 
Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA):  REACH is preparing for the 2026 MSNA. Household data collection is 
scheduled for July –August, while consultations on sector indicators will commence simultaneously with ISCG and 
Assessment and Analysis Working Group (AAWG) discussions. The assessment will provide the primary evidence base for 
the 2027 Lebanon Response Plan (LRP). 
VASyR 2026: The 2026 Vulnerability Assessment of Syrian Refugees (VASyR) is underway. This year's assessment 
includes dedicated analysis of post-December 2024 arrivals from Syria, conflict-affected households in southern Lebanon, 
and Vulnerability Assessment of Refugees of Other Nationalities (VARON). Findings will inform vulnerability analysis, 
targeting, and planning for the 2027 LRP.  
Humanitarian Financing: Lebanon secured an additional US$35.5 million in humanitarian funding to support 
implementation of the revised Flash Appeal, including US$12 million from the Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF) 
and US$23.5 million through the third Lebanon Humanitarian Fund (LHF) Reserve Allocation  (3rd in 2026). CERF funding 
will support Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance through WFP and UNRWA (US$6 million), Health and Nutrition through UNFPA 
and WHO (US$4.5 million), and Protection through UNFPA (US$1.5 million). The LHF allocation will support Food Security 
(US$6 million), Shelter and Site Management (US$5 million), WASH (US$4 million) , Health and Nutrition (US$4 million), 
Protection (US$2.5 million), and Emergency Livelihoods interventions through national NGOs (US$2 million).  
 
Access and Civil-Military Coordination 
Humanitarian operations in Lebanon continue to take place in a highly volatile and unpredictable environment. The situation 
is being closely monitored in light of ongoing developments and the renewed ceasefire announcement of 20 June. 
Humanitarian partners continue to utilize the Humanitarian Notification System (HNS) to facilitate movements to areas south 
of the Litani River (SLR) and parts of the north of the Litani River (NLR), particularly within Nabatieh Governorate. However, 
ongoing hostilities, evolving security dynamics, and fluctuating access conditions continue to affect operational predictability 
and challenge safe and timely delivery of humanitarian assistance.

With support from the HNS, humanitarian partners continue to access conflict -affected and hard -to-reach areas 1 across 
southern Lebanon and other affected regions. Between 2 March and 22 June, 261 humanitarian movements were 
supported through the HNS , including 227 missions south of the Litani River facilitated through established notification 
arrangements and in coordination with UNIFIL, in line with prevailing operational modalities.  
While most missions were completed successfully, several faced operational challenges, including delays, postponements, 
restricted operating windows, route limitations, communications disruptions, GPS spoofing, unexploded ordnance (UXO) 
contamination, and cancellations due to changing security conditions. Some missions also encountered nearby hostilities 
and ongoing military activity during implementation. 
These movements enabled the delivery of critical life-saving assistance, including bread, fresh produce, flour, safe drinking 
water, fuel, medical supplies, and core relief items. Where feasible and requested, humanitarian actors also supported the 
relocation of vulnerable individuals from high-risk areas to safer locations.
