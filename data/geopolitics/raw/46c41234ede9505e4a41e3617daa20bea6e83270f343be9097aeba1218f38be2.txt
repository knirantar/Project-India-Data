In May 2026, OCHA conducted a humanitarian access severity monitoring exercise to assess humanitarian actors’ perceptions of access constraints and their ability to conduct operations across Ukraine. Given the different access dynamics across the country, the exercise used two levels of analysis: hromada-level analysis in conflict-affected

front-line and border oblasts, and raion-level analysis in central and western Ukraine.


In the 521 conflict-affected hromadas assessed in front-line and border oblasts, 251 hromadas were classified as having high or extremely high access difficulties, up from 242 hromadas in October 2025.
 The deterioration was most significant at the most severe end of the scale, with hromadas classified as having extremely high access constraints or no access (level 4) increasing from 196 to 211. At the same time, the number of hromadas classified as accessible or having low access constraints (level 1) declined from 243 to 225.


Access deterioration was driven primarily by the proliferation of drones, recurrent attacks affecting humanitarian personnel, assets and facilities, and increasingly constrained operating conditions in border areas.
 These factors reduced operational predictability and limited humanitarian presence, including in areas where the front line did not shift substantially. Overall, partners reported deterioration in access conditions in 42 hromadas. Of these, 15 hromadas worsened from high access difficulties (level 3) to extremely high access constraints or no access (level 4), mostly in Zaporizka and Khersonska oblasts, where five and four hromadas respectively moved into the most

severe category.


The highest concentrations of hromadas with high access difficulties were recorded in areas directly affected by the armed conflict, including areas along the front line
 or exposed to active hostilities, cross-border attacks, drone activity, mines and unexploded ordnance, and movement restrictions. In these areas, humanitarian operations may still take place, but are often intermittent, highly constrained or dependent on specific windows of access.


Most hromadas assessed as having extremely high access constraints or no access (level 4) were in territories of Ukraine occupied by the Russian Federation.
 During the exercise, these areas remained inaccessible to humanitarian actors operating from areas under the control of the Government of Ukraine. The access situation for these hromadas was assessed in the most severe category (level 4), with no breakdown by specific type of access impediment.


In central and western Ukraine, 74 raions were assessed. Of these, 71 raions were classified as accessible or having low access constraints (level 1), 
while three raions, all in Odeska Oblast, were classified as having moderate access constraints (level 2).
