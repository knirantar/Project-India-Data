This report presents findings from data collected between December 2025 and March 2026 across ten Flow Monitoring Points (FMPs) in CRR-S, CRR-N, NBR, and LRR. Data collection was conducted through direct observation and interviews by DTM enumerators, with financial support from the United Nations Peacebuilding Fund (PBF).


Transhumance movements observed during the reporting period follow seasonal patterns, with herders – primarily from Senegal as well as within The Gambia – moving during the dry season in search of water and pasture. These movements are often strategically coordinated, with family members traveling ahead to assess security conditions before herds are relocated.


During the reporting period, a total of 2,222 herders were recorded across the four regions, with the highest numbers observed in CRR-N and CRR-S. The majority of herders (90%) were Senegalese nationals, while 10 per cent were Gambian. Similarly, 90 per cent of recorded livestock movements were cross-border, highlighting the strong regional dimension of transhumance in The Gambia.
