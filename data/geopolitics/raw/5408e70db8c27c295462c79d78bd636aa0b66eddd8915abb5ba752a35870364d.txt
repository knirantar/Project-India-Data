SITUATION OVERVIEW 
• Regional conflicts are exacerbating the 
humanitarian crisis in Afghanistan, with staple 
food prices at elevated levels and transport costs 
tripling due to prolonged border closure with 
Pakistan, re-routed supply chains due to the 
conflict in the Middle East, and increased fuel 
prices. These supply chain pressures are 
particularly acute for imported specialized 
nutritious foods (SNFs), with most WFP-supported 
health clinics currently facing a stock out of these 
commodities.   
• At the same time, Afghanistan is experiencing 
record levels of acute malnutrition, with 3.7 million 
children under five and 1.2 million pregnant and 
breastfeeding women affected in 2026. The 
shortage of nutrition supplies, combined with 
funding constraints, means that one million 
malnourished children will be unable to receive 
life-saving assistance this year. 
• Since early July, WFP's famine-prevention 
programmes in hotspot areas have been 
suspended, putting 1.5 million people at 
heightened risk of life-threatening hunger. Overall, 
WFP is now forced to turn away 13 million acutely 
hungry Afghans. 
• In addition, funding constraints have compelled 
WFP to suspend cash assistance for returnees 
from Iran and Pakistan at all border crossings, 
leaving vulnerable families without critical support 
upon arrival.

OPERATIONAL UPDATES 
General Food Assistance  
• In June, WFP assisted over 1.9 million people, 
delivering nearly 2,700 mt of food and USD 6.9 
million in cash-based transfers. Despite the 
proven eﬀectiveness of WFP's famine-prevention 
programmes in hotspot areas, severe funding 
shortfalls have forced the suspension of these 
life-saving interventions from July.  
• WFP was also forced to phase out returnee 
assistance in June due to critical funding 
constraints, leaving more than 400,000 
vulnerable Afghan returnees without cash-for-
food assistance upon arrival. 
 
Nutrition  
• Despite the arrival of over 1,600 mt of nutrition 
commodities – enough to support 80,000 
children and 25,000 mothers – and ongoing 
nutrition programmes through 1,756 health 
facilities and 74 health posts, worsening food 
insecurity, recurrent shocks, and large-scale 
returns continue to drive needs. Severe funding 
shortfalls mean WFP expects to reach only one 
in seven acutely malnourished women and 
children in 2026. 
School-Based Assistance 
• In June, WFP provided 149 mt of targeted food 
assistance, including Bread+, fortiﬁed biscuits 
and Samosa, to 625,000 primary school children 
in Farah, Nangarhar, Ghor, Jwazjan, Nuristan, 
Paktika, Samangan, Uruzghan, and Zabul 
provinces. 
Maternal and Child Beneﬁt Programme 
• Through the World Bank-funded maternal and 
child beneﬁt programme (inclusive of the MCBP 
Earthquake Response), approximately 9,500 
pregnant women and mothers of children under 
the age of 5 years received Social and Behaviour 
Change Communication (SBCC) on nutrition, 
health, and immunization, and USD 643,000 in 
cash assistance.  
Resilience and Livelihood Support 
• WFP continued to implement resilience activities 
across 50 districts in 21 provinces, reaching over 
14,600 households, including nearly 3,900 
female-headed households. Support focused on 
strengthening food security, climate resilience, 
and livelihoods through community asset 
creation, skills development, agricultural and 
livestock assistance, and cash-based support. 
Key infrastructure, including irrigation canals, 
protection walls, feeder roads, and watershed 
structures, was completed and handed over to 
communities. WFP also worked with partners 
and technical teams to design the next phase of 
resilience programming, drawing on lessons 
learned, community consultations, and risk 
analysis to ensure future interventions remain 
responsive to local priorities and climate risks. 
 
UN Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) 
• UNHAS operated a total of 178 ﬂights to 12 
destinations and facilitated the movement of 
approximately 1,115 passengers from 52 
organizations, including UN agencies, 
donors and non-governmental organizations. 
Supply Chain and Logistics 
• The long-lasting closure of the Pakistan border 
and the suspension of the Dubai–Iran supply 
route have continued to disrupt food deliveries 
to Afghanistan. Alternative corridors via the 
Lapis Lazuli route and through Türkiye and 
Northern Iran are helping restore supplies, with 
around 6,000 mt of specialized nutritious foods 
and vegetable oil beginning to arrive through 
these northern and western routes, enabling the 
gradual resumption of nutrition activities. 
However, transport costs have roughly tripled 
due to longer distances and multiple 
transshipment points. Eﬀorts to clear 
humanitarian cargo stranded in Pakistan since 
October 2025 have also progressed during the 
reporting period.

Stories from the Field 
Hanifa is one of 1,160 participants supported 
through the MCBP Nutrition-Sensitive Livelihoods 
intervention in Sayad District, Saripul Province. After 
her family sold their only productive assets to repay 
debts, she received business training and a 
livelihood grant to expand her food shop. The 
additional income has improved the family's access 
to food, reduced reliance on negative coping 
strategies, and enabled investment in poultry and 
rabbit farming. Her story demonstrates how 
nutrition-sensitive social protection and livelihood 
support can strengthen resilience, diversify incomes, 
and improve food security.

biscuits donated by the Government of Indonesia 
after a 15,000 km journey across nine countries, 
overcoming border closures and regional instability.

schoolchildren through WFP’s school meals 
programme, providing a vital source of nutrition 
amid record levels of hunger and malnutrition.
